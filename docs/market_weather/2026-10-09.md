## What the key terms mean today

**SPY** — the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies and our main benchmark — closed
at **$776.45**, which is **7.4% above** its 200-day moving average of $722.89. The 200-day
average is the average closing price over the last ~200 trading days. When today's price is
above it, the market is in a long-term uptrend. The S&P 500 posted its 28th record close of
2026 on October 6. SPY barely moved from yesterday (+$0.47), so the market is in a tight range.

**Interest rates** — the US 10-year Treasury yield sits near **5.32%**, its highest level
since 2002. When bonds pay 5.3% risk-free, stocks must offer a bigger expected return to
compete. This makes us more demanding about the price we pay for any stock.

**The Fed** (US central bank) raised its short-term rate to **3.75-4.00% on September 16**,
restarting its rate-hiking cycle. Markets now price roughly a **25% chance of another hike on
October 28** and treat a December hike as nearly certain.

**Inflation (CPI)** — the ISM Services Prices Paid index hit **74.0** on October 6, its
highest reading since July 2022. Hot inflation means the Fed is unlikely to stop hiking soon.
The September CPI report releases **Wednesday October 14 at 8:30 AM ET** — the single most
important event to watch this week.

**Earnings season** begins next week. Major banks (JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, BlackRock,
Citigroup) report on Tuesday October 13. Bank earnings often set the tone for the whole
quarter. More company reports = more potential for market-moving surprises.

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Very expensive. The **Shiller CAPE ratio** — which smooths 10 years of earnings against the
current price — stands near **41.1x**, compared to a long-run average of about 17x. The only
time it has been higher was the brief dot-com peak of 44.2x in December 1999, which preceded
a multi-year market decline of roughly 50%. The market has been above CAPE 40 continuously
since May 2026.

Expensive markets can stay expensive for years. This is not a prediction of a crash. But it
means a buyer today has very little margin for error, and we should be patient.
