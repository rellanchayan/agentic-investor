## Lessons

1. **A flagged macro catalyst day demands a morning review regardless of whether we expect to
   trade.** The Sep 28 journal warned that missing a morning review on a high-volatility day was
   the most likely way to miss an entry opportunity. The Sep 30 decision named Oct 2 (jobs
   report) by date. Both warnings were correct, and both were ignored. Naming a date is not
   enough; the review must actually run. The reliability of the morning cycle is as important as
   any thesis.

2. **The SPY gap is now −2.54 pp and growing.** Three months of full-cash holding has cost us
   2.54 percentage points of relative return, with zero volatility on our side. The theoretical
   protection against drawdown is real (SPY drew down −3.06%; we drew down 0.00%), but the
   opportunity cost is also real and compounding. Widening the thesis bench — particularly in
   sectors with no current coverage — is the most direct lever. Research is not optional; it is
   the work that makes future buys possible.

3. **MCD and V remain the nearest actionable names, but both still fail their gates.** V needs
   a roughly 34% price decline to reach the cautious entry price (≤$239.22); it is conviction 4
   and ready otherwise. MCD needs both a 16%+ price decline and a conviction upgrade from 3 to
   4. Neither condition changed this week. The MCD conviction review — understanding what would
   move conviction from 3 to 4 — remains unfinished research work that should be completed
   before a price drop forces a rushed judgment.
