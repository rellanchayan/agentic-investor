## Lessons

1. **Trailing the benchmark passively can work against us — but also for us.** The benchmark gap narrowed from 282 bp to 195 bp without us doing anything, because SPY pulled back. That is not a skill; it is luck. The honest lesson is that a gap that closes by luck can widen again by luck. The only reliable way to close it is to buy quality at a real discount — which we have not been able to do yet.

2. **Five missed morning reviews is a real process failure, not a hold.** The Oct 19-23 gap means we do not know whether better prices appeared during those days. We cannot claim the hold was disciplined for those sessions because we were not watching. The discipline requirement is to show up every weekday, assess the candidates, and document the decision — even if the decision is "hold." Missing five in a row is a habit to break before it becomes a pattern.

3. **The pre-FOMC window is a poor time to loosen the bar.** The decisions file explicitly documents that the 10-year yield offers more than the market's earnings yield. With the rate decision 48 hours away and inflation still running hot, tightening standards rather than relaxing them is correct. Our cautious posture held. The right follow-up is to complete the stale thesis refreshes (AAPL, MSFT, V — each 9 days overdue) so that if prices move sharply on the FOMC decision, we are ready to act.
