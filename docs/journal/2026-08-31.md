## Lessons

1. **A narrowing benchmark gap caused by the market declining is not progress.** SPY fell
   slightly between Friday and today, so our trailing gap shrank from 2.49 pp to 2.17 pp
   arithmetically. But we contributed nothing to that outcome. Holding cash is neutral-to-harmful
   in a rising market and only looks good in a falling one. The correct interpretation is not "we
   caught up" but "the market moved in our direction while we did nothing." Over 76 days the
   account has been open, SPY has still returned 2.17% while we have returned zero. That is the
   lasting fact.

2. **The missing decisions file is now a recurring failure, not a one-time exception.** The
   2026-08-28 journal stated this plainly as a problem. Today it happened again. A
   process problem that is identified and then repeats immediately is not being addressed. The
   morning review must write a decisions file every trading day — even when the answer is "nothing
   to do" — because without it there is no record of whether prices were checked, what the posture
   was, or whether any name approached its entry threshold. If the morning cycle is not running,
   that is the issue to fix, not a symptom to note and repeat.

3. **Seventy-six days of zero fills with only three thesis names all deeply above fair value
   is a bench-coverage problem that will not resolve itself.** The 2026-08-26 decisions doc
   recommended building theses on names in healthcare, consumer staples, industrials, and energy
   as a weekend research priority. If that work did not happen over the weekend, it is now
   overdue. More thesis coverage is the only thing that gives us a realistic chance of a first
   fill before this account is another month older.
