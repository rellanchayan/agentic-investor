## Lessons

1. **The cautious posture added 6% to the required margin of safety — but it was irrelevant to the
   outcome.** Even at the standard 12% bar (no posture bump), all three names would have been
   rejected by wide margins. MSFT needed a -37.5% price drop to clear the cautious bar; at the
   standard bar it still needed -29.0%. V needed -35.1% cautious, -30.1% standard. The honest read
   is that our names are not close calls. The valuation gap is structural, not a matter of posture
   calibration. Posture is a useful tool at the margin; it cannot substitute for a deeper bench of
   names closer to fair value.

2. **A decisions file was written today after a twelve-day gap. That is the right direction — but
   gap-closing is not the same as consistent process.** The morning cycle needs to run every trading
   day, not when circumstances permit. Each missed review is a day during which a price move toward
   a thesis target goes unobserved. The 2026-08-26 review happened to find nothing actionable.
   A session where the market dropped 5–10% on a name we cover would have looked the same in the
   log if no one was watching.

3. **The bench-coverage problem is the central constraint, not price level.** The decisions file
   names only three research-backed ideas — AAPL, MSFT, and V — all in tech and payments, all
   deeply overvalued. Twenty-five other names in today's market context carry no thesis and therefore
   cannot be bought regardless of price. If the purpose of this portfolio is to own a few wonderful
   businesses at sensible prices, the research backlog is the binding limit. The upcoming weekend
   research cycle should prioritize filing-backed theses for names in under-covered sectors
   (health care, consumer staples, industrials, energy) that trade at more realistic valuations.
