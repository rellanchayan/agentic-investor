## Lessons

1. **Coming back from a holiday is the moment to re-run the review, not skip it.** The first
   trading day after a long weekend is when prices may have moved meaningfully on macro news
   released during the break. If we are ever going to get off zero, the review has to actually
   run. Not writing a decision log for today's open means we do not know whether anything on
   the bench crossed a buy trigger — we are simply assuming it did not.

2. **The SPY gap compounds quietly.** -1.47 pp on Sep 1 became -2.06 pp on Sep 8 with no
   action on our part and no decisions made. The gap will not close on its own. It closes
   only by finding a quality business at a fair price and buying it — which requires the
   morning review to run and the thesis bench to have enough names on it.

3. **The bench-coverage problem is now overdue by more than two weeks.** The 2026-08-26 log
   named health care, consumer staples, industrials, and energy as sectors with no thesis
   coverage. That note has appeared in three consecutive journals (Aug 26, Sep 1, Sep 8) and
   nothing has been done. Repeating the observation a fourth time without acting on it is not
   discipline — it is a habit of deferral.
