## Lessons

1. **The SPY gap now stands at -2.23 pp and has widened every week since inception.** The bench
   expanded from three to five names between June and September. All five are still overvalued
   against our required margin of safety. Without a name trading within 18% of its base fair
   value, cash is the only legal position. The honest conclusion: expanding the bench to 8–10
   names remains the single most important outstanding task. A wider bench gives us more chances
   that at least one name is near fair value when conditions are right.

2. **Skipping the morning review is a process failure even when the answer is obviously "no
   trade."** The value of the morning review is not only the trade decision — it is the daily
   confirmation that prices were checked, theses are current, and nothing unusual happened. On
   the days the answer is hold, the review takes ten minutes and costs nothing. On the rare day
   something changes fast, the review is the only mechanism that catches it.

3. **The cost of patience is measurable and growing.** The gap to SPY is -2.23 pp. At $1,000,000
   that is about $22,300 of foregone return relative to simply holding SPY. That number will
   grow as long as we hold only cash. It does not mean we should lower the bar — paying too much
   for a stock locks in a loss from day one. But it does mean that every week without a thesis
   on a fairly priced name has a real dollar cost, and writing that cost down plainly is the
   honest thing to do.
