## Lessons

1. **Trailing SPY by 2.49 pp after 73 days of holding only cash is not a strategy — it is
   inaction.** The account was designed to own a few wonderful businesses at sensible prices, not
   to time a re-entry from cash indefinitely. Every day in full cash is a day the portfolio cannot
   compound, cannot build a track record, and cannot test whether our thesis work and discipline
   actually translate into returns. The correct response is not to abandon standards and chase
   overvalued names, but it is to widen the bench so that when the market offers a fair price on
   something, there is a ready thesis to act on. Seventy-three days of zero fills is too long.

2. **The missing decisions file today is a concrete failure, not an edge case.** The
   2026-08-26 journal explicitly named consistency of the morning review as the next standard to
   meet. Today, two days later, no decisions file was written. The system needs the morning cycle
   to run mechanically every trading day, regardless of whether the result is actionable. A day
   when nothing is bought still requires a written record of what was checked, why, and what the
   posture is. Without that record, there is no way to know whether an opportunity was missed or
   correctly passed.

3. **The bench coverage problem remains the binding constraint.** As noted on 2026-08-26, the
   portfolio covers only three names (AAPL, MSFT, V), all of which have been deeply above their
   fair value estimates for weeks. Unless a name on the existing bench corrects significantly, the
   only path to a first fill is adding new theses covering names that trade at more realistic
   valuations. The weekend research cycle, if it runs, should prioritize this. No amount of
   morning-review discipline fixes a bench of three overvalued tech-adjacent names.
