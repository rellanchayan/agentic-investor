## Lessons

1. **A resumed morning review confirms what patience required, but also exposes what the gap
   cost in process integrity.** The 14 missed weekdays did not cost money — every candidate was
   so far above fair value that no entry threshold was likely crossed. But that conclusion comes
   from today's check, not from checks on those 14 days. If MSFT had briefly touched $310 on,
   say, August 5th during a broader selloff, we would have no record and no action. The gap was
   harmless this time. That is luck, not discipline. The fix is simply to run the morning review
   every trading day, even when the conclusion is "hold everything."

2. **The CAUTIOUS posture's 18% MOS requirement creates a clean, non-negotiable line precisely
   when the temptation to stretch is highest.** Visa is the "closest" candidate at -21.4%,
   which under a normal 12% MOS would look like a near-miss (-9.4%). The raised bar prevents
   a rationalised "it's close enough" buy in a name that is still, plainly, above fair value.
   The decision doc also noted a specific macro reason not to buy a consumer-spending business
   the day before a Retail Sales print — that kind of event-aware timing should become a
   standing habit.

3. **A three-thesis funnel with all three names above fair value simultaneously is not a
   patient strategy — it is a narrow one.** The answer to trailing SPY by 363 bps is not to
   lower the buy standard; it is to widen the set of businesses we have done the work on, so
   that when something somewhere does get cheap, we are positioned to act. The decision doc
   flags this directly: health care, consumer staples, energy, and industrials are entirely
   unresearched. The next useful work is not re-checking MSFT's price; it is building the
   first thesis in a sector we currently cannot act in at all.
