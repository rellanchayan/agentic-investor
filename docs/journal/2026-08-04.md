## Lessons

1. **A 1.79 pp single-day move accumulates fast when the process has been dark for eight
   days.** SPY gained 1.79 pp today. Any position in MSFT, V, or AAPL — had the July 23
   review produced a buy at the prices then quoted — would have participated in some portion
   of that gain. It did not, because no buy was placed, and no buy was placed because no
   morning review ran. Each missed morning is not just a documentation gap; it is a day on
   which the system had no opportunity to act on what a review might have found. Eight missed
   mornings means eight days of closed options.

2. **The deficit is now 2.74 pp and it arrived faster than the process could see it.** The
   2026-08-03 journal noted a 0.95 pp deficit. One session later it is 2.74 pp. The playbook
   principle "beat SPY over years, not days" is sound, but it assumes the morning process runs
   each day so that each day's data is available to the system. A strategy that reviews
   occasionally cannot claim to be executing on a continuous record — it is executing on
   twelve-day-old information, with no way to know what happened in between.

3. **Twelve calendar days since the last review means the July 23 entry thresholds are no
   longer reliable.** On July 23, MSFT needed to fall to approximately $310 and V to
   approximately $243 to meet the cautious-market margin-of-safety requirement. Those thresholds
   were derived from fair-value estimates built on assumptions that were current on that date.
   Twelve days is enough time for an earnings release, a guidance revision, or a macro shift to
   change what any of these businesses is worth. The next morning review must re-verify the
   underlying business assumptions in each thesis before using any of these prices as entry
   guides. A price-only check against a stale fair value is not sufficient.
