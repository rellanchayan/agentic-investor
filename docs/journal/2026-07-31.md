## Lessons

1. **Seven trading days without a morning review is no longer a streak — it is the default
   mode.** The playbook lesson written on 2026-07-21 stated: "if the system is in
   watching-mode with named candidates, the morning review is mandatory, not optional." The
   lesson written on 2026-07-22 restated it. The 2026-07-30 journal named it again. The
   pattern is documented and recurring, which means documenting it one more time is not
   sufficient. The morning review will not resume by itself; something structural needs to
   change — either the scheduling of the morning run, or an explicit decision to pause the
   account until conditions allow regular operation.

2. **SPY's intraday recovery today (from -1.19% to -0.50% cumulative) is a concrete
   example of cash advantage eroding.** We did not observe prices today, so we cannot
   say whether MSFT, V, or AAPL crossed their entry thresholds during the session. If
   any of them did and then rallied back above threshold by close, we missed the
   opportunity entirely — and we missed it not because of a deliberate "too expensive"
   decision but because no one was watching. A missed-buy-because-it-was-too-expensive
   is a disciplined decision. A missed-buy-because-no-one-checked is a process failure.
   These are not the same thing, and the journal must not confuse them.

3. **The account will eventually hold all $1,000,000 in cash for so long that the
   opportunity cost of inaction exceeds the benefit of waiting for the perfect price.**
   Patient, price-disciplined investing is the core idea, but it requires active watching
   to be patient rather than passive. At some point, deploying a first position at a
   reasonable (not perfect) price and building the discipline of active management is
   worth more than maintaining a pristine zero-trade record in cash.
