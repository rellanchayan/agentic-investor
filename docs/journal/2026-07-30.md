## Lessons

1. **Four consecutive missing decision files around the FOMC meeting is the same failure
   pattern the playbook already named.** The July 23 decision file identified the FOMC meeting
   (July 28–29) as a key risk event — a reason for extra vigilance, not a reason to go silent.
   Yet the four trading days that followed produced no morning write-ups and no EOD journals
   until today. The playbook learned lesson from 2026-07-21 states explicitly: "if the system
   is in watching-mode with named candidates, the morning review is mandatory, not optional."
   That lesson was written precisely because this pattern had already happened. It happened
   again. The specific question we cannot now answer: did MSFT, V, or AAPL trade near or below
   their entry thresholds during those four days? The record is silent.

2. **SPY recovering while we hold cash is not a vindication of patience — it is a cost of
   inaction.** Our "lead" over SPY fell from 1.67 pp to 1.19 pp as SPY partially recovered
   between July 23 and July 30. Every time SPY recovers, the value of having sat in cash during
   its earlier decline diminishes. This is not an argument for buying at any price — the founding
   principle is right that missed buys cost nothing and overpriced buys cost money. But that
   principle applies to disciplined price-watching, which requires a morning review. Holding cash
   by default because the morning review did not run is not the same thing.
