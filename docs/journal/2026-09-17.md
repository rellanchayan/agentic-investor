## Lessons

1. **The morning review ran today — the correction worked, but the market did not cooperate with
   our thesis.** Yesterday's lesson named the missed FOMC-day review as the failure and said
   "check MSFT and V prices against their entry bars." We ran the check. MSFT is at $497.58 and
   needs to be at $327.73. V is at $369.51 and needs to be at $239.22. The FOMC hike that we
   expected might bring names closer to fair value did the opposite on the day after: SPY rebounded
   and the stocks moved further away. A written intention to "watch the catalyst" is not a
   prediction that the catalyst will help us. Markets can absorb a rate hike and rally. We should
   not be surprised by this.

2. **The SPY gap widened from -0.46 pp to -1.61 pp in one day, and it was the right decision not
   to buy.** The gap is a real cost of staying in cash, and it compounds. But the gap from buying
   AAPL at a 43% premium to fair value, or MSFT at a 24% premium, would be worse and would be
   permanent rather than temporary. The correct frame is: the gap closes when we buy quality names
   at sensible prices, not when we buy at any price to feel deployed. Patience is a position. A
   3.75–4.00% rate environment means cash earns a real return while it waits.

3. **Upward thesis revisions need to be watched carefully for motivated reasoning.** Today's
   refresh raised base fair value for AAPL and MSFT. The decision notes flag this openly: "it would
   be easy to let bands drift upward until a stock looks buyable." Both names still fail by more
   than 24 pp, so there is no immediate integrity concern. But the pattern to guard against is
   incremental upward revision — each one defensible in isolation — that gradually narrows the gap
   until a stock looks buyable without the price actually having fallen. The thesis refresh process
   should explain why a number moved, not just move it.
