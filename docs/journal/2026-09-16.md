## Lessons

1. **Today was the day the September 14 journal told us to show up for — and we did not show
   up.** The prior journal named FOMC day by date, described the specific mechanism (rate hike
   could move MSFT/V closer to entry), and called it "a reason to be present, prices checked,
   theses refreshed, and ready." None of that happened. The lesson is not "FOMC matters" — we
   already knew that. The lesson is that a written intention in a journal entry is not a
   commitment mechanism. The morning review process needs a reliable trigger that runs on every
   trading day, not only when someone manually starts it.

2. **The gap vs SPY narrowed from -1.36 pp to -0.46 pp over two days, and we did nothing.**
   That is the right outcome for today: we should not have bought into a falling market without
   prices checked and a fresh thesis. But it also illustrates the broader problem. The only way
   we can close the gap to SPY over time is to deploy capital into quality businesses at sensible
   prices. That requires the morning review to actually run on days when markets move. Two
   consecutive missed mornings on a week we specifically flagged as important is a pattern worth
   taking seriously.

3. **The bench is still three names wide (MSFT, V, AAPL), all cited in the last review as
   materially above fair value.** The FOMC selloff may have changed that math. Until the morning
   review runs and checks live prices against the entry bars, we do not know. Writing that in a
   journal does not substitute for running the numbers. The first task tomorrow is to run the
   morning review, check MSFT and V prices against their 82%-of-base-fair-value entry bars, and
   decide honestly whether the math has changed.
