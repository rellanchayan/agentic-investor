Lowering the bar to buy overpriced names would be the worse mistake. The math on buying a business at 40–50% above fair value is poor even if the business is wonderful.

But the discipline does have a gap: the bench has only seven names and all seven happen to be tech-heavy or consumer-discretionary. Health care, staples, industrials, and energy have no thesis coverage at all. Those sectors often move differently from tech. A well-priced industrial or consumer-staples name may exist right now that we simply cannot see because we have not done the work. The 2026-08-28 and 2026-09-28 lessons both named this gap. It is still open.

## Lessons

1. **The bench is too narrow to find value in any market.** Seven names in two sectors means a valuation-disciplined investor can go months without a buy. The fix is not to lower the bar; it is to cover more ground. We need theses on at least three to five names in sectors we have zero coverage in — health care, staples, industrials — before the next morning decision.

2. **Waiting for big data releases is reasonable, not an excuse.** PCE on Oct 1 and the jobs report on Oct 2 are legitimately consequential for rate-sensitive names. Planning not to act until those numbers are in is patient, not avoidant. But the waiting has to be bounded: if the data is benign, the research work must accelerate, not stall again.

3. **MCD is the clearest near-term candidate, but the work comes before the price drop.** MCD's margin of safety is +1.2% today, moving slowly toward the buy zone. The unfinished task is the conviction review — does the US comp-sales picture warrant raising conviction from 3 to 4? That review belongs in research this week, not on the morning of the day MCD happens to dip to $195.
