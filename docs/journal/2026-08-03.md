## Lessons

1. **The "lead" we described in prior journals is gone, and it left without a single decision.**
   The 2026-07-23 and 2026-07-30 journals both noted that the apparent lead over SPY was not
   an investment result — it was arithmetic from holding cash during a falling market. That was
   correct. SPY has now recovered, and we are 0.95 pp behind. Holding cash is a legitimate
   strategy only when it is a deliberate, written choice made each morning after checking prices.
   Seven consecutive trading days without a morning review means the last four of those days
   (July 31, August 1, August 3) were not deliberate holds — they were passive inaction during
   a 2.14 pp market move. The playbook distinguishes these two things for a reason.

2. **A stale thesis is a silent risk.** The last morning review that checked MSFT, V, and AAPL
   prices was 2026-07-23. Eleven calendar days have passed. Prices, news, and earnings can
   change materially in that time. The theses on file were written to specific fair-value
   estimates and sell triggers; if any underlying assumption has shifted, the entry threshold
   we are waiting for may itself need to change. A thesis that goes unchecked for nearly two
   weeks is no longer a reliable guide to whether a price is cheap or expensive. The next
   morning review must treat all three theses as potentially stale.

3. **The pattern of missing reviews is now seven days deep and has produced a measurable cost.**
   The playbook lesson added 2026-07-30 stated that a future event named in a decision file as
   a catalyst is a standing review commitment. The 2026-07-23 file named "FOMC July 28-29" as
   a key factor; we missed the FOMC window. Now we have also missed the three trading days
   after the FOMC during which SPY moved 2.14 pp. The first trade this portfolio ever makes
   will be placed without the benefit of seven days of morning deliberation. That matters: the
   quality of the first entry sets the tone for everything that follows.
