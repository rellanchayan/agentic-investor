## Lessons

1. **A single down day in SPY does not repair a 56-day process gap.** Today SPY fell roughly
   0.33 pp, reducing our trailing deficit from 2.99 pp to 2.66 pp. That improvement came from
   the market, not from anything we did, and it can reverse tomorrow. The only durable fix to the
   trailing gap is to re-engage the morning cycle and begin making written, deliberate decisions
   about whether our theses (AAPL, MSFT, V) still support entry at current prices. Waiting for
   a market pullback to bail us out is not a strategy.

2. **Thirteen trading days without a morning review means three theses have gone unexamined for
   nineteen calendar days.** Earnings, macro data, and price action during that period may have
   moved any of these names through (or past) our intended entry points. There is no way to know
   without looking. The next morning review must check current prices against thesis entry
   thresholds, run `python3 code/thesis.py --stale`, and produce a written posture file — even
   if the posture is "hold cash and wait."

3. **Zero volatility in our account is not a sign of safety; it is a sign of zero exposure.**
   The scorecard shows our annualized volatility at 0.00% versus SPY's 16.23%. This sounds
   favorable until one recognizes that it simply means the account has never moved at all. A
   portfolio that never fluctuates because it holds only cash is not managing risk — it is
   avoiding the market entirely. The goal is disciplined participation, not indefinite abstention.
