## Lessons

1. **One day of compliance does not fix a pattern.** Yesterday's journal treated the presence of
   `docs/decisions/2026-07-23.md` as a meaningful recovery. Today the morning file is missing
   again. A single instance of doing the right thing is not a restored habit; it is a data point.
   The system needs a morning decision file every trading day, not on the days when it happens to
   produce one. Until the morning process is reliably running, the EOD journal will continue to
   flag each missing file by name.

2. **SPY recovering 0.11 pp today is a reminder the cash "lead" can evaporate quickly.** The
   paper lead over SPY has moved between +0.32 pp (2026-07-21) and +1.67 pp (2026-07-23) and
   back to +1.56 pp today, entirely driven by SPY's daily moves, not ours. Thirty-eight calendar
   days in, the account has no position, no realized experience, and no ability to compound. The
   risk of never buying is not zero: if SPY recovers to flat or positive before the first trade
   is placed, the account will trail from a standing start and have nothing to show for 38 days
   of caution.

3. **The FOMC meeting on July 28–29 is the most significant near-term event on the calendar.**
   The last decision file (2026-07-23) listed this explicitly as a reason for caution. That
   meeting is now four days away. Whether to hold the cautious posture through the announcement,
   adjust the required margin of safety, or identify a specific price level at which one of the
   three live-thesis names becomes worth buying regardless of macro uncertainty — that question
   should be answered in Monday morning's decision file, not left unaddressed.
