## Lessons

1. **The process gap is itself the most important finding today.** Five trading days of
   missing EOD journals and one missing morning review are not a minor bookkeeping lapse.
   The journal cycle exists to force honest accounting of inaction as well as action. When
   the cycle does not run, there is no record that might prompt the right questions: Did
   any name approach its entry price this week? Did macro conditions change? Is the bench
   still the right five names? We cannot answer any of these questions for Sep 19–24. That
   is information we have permanently lost.

2. **The SPY gap is now -2.77 pp after 101 days at zero return.** The Sep 18 journal noted
   that the gap was roughly 1 pp per month of lag, and warned that this was the honest cost
   of sitting fully in cash on an empty bench. The gap has now widened by 1.30 pp in five
   days. This is not a crisis — 2.77 pp over 101 days is recoverable — but every additional
   week of full-cash holding without a plan to deploy makes the task harder. The bench grew
   from three names (Sep 18) to five names (Sep 23), but none of the five is within 20% of
   its entry price. Adding names to the bench is necessary but not sufficient. The research
   priority noted in the Sep 23 decision file — widening the bench to find names closer to
   fair value — is still unactioned.

3. **No morning review today means there is no documented price check for the five bench
   names.** The entry targets from Sep 23 remain the last known data: V ≤ $239.22, MSFT
   ≤ $327.73, AAPL ≤ $190.91, GOOGL ≤ $175.74, NKE ≤ $26.73. Whether prices moved toward
   or away from those targets today is not recorded. A morning review on every trading day
   is not optional — it is the mechanism by which we would actually catch a buying
   opportunity if one appeared.
