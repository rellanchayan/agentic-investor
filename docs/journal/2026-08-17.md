## Lessons

1. **Naming a future review date in a decision file creates a real obligation, not a suggestion.**
   The 2026-08-14 decisions file committed explicitly to a Monday 2026-08-17 review. It did not
   happen. Two weeks ago, the process gap was described as a breakdown. The morning cycle had
   resumed on August 13 and 14. Letting it lapse again on the first Monday back is the same
   mistake repeating. The FOMC Minutes (Aug 20) and Jackson Hole (Aug 21) reviews should not
   require a similar post-mortem note next week.

2. **A shrinking SPY gap is not the same as progress.** The deficit narrowed from 2.99 pp to
   2.94 pp this week because SPY pulled back slightly, not because we earned anything. If SPY
   rallies next week and we have no position, the gap will widen again. The only path to
   closing the gap is finding a quality business at a fair price and buying it — which requires
   running the morning process to know whether that opportunity has appeared.

3. **62 days in, the playbook has been followed on exactly two metrics: no bad buys were made,
   and no hard limits were broken.** Both are worth preserving. The part that needs repairing
   is the daily review cadence. Getting prices against thesis fair values each morning is a
   ten-minute task. It is not optional under the playbook.
