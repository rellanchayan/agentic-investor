## Lessons

1. **Running the morning cycle is not optional, especially before binary events.** The decision cycle is not just about placing orders; it is the mechanism that confirms the posture is still correct, checks current prices against updated fair values, and keeps the thesis library current. Missing five reviews in a row, including the run-up to FOMC and three major earnings reports, means we were operating on seven-day-old data. The thesis refresh for AAPL, MSFT, and V — flagged on Oct 16 — remains undone. That is the first task when the morning cycle resumes.

2. **The SPY gap narrowing is not something we earned.** The gap fell from ~282 bp to 123 bp because SPY declined this week, not because we made good decisions. Taking credit for that would be dishonest. The correct reading is: SPY's decline is a reminder that prices do move, that the cautious posture was directionally right about near-term risk, and that the next move down could bring some names closer to fair value — which makes having current theses even more important.

3. **A wide thesis bench is still the highest-priority research task.** As of Oct 16, only V had conviction 4, and it needed a 41% price drop to become a buy. That means even if the market had fallen 20% this week, we would have had no names ready to buy. The Oct 16 decision flagged MA, PG, KO, JNJ, HD, and UNP as good candidates for initial thesis work. None of that research happened. Until the bench has at least two or three conviction-4 names with entry prices within striking distance (say, a 25-30% price decline), we will stay fully sidelined through every correction.
