## Lessons

1. **The market pulled back and we were watching from the sidelines — that is exactly right,
   and it confirms the discipline.**
   SPY fell from roughly +2.99% (Aug 10 journal) to +2.24% today. We caught none of that
   decline because we hold nothing, and that is correct: all three thesis names were above their
   high fair-value bands as of August 14. A pullback in SPY does not automatically mean our
   targets are hit. The next morning review should fetch fresh quotes and re-run the margin-of-
   safety arithmetic to see whether any name has crossed into a watchable zone. We do not assume
   the answer is yes until the numbers say so.

2. **Two decision files in a row (Aug 13–14) is progress; two missing in a row (Aug 17–18) erases
   it.**
   The August 14 file was a thorough piece of work — posture, three-name arithmetic, explicit
   catalyst calendar, forward review dates. It should have been a template for daily practice.
   Instead, the next two trading days have no record. The FOMC Minutes (Aug 20) and Jackson Hole
   (Aug 21) were called out explicitly as mandatory review dates. Those are now two and three days
   away. The Aug 14 commitment must be honoured; missing those catalysts after writing them into
   the decision file would be a clear process failure.

3. **63 days open, 0 trades — the thesis bench is the binding constraint, not the daily cycle.**
   Even if we ran a flawless morning review every day, we currently have three candidates, all
   dramatically overpriced. The real work is building more theses so that when the market does
   pull back, we have a wider list of names to check against entry prices. The August 14 file
   named 26 names under "no thesis, therefore no buy." Saturday's weekly research should
   prioritise turning at least one of those into a real filed thesis. A single new thesis on a
   quality business trading at a fair price is worth more than any number of "hold" decisions
   on names we already know we cannot buy.
