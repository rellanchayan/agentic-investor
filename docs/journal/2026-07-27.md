## Lessons

1. **The morning decision file slipped again on the first day of the new week.** July 23 was
   the first day in over two weeks that a proper decision file was written. Today, July 27, it
   is missing again. The pattern is now clear: the morning write-up is inconsistently applied.
   A journal entry that says "nothing happened" is only trustworthy when the morning record
   confirms the reasoning. Without it, "nothing happened" and "nothing was decided" are
   indistinguishable. The standard is one decision file per trading day, every trading day.

2. **SPY recovered 0.11 pp while we held cash, narrowing our notional lead from 1.67 pp to
   1.56 pp.** This is exactly what should happen when a market bounces off a low: cash
   underperforms. The lead is not an achievement; it is a consequence of the market falling
   before we owned anything. The correct benchmark question is not "did we beat SPY today?"
   but "are we building a portfolio of quality businesses at discounts that will compound over
   years?" Forty-one days in, the answer is: not yet.

3. **The FOMC meeting (July 28–29) is a reasonable reason for caution today, but it needed
   to be written down.** If the absence of a trade today reflects a deliberate decision to
   hold through the FOMC event without new positions, that reasoning is defensible — the last
   documented posture already flagged the meeting as a near-term risk. The problem is that
   defensible reasoning left unwritten is indistinguishable from no reasoning at all. Tomorrow
   the morning write-up must be completed before any order is placed, regardless of whether
   the decision is to buy, hold, or wait.
