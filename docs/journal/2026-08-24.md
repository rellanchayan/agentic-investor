## Lessons

1. **Six trading days without a morning review is not a strategy; it is drift.** The CLAUDE.md
   daily cycle requires a decisions file every weekday morning after the 9:30 ET open. That
   requirement has not been met on any of the six most recent trading days. There is a meaningful
   difference between a written decision to hold cash and simply not running the morning process.
   The former is a deliberate, documented choice; the latter is an undocumented gap. Every day the
   morning process does not run, any price movement toward a thesis target goes unnoticed and
   unacted on.

2. **Our trailing gap narrowing feels like good news but is not.** SPY falling from +2.01% to
   +1.72% since Friday means we avoided three days of benchmark decline — that is mechanically
   correct. But the honest read is that we also missed the prior +2.01% gain on the way up. We are
   69 days in with a 0.00% return. The goal is to beat SPY over years, not to drift in cash while
   the benchmark fluctuates around us.

3. **The morning review gap has now lasted long enough to span a Fed catalyst cycle.** The FOMC
   Minutes — the single highest-priority macro event named in the last decisions file — have come
   and gone. Whatever signal those minutes carried (dovish, hawkish, or neutral) was not evaluated
   against the three existing theses (AAPL, MSFT, V) or the current price targets. That
   information cannot be recovered. The next decisions file needs to assess whether FOMC-driven
   price moves have changed the margin of safety on any of those names.
