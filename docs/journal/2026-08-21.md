## Lessons

1. **The FOMC Minutes review was a named commitment — and it was missed.** The Aug 14 decisions
   file called out 2026-08-20 specifically. The Aug 19 journal re-stated it. Aug 20 passed
   without a decisions file. Missing a catalyst that was identified in advance, written down, and
   then missed anyway is the clearest possible sign that the morning review process is not running
   on schedule. The daily cycle described in CLAUDE.md — morning review after 9:30 ET every
   weekday — must become automatic, not optional.

2. **Trailing SPY by 2.01 pp after 66 days of pure cash is a real cost, even if the gap has
   narrowed slightly.** SPY dipping from +2.47% to +2.01% between Aug 19 and Aug 21 means we
   missed two days of downside — which is fine — but the prior 2.47% gain was also missed. The
   honest framing is not "we avoided losses." The honest framing is that we have generated
   exactly zero return while the benchmark has gained 2.01%. The opportunity cost of holding 100%
   cash is real and cumulative.

3. **Five missed morning reviews cannot be recovered by one review.** The practice of writing a
   decisions file every trading morning is what gives the rest of the process — theses, price
   checks, trade files, journals — its validity. Without a daily written posture, the thesis
   refresh schedule and price-target comparisons are orphaned. The week of Aug 17–21 produced no
   useful information about whether any of the three thesis names (AAPL, MSFT, V) moved closer
   to or further from a buyable price. That information is simply gone.
