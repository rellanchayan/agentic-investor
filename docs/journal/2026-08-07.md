## Lessons

1. **The SPY gap is now 3.01 pp and widening without any decision from us.** The 2026-08-03
   journal noted we had crossed from a cash "lead" into a 0.95 pp deficit. Four trading days
   later it is 3.01 pp. We did not choose to be behind; we passively fell behind because the
   market rose and we held cash without checking prices each morning. A deliberate daily hold
   decision — written, priced, and reasoned — is a decision. Absence is not a decision; it is
   inaction that produces the same outcome as a decision to hold, without any of the analysis.

2. **Eleven consecutive trading days without a morning review is a structural failure, not a
   bad week.** The playbook states that every weekday morning after the 9:30 ET open, the
   morning agent runs: gate → reconcile → snapshot → market weather → thesis freshness →
   decide → risk-check → submit → write `docs/decisions/<date>.md`. That cycle has not run
   on any of the last 11 trading days. During that same window, SPY moved +2.06 pp (on top of
   an earlier move). Whether MSFT, V, or AAPL crossed their entry thresholds on any of those
   days cannot be determined from the record, because no price checks were written down. A
   process that only runs when manually triggered is not a process — it is an intention.

3. **The cost of inaction compounds.** At $1,000,000 of starting equity, each percentage point
   of SPY return we miss represents approximately $10,000 of paper return foregone. We are
   already 3.01 pp behind after 52 calendar days. If the market returns its long-run average
   of roughly 10% per year and we remain in cash, the deficit grows by roughly 2.5 pp per
   month. The investment thesis for this account — own wonderful businesses at sensible prices,
   add slowly, beat SPY over years — cannot be tested until the first position is entered. The
   prerequisite for that is a daily morning review that checks prices against fair value and
   writes down the result, every trading day without exception.
