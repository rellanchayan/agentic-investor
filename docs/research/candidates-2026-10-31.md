## Proposed additions

### 1. MCO — Moody's Corporation (Financial Services / Financials)

Moody's rates credit — it assigns letter grades (Aaa down to C) to corporate and
government bonds. When a company or government wants to borrow in the public markets,
it almost always hires Moody's (and S&P Global) to rate the debt. Institutional investors
often require at least two ratings before they will buy a bond, and regulations around
the world embed those two names by name. That structure — built into law, market practice,
and investor mandates over more than a century — has created an oligopoly that is nearly
impossible for a newcomer to break.

**Moat type:** Regulatory oligopoly plus brand intangible. Bond issuers have no real
alternative to the big two agencies; switching is not an option because the buyers of
their debt require Moody's and S&P ratings by name.

**Recent financials (sources cited below; verify against Moody's 10-Q before relying):**
- Adjusted operating margin: approximately 53% in Q1 2026 (up 150 basis points YoY)
- Free cash flow: approximately $2.97 billion for the latest full year, and $844 million
  in Q1 2026 alone (up 26% YoY)
- Management narrowed full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $16.50-$17.00
- Share buyback target raised to up to $3 billion for 2026

**Why it qualifies:** Very large, S&P 500 member, highly liquid. The business model is
capital-light (capex roughly 4.5% of revenue). FCF margins are among the highest in the
financial sector. Consistent profitability over multiple cycles.

**Gap filled:** Capital markets infrastructure — the credit-rating side of the financial
system — is completely absent from our watchlist today.

**Concerns:**
- The bond issuance cycle is the primary revenue driver. In a recession or a period of
  reduced capital markets activity, rating fees decline materially. FCF is not immune to
  cyclicality, though Moody's Analytics (data subscriptions) provides some cushion.
- Debt levels are meaningful — TipRanks flags that high absolute debt limits balance-sheet
  flexibility if issuance weakens.
- Valued at a premium: the market knows this is a high-quality business.

### 2. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical, Inc. (Surgical Robotics / Health Care)

Intuitive Surgical builds the da Vinci robotic surgery platform and sells the instruments
and accessories used in each procedure. A hospital that installs a da Vinci system invests
in surgeon training, workflow redesign, and outcomes documentation that is specific to
Intuitive's ecosystem. Switching to a competitor means re-training every surgeon,
re-validating clinical outcomes, and persuading hospital administrators to restart a
multi-year adoption curve. With more than 11,000 systems installed globally as of mid-2026,
the installed base itself is the moat: it generates a compounding stream of per-procedure
instrument and accessory revenue (the razor and the blades) that does not require Intuitive
to win new hospital relationships to grow.

**Moat type:** Switching costs (surgeon training + clinical evidence + hospital workflow
integration) plus the installed-base razor-and-blade model.

**Recent financials (sources cited below; verify against Intuitive's 10-Q before relying):**
- Operating margin: approximately 30.5% in Q1 2026
- Free cash flow: first-half 2026 FCF up approximately 71% year-over-year, totaling roughly
  $1.8 billion for H1 2026; $8.6 billion in cash and investments on the balance sheet
- Q1 2026 FCF alone was approximately $808 million per one aggregator (source figures vary;
  verify directly)

**Why it qualifies:** Large-cap (market cap well above $100B), liquid, NASDAQ-listed,
consistently profitable with growing FCF. Business model is simple to understand: sell
robots at a modest margin, earn high-margin recurring revenue on instruments and service.

**Gap filled:** SYK (orthopedic implants and Mako robotics) covers devices in our health
care bucket. ISRG covers a different sub-sector — soft-tissue surgical robotics —
with a distinct economic model. The two names are complementary, not redundant.

**Concerns:**
- Procedure-volume saturation: as general/urological/gynecological surgery adoption
  matures, incremental volume growth may slow. Growth is increasingly dependent on new
  surgical specialties (e.g., thoracic, colorectal) and on geographic expansion.
- Premium valuation: price-to-book approximately 7x industry average per one source.
  The business trades at a significant premium to intrinsic value in most market
  environments, so a margin of safety may require patience.
- Competitive entry: a handful of well-funded competitors (Medtronic, J&J Ottava) are
  developing robotic surgery platforms. Intuitive's first-mover advantage and installed
  base are real, but competitive risk is nonzero over a 10-year horizon.

### 3. NFLX — Netflix, Inc. (Streaming Entertainment / Communication)

Netflix is the world's largest paid streaming service, with over 300 million subscribers
globally as of late 2025. It produces and licenses a vast content library across film,
episodic series, sports, and live events in more than 190 countries. Revenue comes from
monthly subscription fees and, increasingly, from an ad-supported tier that broadens the
addressable market.

**Moat type:** Content scale and brand intangible. Netflix's massive subscriber base
allows it to spend at a level — roughly $17-18 billion in content per year — that few
competitors can match and still earn a profit. That spending generates the algorithm and
library depth that keeps subscribers from churning. It is closer to a financial moat
(scale advantage) than a classic network-effect moat.

**Important caveat on moat strength:** Morningstar rates Netflix's moat as **narrow**,
not wide. Their view is that the subscriber base creates a content-reinvestment edge
but not an unassailable structural barrier. The research analyst should weigh this
carefully. A narrow moat means the competitive position is more dependent on continued
execution than a wide-moat franchise.

**Recent financials (sources cited below; verify against Netflix's 10-K or latest
shareholder letter before relying):**
- Operating margin: approximately 29.5% for fiscal 2025 (expanded ~3 percentage points YoY)
- Free cash flow: approximately $9.5-12 billion for fiscal year 2025 (sources disagree on
  the exact figure; management guided approximately $11 billion for 2026)
- Net debt/EBITDA: approximately 0.4x at end of 2025; balance sheet is healthy
- Revenue growth guidance: 11-13% organic growth for 2026

**Why it qualifies:** Very large, liquid, NASDAQ-listed. The business turned FCF-positive
in 2022 and has grown FCF rapidly since. Profitable at operating and net income levels.
The business model is understandable: charge subscribers monthly, spend on content, keep
more than you spend.

**Gap filled:** DIS is our only communication/media name. Adding a streaming pure-play
diversifies this thin sector bucket and provides a different economic profile (pure
subscription plus advertising, no theme parks or linear TV).

**Concerns:**
- Narrow moat rating: unlike Moody's or Intuitive's structural advantages, Netflix's
  position depends on content quality and algorithmic recommendation staying ahead of
  competitors. Disney+, Amazon Prime, and YouTube are all formidable.
- Content cost inflation: if streaming competition intensifies, content costs per
  subscriber could rise faster than pricing power allows.
- Growth deceleration: Morningstar explicitly notes that 2026 guidance confirmed
  decelerating growth, which is expected as the platform matures.
- This is the least "wide-moat" of the three candidates proposed today. The analyst
  should give particular scrutiny to the competitive dynamics before building a full thesis.

## Names considered and rejected

**META (Meta Platforms):** Morningstar rates META's moat as wide (network effects across
Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp). However, META's 2026 capital expenditure guidance of
$130-145 billion is consuming virtually all operating cash flow: Q2 2026 operating cash
flow was approximately $31.9 billion but FCF was approximately $784 million, according to
the SEC filing. Our criteria require consistent positive FCF. With FCF margins collapsing
from 30%+ to low single digits due to AI infrastructure spending, META fails the "consistent
profits and positive free cash flow" test right now. It could re-qualify once the capex
cycle plateaus. The moat is real; the financials are not in the right shape today.

**BLK (BlackRock):** The world's largest asset manager with iShares ETF and the Aladdin
risk-management platform. Moat case is solid. However, FCF has declined at roughly a
-10% CAGR from 2022-2025, FCF margins compressed from ~23% to ~15%, and the quarterly
pattern is volatile. At ~54x price-to-FCF, the valuation is demanding relative to
declining cash generation. Not rejected permanently — a cyclical recovery in asset
management fees could restore the FCF trend — but not the cleanest candidate today.

**TMO (Thermo Fisher Scientific):** Strong moat from lab workflow integration. However,
FCF has been flat to declining (-1.6% CAGR over 5 years), FY2025 FCF was down 13%
year-over-year, and total debt stands at approximately $40 billion. The moat is real but
the financial trajectory is not what we look for in a first addition to the watchlist.
