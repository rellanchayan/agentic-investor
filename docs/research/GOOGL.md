## 1. What the company actually does

Alphabet is the parent company of Google. Think of it as four businesses:

1. **Advertising (~68% of revenue).** Billions of people search on Google and watch YouTube.
   Advertisers pay to be shown next to that attention. In the June 2026 quarter:
   Google Search & other **$63.271B (+17%)**, YouTube ads **$11.055B (+13%)**, and the Google
   Network — ads Google places on *other people's* websites — **$7.303B (−1%)**.
2. **Subscriptions, platforms and devices — $12.911B (+15%).** YouTube Premium and TV, Google One,
   Google's cut of the Play app store, and Pixel phones.
3. **Google Cloud — $24.768B, up 82%.** Renting computing power, storage, Google's own AI chips
   (TPUs) and AI software to businesses. Profit here exploded: segment operating income of
   **$8.814B**, a 35.6% margin, versus $2.826B (20.7%) a year earlier. Signed-but-not-yet-delivered
   contracts (the "backlog") stand at **$514B**.
4. **Other Bets — $0.382B of revenue and a $1.799B operating loss.** Waymo self-driving cars,
   Verily, and other long shots.

Total Q2 2026 revenue was **$119.796B (+24%)** and operating income **$40.770B (+30%)**, a 34%
operating margin. ([Q2 2026 press release, SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm))

## 2. The moat — why competitors struggle to take this away

A **moat** is a lasting advantage that keeps rivals at bay. Alphabet has several at once, which
is why we rate it **wide**:

- **Network effects.** Every search makes the next search better (Google sees what you clicked).
  YouTube is a two-sided marketplace: viewers go where the creators are, creators go where the
  viewers are. Neither can be bought.
- **Distribution by default.** Chrome and Android are the pipes most of the world's internet runs
  through. Being the default is worth enormous amounts — which is exactly why regulators are
  attacking it.
- **Scale economies.** Alphabet designs its own AI chips (TPUs) and builds its own data centres.
  At $200B/year of spending, almost nobody else can match the per-unit cost.
- **Switching costs.** Once a company runs its data and applications on Google Cloud, moving is
  painful and expensive. The $514B backlog is customers committing years of spend in advance.

**Vertical integration is the newest piece:** Alphabet owns the silicon, the models (Gemini), the
data centres *and* the distribution. Very few companies own all four.

## 4. The two things you must understand before looking at the price

### (a) Reported earnings are badly distorted right now — in Alphabet's favour

Q2 2026 net income was **$112.193B** on operating income of only **$40.770B**. The difference is
**$97.983B of "other income"** — almost entirely an *unrealised paper mark-up* on Alphabet's stakes
in **Anthropic** (carried at roughly **$124.3B**) and **SpaceX**. Over the first six months of 2026,
the cash-flow statement shows a **$135.803B** gain on debt and equity securities.

So if you look at a stock screener today, GOOGL appears to trade at about **17.6x earnings**
(trailing GAAP diluted EPS of $19.93) — which looks like a bargain. **It isn't.** That number is
measuring paper gains on private AI companies, not the operating business. Those marks can reverse
just as fast as they appeared.

### (b) Free cash flow has essentially stopped, and the buyback with it

Alphabet is in the middle of a capital-spending shock:

- Capital expenditure guidance for 2026 was **raised to $195–205B** (from $180–190B), and
  management says 2027 will be **"significantly higher."**
- Capex is now **5.25x depreciation**. H1 2026 capex alone was **$80.598B**.
- Free cash flow fell from **$73.266B (FY2025)** to **$4.261B in H1 2026**; Q2 was **−$5.9B**.
  The CFO said FCF "will remain under pressure."
- **Share buybacks went to zero.** $0 in Q1 2026 and $0 in Q2 2026, versus $13.238B in Q2 2025
  alone and $45.709B for all of FY2025.
- Instead of returning capital, Alphabet **raised** it: an **$80B equity programme** announced
  1 June 2026 ($15B mandatory convertible preferred, $15B common stock, a **$40B at-the-market
  programme still running**, and a $10B private placement to Berkshire Hathaway at ~$350/share),
  **plus** roughly $50B of new debt (long-term debt doubled from $48.5B to $100.2B in six months).

Shares outstanding went from 12,088M to 12,230M in six months, and there are now 19 million
preferred depositary shares that did not exist before. **We are being diluted, not compounded.**

## 5. Fair value — how we got the band

All arithmetic was done by `code/valuation.py`, not by hand.

**Method 1 — P/E on normalised "core" earnings.** We throw away *all* non-operating income, so no
paper gains sneak in:

> TTM operating income **$147.628B** × (1 − 19.0% Q2 2026 effective tax rate) = **$119.6B**
> ÷ 12,309M diluted shares = **core EPS of $9.71**

Multiples **21x / 26x / 31x** → **$203.91 / $252.46 / $301.01**.
*Why those multiples?* Alphabet's 10-year median P/E is about **27.5x**
([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/pettm/GOOGL) — an aggregator, used only as a sanity
check). We set the base one-and-a-half turns *below* its own history because cash conversion has
structurally worsened. 21x is a de-rating if the AI capex disappoints; 31x is the case where AI
monetisation proves out.

**Method 2 — Free-cash-flow yield.** We deliberately did **not** use trailing-twelve-month FCF per
share ($4.33) because it is a trough caused by a one-off build-out. We used **FY2025 FCF per share
of $5.99** ($73.266B ÷ 12,230M shares), the last complete fiscal year. Required yields
**4.0% / 3.4% / 2.8%** → **$149.75 / $176.18 / $213.93**.

**Blended band: $176.83 / $214.32 / $257.47.** At $350.16 the margin of safety is **−63.4%** —
the stock trades far above even the high end.

### Sensitivity — we show our work, including where it hurts our own conclusion

| View | Band | Margin of safety at $350.16 |
|---|---|---|
| P/E only (most generous defensible view) | $203.91 – $301.01 | **−38.7%** |
| Our blended headline | $176.83 – $257.47 | **−63.4%** |
| Stress: FCF yield on *actual* TTM FCF ($4.33/sh) | $108.25 – $154.64 | **−175%** |
| Blend **plus** net non-operating assets (+$22.39/sh) | ~$199 – ~$280 | **≈ −48%** |

That last row matters and we did not hide it: Alphabet holds $55.911B cash + $186.563B marketable
securities + $131.461B of non-marketable investments (Anthropic, SpaceX etc.) less $100.164B of
debt = **$273.771B, about $22.39 per share**, that our operating-earnings valuation ignores
completely. Adding it still leaves the stock ~48% above fair value.

**Every way we cut it, GOOGL is expensive on our framework.** That is not the same as saying the
market is wrong — the market is paying for AI optionality we are choosing not to underwrite.

## 6. The bull case (the real one)

- Revenue **accelerating** at $450B+ scale: +24% in Q2 2026 against a 12.5% three-year CAGR.
- Cloud is a genuine second engine: +82% revenue, margin from 20.7% → 35.6%, **$514B backlog**
  with >50% expected to convert to revenue within 24 months.
- **Search is not being eaten — yet.** Search revenue grew **17%** in Q2 2026, its fastest in
  years. AI Mode in Search passed 1 billion monthly users; the Gemini app 950 million.
- Fortress balance sheet: **$142.3B net cash**, ~37.7% ROIC, 60.9% gross margin.
- ~$10.75/share of hidden asset value in the Anthropic stake alone.
- Berkshire Hathaway put $10B in at ~$350/share in June 2026 — a quality signal, though note it is
  a signal about the *business*, not about the *price being cheap*.

## 7. The risks (written as hard as the bull case)

1. **Price.** ~36x our normalised core EPS versus a ~27.5x 10-year median. This is the main risk.
2. **Free cash flow has stopped.** Near-zero in H1 2026, negative in Q2, guided to stay under
   pressure. We are being asked to own a business that currently distributes almost no cash.
3. **The buyback is gone and we are being diluted** — $80B of equity issued, $40B ATM still
   running, preferred stock created.
4. **Earnings quality is poor.** $135.8B of six-month pre-tax "profit" is an unrealised mark on
   private AI companies. If AI private valuations fall, that reverses.
5. **The AI capex bet is unproven.** Capex at 5.25x depreciation means today's margins are
   flattered relative to the depreciation charge that is coming.
6. **Regulation on three fronts.** US Search case: final judgment entered Dec 2025 (distribution
   restrictions + forced data-sharing/syndication), on appeal in the D.C. Circuit. US ad tech: a
   forced AdX divestiture was **rejected** in Sept 2026 (a win), final judgment still being
   drafted. EU: an **€890M DMA fine** in July 2026 with compliance due ~21 Sept 2026 and daily
   penalties of up to 5% of worldwide daily turnover if missed, on top of a €2.95B adtech fine in
   Sept 2025. ([EC press release](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1670),
   [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/google-dodges-antitrust-breakup-of-ad-tech-business/))
7. **AI-native competition for the query** (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot). We could **not** find a
   trustworthy, consistent market-share number for this — aggregator estimates contradict each
   other badly — so we mark it **unknown** and watch Search revenue growth instead.
8. **68% of revenue is advertising**, which is cyclical, at exactly the moment capex commitments
   are locked in.
9. Google Network revenue is shrinking (−1%) and Other Bets losses are widening (−$1.799B).
10. Dual-class shares: we have essentially no vote on a capital-allocation decision of this size.

## 8. Sell triggers — the rules we follow later, written now while calm

1. Google Search & other revenue grows **<5%** year over year for two consecutive quarters (now +17%).
2. Total Alphabet revenue **declines** year over year in any quarter.
3. Google Cloud revenue growth falls **below 20%** for two consecutive quarters (now +82%), or the
   $514B backlog declines year over year.
4. Gross margin falls **below 52%** for two consecutive quarters (now 60.9%).
5. Operating margin falls **below 25%** for two consecutive quarters (now 34%).
6. Free cash flow is **negative for a full fiscal year**, or TTM FCF falls **below $25B** (now $53.3B).
7. Capex exceeds **50% of revenue** in a fiscal year (2026 guidance ≈ 43% of an annualised H1
   run-rate), or 2027 capex guidance exceeds **$280B** without matching Cloud acceleration.
8. Net debt turns **positive** and net-debt-to-EBITDA rises **above 1.0x** (now −0.82x, net cash).
9. Diluted share count rises **more than 5%** above 12,309M without the buyback restarting.
10. Estimated ROIC falls **below 15%** on a sustained basis (now ~37.7%).
11. A structural remedy actually takes effect forcing divestiture of Chrome, Android, AdX or DFP,
    or ending default search placement payments outright.
12. Non-marketable investments are written down **more than 40%** from $131.461B.
13. Any serious accounting red flag — restatement, SEC enforcement, a change in data-centre asset
    useful lives that boosts reported margins, or a material weakness in internal controls.

## 10. Honest bottom line

Alphabet is one of the highest-quality businesses we have looked at: a wide moat, 60.9% gross
margins, ~37.7% returns on capital, $142.3B of net cash, and revenue *accelerating* at half a
trillion dollars of scale. We would genuinely like to own it.

But three things stop us today. The price embeds roughly 36x normalised core earnings against a
27.5x history. Free cash flow — the number we care about most — has gone to roughly zero, and the
company has swung from buying back $46B of stock to issuing $80B of equity. And the headline
earnings that make the stock *look* cheap are paper gains on private AI stakes, not cash.

**Conviction 3/5. WATCH, DO NOT BUY at $350.16.** Our base fair value is $214.32. We would want to
see either a much lower price, or hard evidence that ~$200B a year of capital spending is earning a
return, before this becomes a buy.
