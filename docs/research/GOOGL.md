# Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) — Research Thesis

**Thesis ID:** GOOGL-2026Q2 | **As of:** Q2 2026 (quarter ended 2026-06-30) | **Retrieved:** 2026-07-27
**Price (Alpaca):** $325.31 | **Conviction:** 3 / 5 | **Data quality:** filing-backed

## What the business does (plain English)
Alphabet is the holding company for Google. It makes money in three parts:

- **Google Services — $94.5B in Q2 2026 (+15% YoY).** This is the profit engine. Advertisers pay
  Google to put ads next to search results and videos. Inside it: Search & other **$63.3B**,
  YouTube ads **$11.1B**, and subscriptions/platforms/devices (YouTube Premium, Google One, the
  Play store, Pixel phones) **$12.9B**.
- **Google Cloud — $24.8B in Q2 2026 (+82% YoY).** Renting computing power, databases, and AI
  models (Gemini, and Google's own TPU chips) to businesses. This segment made **$8.8B** of
  operating profit in the quarter alone, versus $13.1B in all of FY2025.
- **Other Bets — $0.4B revenue, −$1.8B operating loss.** Waymo self-driving cars, Verily, and
  other moonshots. Tiny revenue, real losses.

Total Q2 2026 revenue was **$119.8B, up 24%**, with operating profit of **$40.8B** (a 34%
operating margin).

> **Important warning about the headline profit.** Alphabet reported Q2 2026 net income of
> **$112.2B** and EPS of **$9.11**. Do not use those numbers. **$98.0B of that came from
> "other income"** — almost entirely *unrealized paper gains* on private company stakes
> (mainly Anthropic and SpaceX). No cash changed hands. Those marks can reverse. Throughout
> this note we value the **operating** business instead.
> ([Q2 2026 8-K Exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm))

## Why it has a moat (a wide one)
A "moat" is a lasting advantage that keeps competitors away. Alphabet has several:

- **Network effects / data flywheel.** Every search makes the next search better. More users →
  better results → more users. YouTube works the same way between creators and viewers.
- **Default distribution.** Android, Chrome, Maps, Gmail and YouTube each have over a billion
  users. Google starts every fight already standing in the doorway.
- **Scale economies.** Alphabet designs its own AI chips (TPUs) and builds its own data centres.
  Very few companies on earth can afford to own the whole AI stack — chips, data centres, models,
  and distribution.
- **Brand.** "Google" is literally a verb.

I rate the moat **wide**. The moat is not what worries me here — the *price of defending it* is.

**How ROIC was derived (FY2025, all inputs from the 10-K):** operating income $129.039B × (1 −
16.8% effective tax rate, itself $26.656B ÷ $158.826B) = NOPAT **$107.4B**. Invested capital =
equity $415.265B + long-term debt $46.547B − cash & marketable securities $126.843B = **$335.0B**.
$107.4B ÷ $335.0B = **32.1%**. The same calculation on FY2024 gives **39.1%** — so returns on
capital are already falling as the AI spending base builds. That is the single most important
trend line in this thesis.

## The one big thing: capex

This is the whole argument, so it deserves its own section.

Alphabet is spending an extraordinary amount of money on AI infrastructure. Capital spending has
gone from **$31.5B in FY2022** to **$91.4B in FY2025**, and on the Q2 2026 call CFO Anat Ashkenazi
**raised full-year 2026 guidance to $195–205B**, up from the $180–190B guided previously and the
$175–185B first given in January. Q2 capital spending alone was **$44.9B**.

The consequence is already visible in the cash flow statement. In the first half of 2026 Alphabet
generated **$84.9B** of operating cash flow and spent **$80.6B** on capex, leaving just **$4.3B**
of free cash flow for six months. **Q2 by itself was free-cash-flow negative at −$5.9B.**

Two honest readings:

- **Bull:** this is growth capex, not maintenance. Cloud is growing 82% and management says demand
  exceeds supply. Alphabet is buying a durable position in the most important computing platform
  shift in decades, and it can pay cash for it out of a $144B net-cash balance sheet.
- **Bear:** today's capex is tomorrow's depreciation expense. Depreciation was $21.1B in FY2025
  and **$13.6B in the first half of 2026 alone**. If AI demand disappoints, Alphabet will own a
  mountain of fast-depreciating silicon, margins compress, and the ~32% ROIC keeps sliding.

I do not know which is right, and I refuse to pretend I do. That uncertainty is why conviction is
3 and not 4.

## Fair value

The code (`code/valuation.py`) does all the arithmetic; I only supply cited inputs.

**Step 1 — build an honest EPS.** Reported trailing EPS (~$19.90) is garbage for valuation because
it is stuffed with paper gains on Anthropic and SpaceX. Instead:

> TTM operating income **$147.628B** (= FY2025 $129.039B − H1 2025 $61.877B + H1 2026 $80.466B)
> × (1 − 17.5% tax) ÷ **12,309M** diluted shares = **$9.89 normalized EPS**.

A 17.5% tax rate is slightly *above* Alphabet's actual FY2025 effective rate of 16.8%, so this is
deliberately conservative.

**Step 2 — apply sensible multiples.** 24× / 30× / 36×. Alphabet has historically traded in the
low-to-mid 20s on earnings; 30× as the base case gives credit for 24% revenue growth, an 82%-growth
cloud business, and a wide moat, without assuming the AI bet is already won.

|---|---|---|---|
| **P/E — $9.89 × 24 / 30 / 36** | **$237.36** | **$296.70** | **$356.04** |

**Fair value band: $237 low / $297 base / $356 high.**
At **$325.31** the margin of safety is **−9.6%** — we would be paying *above* our base estimate.
Verdict from the tool: *"fair, leaning expensive."*

**Two adjustments I made deliberately, in opposite directions:**

- *Conservative:* the band gives **no credit** for roughly **$21.8/share** of net cash
  ($144.3B) plus non-marketable equity stakes ($124.3B). That is real value not in the number.
- *Aggressive-sounding but honest:* I **excluded** the free-cash-flow method from the headline
  band. Running it on TTM FCF/share of $4.33 at 3.5%/3.0%/2.5% target yields gives
  **$124 / $144 / $173**, and blending it in would drop the band to **$181 / $221 / $265**. That
  method is only correct if *all* $132.4B of trailing capex is maintenance spending — which is
  plainly false when FY2022 capex was $31.5B. **But treat $124–173 as the genuine downside anchor
  if the AI buildout earns nothing.** The gap between $124 and $297 is the honest width of my
  uncertainty about this company right now.

## The risks (written as hard as the bull case)

1. **Capex shock.** $195–205B guided for 2026 versus $91.4B spent in 2025. Q2 free cash flow was
   already negative. This is the dominant risk and it is not hypothetical.
2. **Depreciation drag.** Today's spending becomes tomorrow's expense; margins can compress even
   with flat revenue.
3. **No margin of safety.** At $325.31 we are ~10% above base fair value, and far above the
   FCF-based downside anchor. We would be paying full price for a *successful* outcome.
4. **Earnings quality.** $98.0B of Q2's $112.2B net income was unrealized marks on private stakes.
   Those can reverse violently and say nothing about the operating business.
5. **AI could still eat search economics.** Chatbot answers may reduce monetisable queries or
   cost-per-click even in a world where Google wins the technology. Search revenue is still growing
   17%, so this has not happened — yet.
6. **Regulation.** Ongoing US antitrust actions over search distribution and ad tech, plus EU
   scrutiny. Remedies could ban default-search payments or force ad-tech divestiture.
7. **Advertising is cyclical.** Roughly 79% of revenue is still Google Services; a recession hits
   ad budgets quickly.
8. **Dual-class shares.** Founders keep voting control. Outside shareholders have little say over
   capital allocation — which matters enormously during a $200B/year spending program.

## Sell triggers (what would break this thesis)

These are the concrete, measurable rules the portfolio manager should use. If any fires, revisit
and probably sell:

1. **Google Services revenue declines YoY for two consecutive quarters** — the first real evidence
   AI is eating search.
2. **Google Cloud revenue growth falls below 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters while quarterly
   capex stays above $40B** — spending without demand.
3. **Free cash flow is negative for a full fiscal year, or TTM FCF falls below $25B.**
4. **Consolidated operating margin falls below 25% for two consecutive quarters** (currently 34%).
5. **Gross margin falls below 50% for two consecutive quarters** (currently ~60%).
6. **ROIC falls below 15% on a trailing basis for a full fiscal year** (currently ~32%).
7. **Net-debt-to-EBITDA rises above 1.5×** — the net cash cushion is gone and capex is debt-funded.
8. **A final, non-appealable antitrust remedy** forcing divestiture of Search, Android, YouTube or
   the ad-tech stack, or banning default-search-placement payments.
9. **A material accounting restatement, or a large write-down of the non-marketable equity
   portfolio** that reveals aggressive prior marks.
10. **Total revenue shrinks YoY for two consecutive fiscal years.**

## What would make me buy

- A pullback toward or below **$237** (the low end of the band), which would open a real margin
  of safety.
- Q3 2026 results (expected late October 2026) showing Cloud backlog converting to revenue and
  capex guidance *stabilising* — free cash flow would then recover sharply from the depressed
  $53.3B TTM level.

## Conviction: 3 / 5

A genuinely wonderful business — wide moat, ~60% gross margins, ~32% ROIC, $144B net cash — but
bought today at roughly 33× normalized operating earnings and a 1.3% free-cash-flow yield, in the
middle of the largest capital spending program in its history whose returns are unproven. Quality
says 4–5; price and capex uncertainty pull it to **3**. **Watch, do not chase.**
