# Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) — research thesis

- **As of:** 2026-Q2 (numbers reflect the quarter ended 2026-06-30)
- **Written:** 2026-08-06 · **Refresh due:** 2026-10-25 (Q3 2026 earnings)
- **Price:** $359.82 (Alpaca, 2026-08-06)
- **Fair value band:** low **$199** · base **$259** · high **$328**
- **Margin of safety:** **−39%** — the stock is *above* the top of our band
- **Conviction: 3 / 5** — a wonderful business at a price we do not want to pay
- **Verdict: WATCH, do not buy at $360.**

---

## 1. What the company actually does

Alphabet is the holding company for Google. Strip away the jargon and there are three businesses.

**Google Services — the money machine.** Advertising on Search, YouTube, and partner sites,
plus subscriptions (YouTube Premium, Google One) and devices (Pixel). In Q2 2026 it produced
**$94.5 billion of revenue (+15% year-on-year) and $39.5 billion of segment operating profit**.
Search & other alone was **$63.3 billion, +17%**. YouTube ads were $11.1 billion. This segment
is roughly 79% of revenue and essentially *all* of the group's profit.
([Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm))

**Google Cloud — the growth engine.** Renting computing power and AI services to businesses.
Q2 2026 revenue **$24.8 billion, up 82%**, with segment operating profit of **$8.8 billion**
(up from $2.8 billion a year earlier). Google Cloud exited 2025 at a **>$70 billion annual run
rate**. In March 2026 Alphabet bought the cloud-security company **Wiz for $29.5 billion** and
folded it into this segment.
([Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm),
[FY2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000012/googexhibit991q42025.htm),
[Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000071/goog-20260630.htm))

**Other Bets — the lottery tickets.** Mostly Waymo (self-driving taxis). Q2 2026 revenue
$0.4 billion against an operating **loss of $1.8 billion**. Interesting, not yet material.

Full year 2025: revenue **$402.8 billion**, operating profit **$129.0 billion** (32% margin),
net income **$132.2 billion**, diluted EPS **$10.81**.
([FY2025 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000012/googexhibit991q42025.htm))

## 2. The moat — **wide**

A "moat" is a lasting advantage that keeps competitors out. Alphabet has several stacked on
top of each other:

- **Network effects.** More searches → better results → more searches. YouTube is a two-sided
  network: creators go where the viewers are, viewers go where the creators are. Neither can be
  bought with money.
- **Brand and habit.** "Google it" is a verb. Default behaviour is extraordinarily sticky.
- **Distribution.** Chrome, Android, Maps, Gmail and Play put Google in front of billions of
  people every day. Advertisers cannot reach that audience anywhere else at that scale.
- **Scale economics in AI.** Alphabet owns the whole stack — its own chips (TPUs), its own data
  centres, its own frontier models (Gemini), and its own distribution. Perhaps three companies
  on earth can say that.

I rate this **wide**. The honest caveat is that a wide moat protects the *business*, not the
*share price*, and it does not protect against a technology shift that changes how people ask
questions in the first place (see risks).

## 4. Fair value — and why we think the price is too high

### The earnings problem you must understand first

Alphabet's reported profits have become misleading. In Q2 2026 net income was **$112.2 billion**
and EPS was **$9.11** — but **$99.0 billion of that was a paper gain on shares it owns in other
companies**, mostly **SpaceX**, which Alphabet now carries at about **$94.1 billion**. That single
item added **$6.26** to quarterly EPS. It is not cash, it is not from operating the business, and
it can reverse. Anyone quoting Alphabet's trailing P/E from a data website today is quoting
nonsense.
([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000071/goog-20260630.htm))

### Our normalized earnings

We start from operating profit instead:

> TTM operating income **$147.6B** × (1 − 17% tax) ÷ **12,309M** diluted shares = **$9.95 of
> normalized core EPS**.

### The multiples we chose

Alphabet is a wide-moat compounder currently growing revenue 24% and operating profit 30%. It
has historically traded in the low-to-mid 20s times earnings. We use a deliberately wide range
to reflect genuine uncertainty about the AI capex cycle:

| Multiple | Fair value |
|---|---|
| 20× (bear: Search monetization erodes, capex doesn't pay off) | **$199** |
| 26× (base: growth normalizes to mid-teens, capex earns its cost of capital) | **$259** |
| 33× (bull: Cloud keeps compounding, AI expands the pie) | **$328** |

At **$359.82** the stock is **39% above our base case and above the top of the band**. Put
differently, we would be paying today roughly what we think the business will be worth in
2028 if everything goes right.

### Why we dropped the free-cash-flow method

Normally we cross-check with a free-cash-flow yield. We **left it out of the band** because
Alphabet is mid-way through an unprecedented spending programme and trailing cash flow is not
representative of steady-state economics. For transparency: FY2025 free cash flow per share was
$5.99, which at a 3.5%/3.0%/2.5% target yield implies **$171 / $200 / $240** — *lower* still. So
dropping it is, if anything, generous to the bull case.

### Trend check (Alpaca data only)

Latest close **$359.80**; 50-day average **$357.19**; 200-day average **$328.63**. The stock is
above both averages — a clear uptrend. Its 250-day range is **$199.32 to $402.63**: it has roughly
doubled off the low. Momentum is with the bulls; value is not.

## 5. The risks — written as hard as the bull case

1. **We would be overpaying.** −39% margin of safety. That is the single biggest risk here.
2. **The capital spending is staggering and unproven.** Management guided **2026 capex to
   $175–185 billion** versus $91.4 billion spent in *all* of FY2025. Q2 2026 alone was $44.9
   billion, and free cash flow that quarter was **negative $5.9 billion**. If AI demand
   disappoints, Alphabet is left depreciating a mountain of hardware.
3. **The financing has changed character.** Buybacks went to **zero** in the first half of 2026
   (from $28.3 billion in H1 2025). Alphabet issued **$49.6 billion of equity**, opened a **$40
   billion at-the-market share-selling programme**, and issued **$20.3 billion of notes**. Shares
   outstanding rose from 12,088M to 12,230M. Owners are being diluted to fund the build.
4. **AI chat could eat Search.** Search advertising is essentially the entire profit pool. It is
   growing 17% today and management says AI features *increase* queries — but a shift of valuable
   commercial queries to assistants that monetize worse would hurt Alphabet more than anyone.
5. **Regulators are relentless.** The EU upheld the Android decision on 2026-07-02 and Alphabet
   paid **$5.2 billion** in July 2026. A further **$2.1 billion** was accrued in Q2 2026 for a
   Swedish judgment (PriceRunner/Klarna). Structural remedies remain possible.
6. **Advertising is cyclical.** A recession would hit revenue exactly when the capex commitments
   are largest.
7. **Reported earnings are now market-noise.** The same mark-to-market rules that produced a $99
   billion gain will produce enormous headline losses in a drawdown.

## 6. Sell triggers — the exact rules

If we ever own this, we sell (or at least trim hard) when any of these are true:

1. **Google Search & other revenue falls year-over-year for two consecutive quarters.**
2. **Google Cloud revenue growth falls below 20% year-over-year for two consecutive quarters.**
3. **Consolidated operating margin falls below 25% for two consecutive quarters** (from 34% now),
   **or** gross margin falls below 50% for two consecutive quarters.
4. **Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow turns negative, or stays below $30 billion, for two
   consecutive quarterly reports.**
5. **Net debt / EBITDA rises above 1.0x** — i.e. the $142.3B net cash position flips to real net
   debt, meaning the AI build is being financed rather than earned.
6. **Diluted share count rises more than 5% year-over-year.**
7. **Derived ROIC falls below 15% for four consecutive quarters.**
8. **A final, non-appealable order forcing divestiture of Chrome, Android, or the ad-tech stack**,
   or banning default-placement payments in a way that materially changes Search economics.
9. **Any serious accounting restatement or material governance red flag.**

## 7. What would make us buy

- Price falls toward **$260** (our base) or below — that is roughly a 28% decline from here.
- Cloud operating margin keeps expanding (35.6% in Q2 2026) *while* capex growth flattens —
  proof the spending is earning its keep.
- 2027 capex guided flat or down, which would let free cash flow snap back.
- Buybacks resume and the ATM equity programme is retired.
