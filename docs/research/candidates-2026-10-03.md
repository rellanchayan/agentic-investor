## Proposed additions

### 1. META — Meta Platforms, Inc. (Social Media / Communication)

Meta operates the four most-used social networking and messaging products in the world:
Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger. Across the "Family of Apps," approximately
3.3 billion people use at least one product daily. The business model is the same for all
four — free to users, monetised through advertising — but the scale is so large that no
advertiser targeting a mass consumer audience can credibly ignore it. Meta's advertising
platform is second only to Google in digital ad revenue globally.

The moat is primarily network effects: each additional user makes the platforms more
valuable to the users already there, which attracts more advertisers, which funds better
products, which attract more users. WhatsApp and Facebook also benefit from a different
network-effect dynamic — they are embedded in social infrastructure in ways that are very
hard to displace even if a superior alternative emerges (Snapchat, TikTok, and Threads have
all tested this and none has materially shifted time-spent away from the core apps at scale).

Financially, the business is exceptional. H1 2026 revenue was $117.1 billion ($56.3B Q1,
$60.8B Q2), up approximately 28–33% year-over-year (source: Meta Q1 2026 and Q2 2026
earnings press releases, SEC filings). Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow as of
June 2026 was approximately $41 billion (source: Gurufocus / financecharts.com citing
SEC cash-flow statements). Market cap as of late September 2026 was approximately
$1.88 trillion (source: Macrotrends market-cap history).

The one honest risk to flag: Q2 2026 net income fell 14% year-over-year to $15.8 billion
despite 28% revenue growth, because the company is ploughing enormous sums into AI
infrastructure and data-centre buildout (source: beancount.io Q2 2026 earnings analysis).
This capex cycle is deliberate and disclosed, but the research analyst should confirm that
it is temporary (i.e., the cash flow inflection turns back up) rather than structural
margin compression. If the capex investment produces the AI-driven ad-pricing and
recommendation improvements management projects, the moat deepens further. If it does
not, the investment will have been a poor use of capital.

**Moat type:** Network effects across four platforms at global scale.
**Gap filled:** Communication is represented only by DIS, which has entirely different
economics (content/parks). META fills the social-network moat we have no exposure to.
**Why watch now:** The stock pulled back from its highs on AI capex concerns in Q2 2026.
A clear fair-value model and entry threshold will let the analyst act quickly if any
further weakness brings the price toward a sensible margin of safety.

### 2. SPGI — S&P Global Inc. (Financial Data & Ratings / Financials)

S&P Global is a financial data and analytics company best known for its credit ratings
(the "S&P" in "S&P 500" is it), but ratings are only part of the story. The company
operates five segments: Ratings, Market Intelligence (data terminals and analytics for
financial professionals), Commodity Insights (Platts energy pricing benchmarks), Mobility
(vehicle and automotive data), and Indices (the S&P Dow Jones indices franchise, the most
widely tracked benchmark suite in the world). Together, the benchmark businesses — Ratings,
Indices, and Platts — account for roughly 65% of revenue and 80% of operating profit
(source: Rijnberink Invest Insights deep-dive analysis, citing company filings).

The moat is one of the most durable in the financial world. On the ratings side, S&P and
Moody's together control over 80% of the global credit-ratings market (source: Fool.com
"S&P Global and Moody's Have Rated Debt for More Than 100 Years," citing industry data).
Debt issuers need a rating from at least one of them for any significant offering; many
investment mandates and regulatory capital rules reference NRSRO ratings by name, making
a new entrant practically impossible to trust with a fresh mandate. The S&P 500 and
Dow Jones indices are even more entrenched — they are baked into trillions of dollars of
passive funds, derivatives, and compensation benchmarks that cannot easily switch without
a global coordination problem. Market Intelligence has an 85% subscription-revenue mix
with multi-year contracts and a 94% customer retention rate
(source: stockanalysis.com / companiesmarketcap.com, citing company filings).

Financially, H1 2026 GAAP revenue was approximately $8.3 billion ($4.17B Q1, $4.15B Q2),
up 10% year-over-year in both quarters (source: SEC Form 8-K filings, Q1 and Q2 2026
earnings releases). Adjusted operating margins expanded meaningfully: Q1 was 51.8%,
Q2 was 54.3% (source: S&P Global Q1 and Q2 2026 earnings release PDFs via SEC EDGAR and
q4cdn.com). The Ratings segment alone posted a 67.8% adjusted operating margin in Q1
(source: same). Market cap as of early October 2026 is approximately $114 billion
(source: companiesmarketcap.com; stockanalysis.com market-cap page).

**Moat type:** Regulatory entrenchment, intangible assets (100-year ratings brand), and
deep switching costs in both ratings (investment mandates) and data (94% retention).
**Gap filled:** We have no exposure to financial data, analytics, or ratings. SPGI is
the clearest "toll-bridge on capital markets" we could add — the more debt is issued
globally, the more ratings revenue grows.
**Why watch now:** A rising-rate or credit-stress environment can temporarily slow
issuance volumes and compress Ratings revenue — that has historically been the entry
window for SPGI. The analyst should build a fair-value model now so the team is ready
for any issuance-slowdown dip.

### 3. INTU — Intuit Inc. (Tax & Accounting Software / Technology)

Intuit makes the financial software that tens of millions of people and small businesses
depend on: TurboTax (the dominant US consumer tax-filing platform), QuickBooks (the
standard small-business accounting platform in North America), and Credit Karma
(personal credit monitoring, integrated with lending offers). The business model is
primarily subscription — once a small business builds its books, payroll, invoicing, and
banking on QuickBooks, switching to any competitor requires re-entering years of financial
history and retraining every employee, so almost nobody does. TurboTax benefits from a
similar return-filer lock: the prior-year return pre-populates the current year, and the
user's financial data is already in Intuit's system.

Fiscal 2026 (year ended July 31, 2026) results: total revenue grew 14% to approximately
$20 billion — the company crossed the $20B mark for the first time (source: Intuit FY2026
Q4 earnings press release, August 25, 2026, via investors.intuit.com and SEC Form 8-K).
QuickBooks Online Accounting revenue grew 23% and Online Ecosystem revenue (the broader
platform) grew 19% to $9.9 billion. TurboTax Live revenue grew 37%, now representing 53%
of total TurboTax revenue. Operating cash flow for FY2026 was $8.84 billion
(source: same press release). Free cash flow for the trailing twelve months as of April
2026 was approximately $7.7 billion, with a free-cash-flow margin near 38–39%
(source: Gurufocus FCF page; Alpha Spread; Macrotrends FCF history). Gross margin is
approximately 81% and operating margin approximately 27% (source: stockanalysis.com
citing SEC 10-K).

One honest concern the analyst must investigate: as of late September 2026, Intuit's
market cap had fallen to approximately $73.7 billion (source: Gurufocus market-cap page),
compared to roughly $138 billion in January 2026 — a decline of nearly 47% in nine months.
Revenue and FCF both continued to grow strongly during this period, so the compression
is almost certainly multiple-driven rather than fundamental. The most likely explanation
is market fear that generative-AI tools (LLMs that can prepare taxes or generate
accounting entries) could eventually erode the moat. Whether that fear is a long-term
thesis break or a temporary overreaction is exactly the question the research analyst
needs to answer before this name goes to the thesis stage. The business results to date
do not show any moat erosion, but the market is pricing in risk.

**Moat type:** Switching costs (data lock-in, annual filing workflow), intangible brand
assets, and a moderate network effect in the TurboTax expert network (53% of TurboTax
revenue now involves a human preparer assisted by the platform).
**Gap filled:** Technology has eight names but none in tax or SMB financial software.
Intuit is the category leader with an 80%+ gross margin and $7–8B of annual FCF.
**Why watch now:** The ~47% compression from the January 2026 high brings the valuation
much closer to a range where a fair-value model might yield an actionable entry threshold.
The analyst should assess the AI disruption risk explicitly — if the moat is intact, this
could be the entry window the playbook warns us to prepare for in calm markets.

## Names considered and rejected

**Moody's (MCO):** MCO is essentially the same business as SPGI in the ratings duopoly.
Both are very high quality. SPGI is preferred here because it has the more diversified
revenue mix (indices, Market Intelligence, Platts) alongside ratings, which makes earnings
less sensitive to issuance cycles. The analyst could revisit MCO if they prefer a purer
ratings exposure, but adding both would be redundant.

**Netflix (NFLX):** Real FCF, dominant in streaming. Rejected because the moat case is
weaker than it looks — content is expensive and rivalrous (every major media company
competes for the same talent and IP), and switching costs for consumers are near-zero
(monthly cancel-anytime subscriptions). The business is profitable and growing, but
"dominant today" is different from "durable moat." Not proposed.

**Mastercard (MA) is already on the watchlist** — mentioned here as a reminder that the
financial-data gap is specifically about information and analytics, not more payment
networks.

## Analyst priority for next week

Given the playbook lesson that bench expansion carries a compounding cost (~1 pp/month),
the two names most worth moving immediately toward a full thesis are:

1. **META** — the moat is straightforward (network effects at 3B+ DAU scale), the business
   is massively profitable, and Communication is our emptiest sector. The main open
   question is whether the AI capex ramp is temporary — one earnings-call review and a
   capex-normalization scenario should answer it. A thesis could be completed in one session.

2. **SPGI** — the moat is arguably the most durable of the three (regulatory entrenchment +
   century-old brand), margins are elite, and the business model is easy to understand.
   The main modeling question is how to normalise for issuance cycles in the Ratings
   segment. That is one step of complexity beyond META but still very doable.

INTU is high-quality but the AI disruption question requires a dedicated assessment
before the analyst should set a fair-value number. Do not skip that question — a
reduced-moat INTU is worth considerably less than the current trading price.
