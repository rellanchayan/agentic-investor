## Candidates considered

### PROPOSED — LIN (Linde plc)

**Business in two sentences:** Linde is the world's largest industrial-gases company,
supplying oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, and specialty gases to healthcare, manufacturing,
chemicals, and electronics customers. It operates a distribution network of pipelines and
cylinders that customers integrate deeply into their own processes.

**Moat:** Oligopoly structure (Linde and Air Products together dominate; Air Liquide is the
third); regional pipeline networks are enormously capital-intensive to replicate; most large
customers sign long-term take-or-pay contracts; gases are a tiny fraction of the customer's
cost but critical to operations, so switching creates serious operational risk.

**Size and liquidity:** Market cap approximately $225B as of late July/August 2026. LIN trades
on NASDAQ, highly liquid.

**Profitability check:** Q2 2026 — operating cash flow $2.27B, capex $1.44B, free cash flow
$833M for the quarter (~$3.3–3.5B annualized). Operating margin 29.5%. The company has raised
its dividend for 33 consecutive years. Record backlog of $8.1B in gas-supply contracts as of
Q2 2026.

**Sector gap filled:** Materials — the only sector we had zero coverage in.

### PROPOSED — ADP (Automatic Data Processing)

**Business in two sentences:** ADP processes payroll for over one million businesses worldwide,
and also provides HR outsourcing, benefits administration, talent management, and compliance
services. Clients run their entire employee lifecycle through ADP's systems.

**Moat:** Extremely high switching costs — moving payroll to a competitor means migrating
employee records, benefits integrations, tax filings, and compliance history; most businesses
do this only under duress. ADP also holds a float on client funds between collection and
disbursement, generating interest income. It is a Dividend King with 50+ consecutive annual
dividend increases.

**Size and liquidity:** Market cap estimated at roughly $100–120B. ADP trades on NASDAQ,
highly liquid.

**Profitability check:** FY2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) — revenue $21.9B (+6.7% YoY),
net margin ~20%, free cash flow ~$5.2B (~24% FCF margin). One commentary cited the stock
as at a decade-low valuation relative to its own history following a ~35% peak-to-trough
drop (unverified; check directly against ADP filings).

**Sector gap filled:** Business services within Industrials — no payroll/HR name was on the
watchlist.

### PROPOSED — SPGI (S&P Global Inc.)

**Business in two sentences:** S&P Global provides credit ratings, financial data, and
market indices — it rates debt issuances through S&P Ratings, runs Platts (commodity data),
and owns the S&P 500 and Dow Jones indices. The company earns fees every time a bond is
issued and every time a fund benchmarks itself to its indices.

**Moat:** Two-firm oligopoly on credit ratings (S&P + Moody's); issuers of bonds must get
rated by at least one of them or the bond cannot be sold to most institutional investors.
Index licensing is similarly locked in — a fund that drops "S&P 500" from its name would
lose assets overnight. These are as close to toll-booth economics as exist in financial
markets.

**Size and liquidity:** Market cap approximately $120–140B as of mid-2026. SPGI trades on
NYSE; average daily volume ~2M shares.

**Profitability check:** S&P Global 10-Q (period ending June 30, 2026) — operating cash flow
$2.48B for first half of 2026; free cash flow $2.25B H1 2026. TTM FCF approximately $5.57B,
FCF margin ~34.6%.

**Sector gap filled:** Financials sub-sector — we had banks (JPM), payments (V, MA), and
insurance (PGR) but no financial data/infrastructure.

## Candidates considered but rejected

### META (Meta Platforms) — REJECTED

Meta's network-effect moat (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp) is real and durable. Revenue was
$60.8B in Q2 2026 (+28% YoY). However, FY2026 capex guidance is $125–145B for AI
infrastructure. Q2 2026 FCF was only $784M (a 91% YoY drop); FCF may turn negative in H2.
Our criteria requires "positive free cash flow (real leftover cash)" and Meta does not meet
that bar right now. If AI capex moderates and FCF recovers, Meta deserves a revisit.
