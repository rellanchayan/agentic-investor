## Proposed additions

### 1. SPGI — S&P Global Inc. (Financial Data & Ratings / Financials)

S&P Global runs three businesses that are each close to irreplaceable. First, Ratings:
S&P and Moody's together have rated the vast majority of the world's bond issuances for
over a century; issuers are legally required to obtain a rating from a Nationally
Recognized Statistical Rating Organization (NRSRO), and meaningful scale outside the top
two is effectively impossible given the historical acceptance of ratings in debt contracts
globally. Second, the index franchise: the S&P 500 index is the most referenced financial
benchmark in the world; anyone licensing it for an ETF, fund product, or structured note
pays S&P Global a small, perpetual royalty — and the license effectively cannot be moved.
Third, Market Intelligence (formerly SNL/Capital IQ): a data and analytics platform with
high workflow integration and switching costs for institutional financial users.

The financial profile reflects these advantages. FY2025 revenue was approximately $15.3
billion, growing ~8% year over year. Free cash flow for FY2025 was approximately $5.46
billion (sources: [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spgi/financials/income-statement/),
[thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/financials/spgi)). Q1 2026 gross margin
was approximately 70.5% and operating margin approximately 43.9%. Market cap was
approximately $124–132 billion as of mid-2026 (source:
[equibles](https://equibles.com/stocks/SPGI)). NYSE-listed (ticker: SPGI), large and
highly liquid. Balance sheet carries ~$11–14B in debt, mostly legacy from the IHS Markit
merger, well-covered by operating cash flow.

**Moat type:** Regulatory barrier in ratings (NRSRO duopoly), brand/incumbent network
effects in index licensing, switching costs in financial data workflows.
**Gap filled:** We have payments and banking (V, MA, JPM, PGR) but no financial
information infrastructure company. SPGI's economics differ materially — it is essentially
a toll-booth on global capital markets activity, not a balance-sheet-risk business.
**Why watch now:** SPGI has compounded at high rates for a decade. The question is price,
not quality. The research analyst should build a fair-value model so we are ready if any
macro softness or rates-related multiple compression creates an entry window.

### 2. ASML — ASML Holding NV (Semiconductor Equipment / Technology)

ASML is the sole manufacturer of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems — the
machines that print the circuit patterns on the world's most advanced semiconductor chips.
Every leading-edge chip fabricator (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) must buy from ASML for anything
at 3nm or below. There is no second supplier; a 30-year, multi-billion-dollar development
program and physics constraints have made it essentially impossible for a competitor to
replicate the technology. ASML itself supplies more than 200 sub-components from over 800
suppliers globally and is the sole integrator of the complete system.

The financial results in 2026 confirm the strength of demand. ASML raised its full-year
2026 revenue guidance to €43–45 billion in July 2026, the second raise in the year, after
Q2 2026 revenue came in at approximately €9.3 billion (+21% year over year) with net
profit of approximately €2.9 billion. Gross margin guidance for 2026 is 54–56%
(sources: [investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/earnings/asml-q2-outlook-tops-on-ai-chip-demand-lifts-fullyear-forecast-4792148),
[tikr.com Q1 recap](https://tikr.com/blog/asml-stock-raises-2026-guidance-after-e8-8b-q1-revenue-beat)).
Market cap has been reported in the range of approximately $500–674 billion in mid-2026
(sources: [Investing.com ADR snapshot](https://uk.investing.com/equities/asml-holdings),
[tickerleague](https://tickerleague.com/companies/ASML/financials/market-cap)). ASML
trades on NASDAQ as an ADR under the clean ticker ASML.

Note: ASML's Q3 2026 earnings were scheduled for mid-October 2026; results were not yet
in our search results as of this writing. Verify the most recent quarterly figures before
any thesis work.

**Moat type:** Absolute technological monopoly in EUV lithography; no second supplier
exists anywhere in the world for this product.
**Gap filled:** We have chip designers and fabless companies (NVDA, AVGO) but nothing in
the equipment layer. ASML is also our first international-quality ADR candidate, filling
the international gap noted in the watchlist review.
**Why watch now:** AI-driven chip demand has pushed ASML's guidance upward twice in 2026.
At a market cap of $500B+, the stock is unlikely to be cheap. But the analyst should build
a model now — a tariff scare, export-control news on China, or a semiconductor industry
correction could create a meaningful entry window quickly in a stock this cyclically
sensitive.

### 3. RTX — RTX Corporation (Aerospace & Defense / Industrials)

RTX (formerly Raytheon Technologies, renamed RTX Corp) is the world's largest aerospace
and defense contractor by revenue. Its two businesses are structurally very different and
both have strong moat characteristics. Pratt & Whitney makes jet engines for commercial
aircraft (A320neo, wide-bodies) and military jets. Once an airline fleet adopts P&W
engines, it is locked into P&W maintenance, parts, and overhaul services for the 25-30
year life of those engines — the aftermarket business is highly recurring, high-margin,
and predictable. Raytheon makes missiles, air-defense systems (Patriot), and electronic
warfare systems for defense ministries worldwide. Defense contracts are multi-year, funded
by government appropriations, and involve classified technology that creates near-permanent
supplier relationships.

Q2 2026 sales were $24.7 billion, up 14% year over year, with adjusted EPS up 21%
(source: [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000101829/000010182926000025/a2026-07x238xkerexhibit99.htm),
[tikr.com Q1 recap](https://www.tikr.com/blog/rtx-q1-2026-22-1b-revenue-and-21-eps-growth-back-the-defense-thesis)).
Full-year 2026 free cash flow guidance was raised to $8.5–8.75 billion. The order backlog
reached a record $289 billion in Q2 2026, up 22% year over year — $170B commercial and
$119B defense (source: [quartr.com Q2 summary](https://quartr.com/events/rtx-corporation-rtx-q2-2026_ojGnguJR)).
Market cap was approximately $290–302 billion as of August 2026
(source: [equibles](https://equibles.com/stocks/RTX)). NYSE-listed (ticker: RTX), large
and highly liquid.

**Moat type:** Long-cycle switching costs in jet engine aftermarket (P&W); classified
technology and multi-year government contracts in defense (Raytheon).
**Gap filled:** We have industrial machinery (CAT, HON) and railroads (UNP, CSX) under
industrials, but no aerospace or defense business. RTX's revenue profile — long-term
contracts, recurring aftermarket, government-backed backlog — is genuinely different.
**Why watch now:** Defense budgets globally have been rising since 2022 and the trend has
not reversed. At the same time, commercial air travel recovery is still flowing through the
P&W aftermarket. A record $289B backlog provides unusual revenue visibility. The analyst
should build a fair-value model so an entry price is defined before any price softness
arrives.

## Names considered and rejected this week

**MCO (Moody's Corporation):** The natural pair to SPGI in credit ratings. Moody's has
essentially the same NRSRO regulatory moat as S&P Global. SPGI is preferred here because
it is larger, more diversified (index licensing, Commodity Insights/Platts), and has the
cleaner growth profile at the company level since the IHS Markit integration has largely
settled. If the analyst prefers Moody's, it would pass the same smell test and the moat
case is equally strong.

**LMT (Lockheed Martin):** The defense-only alternative to RTX. LMT is more purely a
defense contractor (F-35, missiles, space) without the commercial aerospace exposure of
RTX. RTX is preferred here because the Pratt & Whitney commercial aftermarket provides a
non-government revenue stream that partially offsets the political/budget cycle risk of
pure defense. LMT is not disqualified — if defense is the analyst's focus, LMT's F-35
program lock-in is arguably a stronger moat in its specific program.

**CME Group (CME):** CME operates futures and options exchanges with genuine natural
monopoly economics — futures contracts have strong network effects (liquidity begets
liquidity, and the dominant exchange in any contract is almost impossible to displace). CME
would fill the "financial exchanges" gap. Not proposed this week only because three names
felt sufficient; CME is worth revisiting as a fourth financial-infrastructure candidate
alongside SPGI if the analyst wants to build that bench.

## Analyst priority for next week

Given the playbook's standing lesson on cash drag (~1 pp/month cost from an inadequate
bench), the two names most worth moving to a full thesis first are:

1. **RTX** — Business model is highly understandable (jet engines + defense), the moat
   case is well-documented in public filings, and the $289B backlog makes revenue
   projections relatively tractable. A thesis could be completed in one session.

2. **SPGI** — Three-segment business model is more complex than RTX, but all three
   segments are well-understood and publicly documented. Revenue is highly predictable,
   FCF margins are among the highest in the S&P 500, and the moat is as durable as
   anything on our watchlist.

ASML is high quality but carries more earnings-cycle sensitivity (semiconductor capex
cycles), and the market cap above $500B means the entry bar for margin of safety is a
large absolute number. The thesis work is right but the entry opportunity may take longer
to arrive than RTX or SPGI.
