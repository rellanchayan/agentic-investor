## 1. What the company actually does

Johnson & Johnson is now a pure healthcare company. In November 2023 it spun off its consumer
brands (Band-Aid, Tylenol, Listerine) into a separate company called **Kenvue**, so anything you
read about "J&J the baby-powder company" is about a business it no longer owns. Everything below
is the *new* JNJ: prescription drugs plus medical devices.

Two segments ([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/200406/000020040626000016/jnj-20251228.htm)):

| Segment | FY2025 sales | Share | Pre-tax margin | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Innovative Medicine** | **$60.401B** (+6.0%) | 64% | **36.9%** | Patented prescription drugs |
| **MedTech** | **$33.792B** (+6.1%) | 36% | **12.2%** | Surgical tools, implants, heart devices, contact lenses |
| **Total** | **$94.193B** (+6.0%) | | | |

**The drug side** makes almost all the profit. Its biggest sellers in FY2025:

| Drug | FY2025 sales | vs 2024 |
|---|---|---|

| INVEGA franchise (schizophrenia) | $3,810M | −9.8% |
| IMBRUVICA (blood cancer) | $2,823M | −7.1% |

Look at that table carefully — it is the whole story. Oncology grew 22.1% to $25.4B. Immunology
*shrank* 11.8% to $15.7B because STELARA lost patent protection and cheap biosimilar copies
arrived. JNJ grew 6% anyway. That is what diversification buys you.

**The device side** — Surgery $10.1B (+3.0%), Orthopaedics $9.3B (+1.1%), Cardiovascular $8.9B
(+15.8%, helped by the Shockwave and Abiomed acquisitions), Vision $5.5B (+6.3%) — is slower and
much less profitable, but it is steadier and less exposed to patent cliffs.

## 2. Why it has a moat (and what "moat" means here)

A **moat** is a lasting advantage that keeps competitors away. JNJ's is **wide**, and it comes
from four different places at once:

1. **Patents.** A new drug gets ~10-13 years of legal monopoly. Nobody can copy DARZALEX.
2. **Surgeon switching costs.** A surgeon trained on JNJ's knee system or electrophysiology
   catheters does not casually switch — retraining is risky and hospitals sign multi-year
   contracts. The disposables that go with each device are a recurring revenue stream.
3. **Scale.** $14.665B of R&D a year (FY2025) and a global sales force in essentially every
   country. Very few companies can run 100+ clinical trials at once.
4. **Balance sheet.** Net debt of only ~$28B against ~$34B of annual cash earnings means JNJ can
   simply *buy* innovation it didn't invent — $17.5B on Intra-Cellular in 2025, $15.1B on
   Shockwave in 2024.

The honest caveat: **a drug moat has an expiry date printed on it.** Unlike a brand or a network,
every patent runs out. JNJ's moat is really "we are better than anyone else at replacing what
expires." STELARA went from $10.4B to $6.1B in one year. The company has to do that trick again
and again, forever.

**ROIC arithmetic (shown so you can check it).** Reported pre-tax income was $32.581B, but that

## 4. The talc situation, told straight

This is the single most-asked question about JNJ, so here is the full picture.

- JNJ's former talc-based baby powder is alleged to have caused ovarian cancer and mesothelioma.
  Tens of thousands of people have sued.
- JNJ tried **three times** to end it all at once through a bankruptcy manoeuvre. All three
  failed. The third, an ~$8B/25-year plan, was **rejected in March 2025**, and the FY2025 10-K
  records that "litigation in the tort system recommenced."
- That rejection is why FY2025 earnings contain a **+$7.0B reserve reversal** — JNJ released money
  it had set aside for a settlement that was no longer happening.
- Individual trials since then have been mixed: a **>$1.5B** mesothelioma verdict in Baltimore, a
  **$40M** ovarian verdict in Los Angeles, but a **defence win** in a six-week California ovarian
  bellwether in June 2026.
- **On 2026-07-27, JNJ announced an agreed comprehensive resolution** of the ovarian talc
  litigation with the lead plaintiff firms
  ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/200406/000020040626000155/jnj-20260727.htm)):
  a **$5.5 billion commitment**, first payment capped at **$3B in 2027**, nothing more due before
  2028, covering roughly **76,000 remaining ovarian claims**.
- **It is conditional.** It requires "the express participation of at least of 95% of the
  remaining claims." If the plaintiff bar does not deliver 95%, the deal can fall apart — and
  three prior attempts already did.
- **A charge is still coming.** At 2026-Q2 the reserve was only **~$3.7B** present value. The
  settlement is $5.5B and was announced after the quarter ended. Expect a top-up charge in Q3
  2026 GAAP results.
- **What it does *not* cover:** mesothelioma claims, state consumer-protection suits, Canadian
  litigation, and a securities class action that survived a Supreme Court cert denial in April
  2026 and is now in expert discovery.

**How to size it:** $5.5B is roughly 3½ months of JNJ's free cash flow and about 0.85% of its
market value. Against ~$34B of annual EBITDA, this is affordable, not existential. The problem
was never the size; it was the *uncertainty*. That uncertainty is now mostly — but not entirely —
priced and resolved. Which brings us to the real risk.

## 5. Valuation — and why we are not buying

Two methods, arithmetic done by `code/valuation.py` (not by hand):

**Method 1 — Price-to-earnings.** JNJ's own raised FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance midpoint is
**$11.68**. Multiples of **15x / 17.5x / 20x** → **$175.20 / $204.40 / $233.60**.
*Why those multiples?* The drug-manufacturer industry median forward P/E is around **14.7x**
([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/forward-pe-ratio/JNJ), cross-check only). Our base of
17.5x is a deliberate ~19% premium to peers for JNJ's diversification, fortress balance sheet,
64-year dividend record and now-reduced talc risk. It is *not* the ~23x the market pays today.

**Method 2 — Free-cash-flow yield.** TTM FCF/share **$9.16**, at target yields of
**5.0% / 4.2% / 3.5%** → **$183.20 / $218.10 / $261.71**.

**Blended band: low $179.20 · base $211.25 · high $247.66.**

At **$268.31**, JNJ trades:
- **~23.0x** the FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance midpoint,
- **~29x** trailing free cash flow (a **3.4%** FCF yield),
- **2.0%** dividend yield,
- **21%** above our base fair value and **8%** above the *high* end of our band.

Context from our own Alpaca feed: the 52-week closing range is **$174.20 – $278.45**. The stock is
up **~54%** from that low. At $174.20 it was **14.9x** the very same 2026 guidance. Nothing about
the earnings power changed by 54% in a year — **the multiple did**. Most of the last year's return
was re-rating on the talc resolution, and a re-rating that has already happened is not a reason
to buy; it is the reason the cushion is gone.

Price is above its 50-day ($262.77) and 200-day ($237.13) averages — a clear uptrend. Momentum is
not our reason to own anything, but it tells us the market already likes this story.

**Entry discipline:**
- Do not buy above **$211** (base fair value).
- A first tranche becomes defensible at or below **~$190** (≈10% discount to base).
- A full position only below **$179** (low end of the band).

## 6. The real risks (as hard as the bull case)

1. **Price is the biggest risk.** Paying 23x for ~6% sales growth and ~7–8% EPS growth means your
   return depends on the multiple holding. If it drifts back to 17.5x, you lose ~21% even if the
   business performs exactly as guided.
2. **The talc deal is not done.** 95% participation is a high bar, and JNJ has missed the bar
   three times before.
3. **More talc charges and a leftover tail.** A top-up charge is likely in Q3 2026, and
   mesothelioma, consumer-protection, Canadian and securities claims remain open.
4. **The patent treadmill never stops.** STELARA −41.3%, IMBRUVICA −7.1%, ZYTIGA −20.4%, INVEGA
   −9.8% in FY2025 alone. Under the Inflation Reduction Act, XARELTO and STELARA were subject to
   government-set Medicare prices from 2026, and in January 2026 CMS added **ERLEADA** — a $3.6B
   drug growing 19% — to the 2028 list. Government price-setting is a permanent, widening drag.
5. **MedTech is the weak half.** 5.4% operational growth in FY2025 and only **3.6%** in Q2 2026,
   at a 12.2% pre-tax margin. Orthopaedics grew 1.1%; Spine/Sports fell 2.5%. China's
   volume-based procurement and pulsed-field-ablation competition in electrophysiology are real.
6. **Growth is partly bought, not built.** $17.5B of acquisitions in FY2025, $15.1B in FY2024.
   Goodwill plus intangibles are **$99.2B of $199.2B** total assets. Disappointing pipeline assets
   turn into impairments.
7. **Reported earnings are noisy.** Do not trust any single year's GAAP EPS for this company.

## 7. Sell triggers — the rules we will follow

These are written now, in calm conditions, and are the rules the portfolio manager uses later.

1. The proposed ovarian talc resolution fails to reach ~95% claimant participation and is
   abandoned or materially repriced, **or** total talc reserves rise above **$10B**.
2. Total company revenue declines year-over-year for **two consecutive fiscal years**.
3. Innovative Medicine sales decline year-over-year for **two consecutive quarters** on a
   constant-currency basis.
4. Gross margin falls below **62%** for two consecutive quarters.
5. Net-debt-to-EBITDA rises above **2.0x**, or total debt exceeds **$80B**, without a clearly
   value-accretive acquisition behind it.
6. Free cash flow falls below **$12B** in a full fiscal year, or fails to cover the dividend.
7. Normalized ROIC falls below **12%** for two consecutive fiscal years.
8. The dividend is cut or the 60-plus-year increase streak is broken.
9. Goodwill/intangible impairments exceed **$10B** in any rolling two-year period.
10. Any accounting restatement, disputed auditor change, or material governance red flag.
11. **Price discipline:** do not buy above **$211**; first tranche at or below **~$190**; full
    position below **$179**.

## 9. Bottom line

Johnson & Johnson is exactly the kind of business this portfolio is built to own: wide moat,
67.9% gross margins, ~19% ROIC, ~$20B of annual free cash flow, 0.8x leverage, 64 straight years
of dividend increases, and a genuine growth engine in oncology that is absorbing a brutal patent
cliff without breaking stride. The talc nightmare, which suppressed the stock for a decade, now
has a price tag on it — $5.5B, payable from 2027 — and that price tag is affordable.

The problem is that the market figured all of this out first. The stock is up ~54% from its
52-week low and trades at ~23x forward adjusted earnings, roughly 21% above our base fair value of
$211 and above the top of our band. **Conviction 3/5: high confidence in the business, no
confidence in the entry price.** Put it on the bench, set the alert at $211, and be genuinely
happy to buy below $190. A missed buy costs nothing; an overpriced buy costs money.
