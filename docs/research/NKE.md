# NIKE, Inc. (NKE) — Research Thesis

## 1. What the company actually does

NIKE sells athletic footwear, apparel and equipment under three brands: NIKE, Jordan and Converse.

It does not own most of its factories. It designs the product, pays athletes and teams to make people want it, contracts the manufacturing out (mostly Vietnam, Indonesia and China), and keeps the difference between what a shoe costs to make and what the swoosh lets it charge. That difference is the whole business.

In fiscal 2026 NIKE took in **$46.4 billion** of revenue ([Q4 FY26 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000076/q4fy26exhibit991er.htm)):

| Split | FY2026 revenue | Change |
|---|---|---|
| NIKE Brand | $45.2B | — |
| Converse | $1.2B | **-31%** |
| **By channel** | | |
| Wholesale (Foot Locker, Dick's, JD Sports…) | $27.5B | +6% |
| NIKE Direct (nike.com, SNKRS, own stores) | $17.7B | **-6%** (digital -12%, stores -4%) |
| **By region** | | |
| North America | $20.5B | +5% |
| EMEA | $12.6B | +3% |
| Greater China | $5.8B | **-11%** |
| Asia-Pacific & Latin America | $6.2B | flat |

The shape of that table is the whole story: NIKE is rebuilding the wholesale channel it walked away from a few years ago, and it is shrinking in its two most profitable places — its own digital store and China.

## 2. Does it have a moat? (A "moat" is a lasting advantage that keeps rivals out.)

**Our answer: narrow, and being worn down right now.**

What's still working:
- **Brand.** People pay more for a swoosh. That shows up as a **42.9% gross margin** — of every dollar of sales, 42.9 cents is left after the direct cost of the product.
- **Returns on capital.** NIKE earned an estimated **22.3% ROIC** in FY2026 — 22 cents of after-tax operating profit for every dollar invested in the business. Anything above ~10-15% and steady is a sign of a real advantage. (Our calculation; formula in section 3. It excludes lease liabilities, so treat it as flattering by a few points.)
- **Scale.** Nobody else can outspend NIKE on athletes, teams and sourcing.

Why we will *not* call it wide:
- Revenue has **shrunk** at a **-3.2% annual rate** over three years (FY2023 $51.2B → FY2026 $46.4B).
- **Greater China has declined for eight consecutive quarters** ([Quartz](https://qz.com/nike-stock-12-year-low-jpmorgan-downgrade-081826)).
- NIKE Digital fell 12%; Converse — a brand NIKE owns outright — fell 31%.
- Running footwear, the hottest category, grew about **8.9%** year-on-year while **NIKE lost share** to HOKA, On, New Balance and adidas. On did CHF 3.01B of 2025 sales (+30%); HOKA did $653M in the June-2026 quarter (+19.8%) ([YipitData](https://www.yipitdata.com/resources/blog/corporate-footwear-market-trends-running-growth)).

A wide moat holds share. NIKE's is losing it. If FY2027-FY2028 shows genuine stabilisation, we will revisit the rating.

## 4. Fair value: what we think it's worth, and how we got there

Arithmetic done by `code/valuation.py`, not by hand.

**Method 1 — P/E on trailing earnings.** FY2026 diluted EPS **$2.10** × **13x / 17x / 21x** = **$27.30 / $35.70 / $44.10**.
- *13x* = the brand premium is permanently impaired and earnings keep sliding.
- *17x* = revenue and margin stabilise with no real growth — a fair multiple for a no-growth but high-ROIC consumer brand.
- *21x* = the "Win Now" turnaround works and margins head back toward the ~11% EBIT margin of FY2024.
- We did **not** use the ~30x NIKE commanded in its growth years. That company isn't the one in front of us.

**Method 2 — Free-cash-flow yield.** FY2026 FCF per share **$1.475** ÷ required yields of **6.0% / 5.0% / 4.0%** = **$24.58 / $29.50 / $36.88**. We demand a higher yield than we would for a steady compounder because FCF fell 33% last year and didn't cover the dividend.

**Blended fair value (average of the two bands):**

| Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|
| **$25.94** | **$32.60** | **$40.49** |

**Margin of safety at $36.34 = −11.5%.** The price is *above* our base estimate. Verdict from the model: *"fair, leaning expensive."*

### The honest caveat about the earnings number
FY2026 EPS is distorted in two directions and we cannot cleanly separate them:
- It **includes** a one-time **$0.52** benefit from an expected ~$986M IEEPA tariff refund. Strip it out and clean EPS is **$1.58** — on the same 13/17/21x that's **$20.54 / $26.86 / $33.18**, i.e. the stock would be clearly expensive.
- But it also **absorbed** a year of tariff *costs* that may not recur. Management said the tariff headwind persists through Q1 FY2027 with margin expansion expected from Q2 FY2027 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/nike-nke-q4-2026-earnings.html)).

We deliberately did **not** pick whichever adjustment made the stock look cheaper. We used the reported GAAP figure and widened the multiple range instead.

## 5. The bull case

1. Even in a bad year the brand earned a 42.9% gross margin and 22.3% ROIC on $46.4B of revenue.
2. **Net cash** of ~$1.1B, and $2.0B of long-term debt repaid in FY2026. The balance sheet cannot force a bad decision; NIKE can fund a multi-year fix.
3. **Q4 showed the first margin evidence**: EBIT $1,321M, up 346% y/y, with wholesale +4%.
4. The plan is coherent: deliberately shrink low-quality discounted digital revenue, rebuild wholesale shelf space (+6%) and performance/running product.
5. The ~$986M tariff receivable should be a one-time cash inflow in FY2027, and removal of the ~$1B annual tariff cost would be a real earnings tailwind.
6. Sentiment is washed out: a 12-year low, worst stock in the Dow in 2026, removal from the S&P 100 on 21 September 2026, downgrades from JPMorgan (Underweight) and Baird ($70 → $44 target). The bad news is known.

## 6. The risks — stated as hard as the bull case

1. **China may be broken, not soft.** $5.8B, -11% (-13% currency-neutral), eighth straight quarterly decline. JPMorgan flags a China online-marketplace reset from **January 2027** worth **more than $1B a year** of unmitigated revenue headwind — about 20% of the region ([Quartz](https://qz.com/nike-stock-12-year-low-jpmorgan-downgrade-081826)).
2. **Structural share loss.** Running grew ~8.9%; NIKE lost share to On and HOKA at the very retailers it is trying to win back.
3. **The dividend is not covered.** $2,407M paid against $2,184M of FCF — a 110% payout. Buybacks already cut 95%. Something has to give if FCF doesn't recover.
4. **The earnings base is flattered** by $0.52 of one-time tariff recovery. On clean earnings the stock is ~23x — not cheap for a shrinking business.
5. **Converse is collapsing** (-31%). Proof that a NIKE-owned brand can lose relevance fast.
6. **Tariffs and sourcing.** ~$1B headwind being met with sourcing shifts and price rises into a weak consumer; the refund is a receivable, not cash in hand.
7. **Organisational churn.** Repeated senior-leadership reshuffles through 2026, including eliminating the CTO and chief commercial officer roles ([NIKE newsroom](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-inc-august-27-2026-senior-leadership-announcement)) — two years into a turnaround that is still being restructured.
8. **Time.** Even a working plan may suppress reported results for several more quarters, and we'd be paid only a 4.5% dividend to wait.
9. **The mirror-image risk:** if the turnaround works, our conservative band means we miss part of the recovery. We accept that. A missed buy costs nothing.

## 7. Sell triggers — the rules the manager will follow

These are written now, in calm conditions. We follow them; we do not renegotiate them later.

1. Gross margin (excluding one-time tariff recoveries) **below 40.0%** for two consecutive quarters. *(FY2026: 42.91%.)*
2. A **third consecutive fiscal year of revenue decline** — FY2027 below $46,398M.
3. Trailing-twelve-month **Greater China revenue below $5.0B** *(FY2026: $5,800M)*, or China declines continuing past the end of FY2027.
4. **Free cash flow below $1.5B** in a fiscal year, or below dividends paid for a **second** consecutive year *(FY2026: $2,184M FCF vs $2,407M dividends)*.
5. **Net debt / EBITDA above 1.5x** *(today: −0.24x, net cash)*, or total debt rising while FCF is still falling.
6. **A dividend cut.**
7. Full-year **EBIT margin below 7.0%** *(FY2026: 8.3%; FY2024: 11.1%)*.
8. NIKE Direct still declining into FY2028 **while** wholesale growth stalls below 2% — both legs of the plan failing at once.
9. **CEO Elliott Hill departs**, or a **third** major strategy reset is announced, before FY2028.
10. Any **accounting red flag**: restatement, material weakness in internal controls, disputed auditor change, or inventory growing much faster than revenue for two consecutive quarters.

## 8. What to watch next

- **2026-10-01 — Q1 FY2027 results** (after the close). Management guided to "flattish" H1 earnings and a slightly positive Q1 gross margin. The questions: does the eight-quarter China decline streak break? Does NIKE Direct stop falling? Does the tariff receivable turn into cash?
- **~December 2026 — Q2 FY2027.** Management said margin expansion should begin here. First real test of the margin claim.
- **January 2027 — the China marketplace reset** JPMorgan flagged. Confirm or refute at Q3 FY2027.
- **2026-09-21 — removal from the S&P 100.** Mechanical index selling; it may hand us a better price without changing the business.

## 9. Bottom line

NIKE is a genuinely great brand having a genuinely bad few years, and at a 12-year low it deserves a place on the bench. But "cheap versus its own history" is not the same as "cheap." On the numbers NIKE actually reported, the stock at **$36.34** is roughly **11% above** our base fair value of **$32.60**, and on clean (ex-tariff-benefit) earnings it is ~29% above. Free cash flow doesn't cover the dividend, revenue is still shrinking, and a dated, quantified China headwind lands in January 2027.

**Conviction 2/5. Watch, don't buy.** We would want the **low-to-mid $20s**, or hard evidence of revenue and margin inflection, before this earns capital. Re-check immediately after the 1 October print.
