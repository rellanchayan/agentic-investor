# NIKE, Inc. (NKE) — Research Thesis

**As of:** 2026-10-06 · **Fiscal period:** Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended 31 Aug 2026) plus FY2026 annual · **Thesis ID:** NKE-2027Q1
**Price used:** $34.25 (our own Alpaca feed) · **Conviction:** 1 / 5 (lowered from 2) · **Moat:** narrow
**Verdict: AVOID for now. Do not buy at $34.25.** Our fair-value band is **$21.19 / $26.68 / $33.35**. The price is above even the top of that band. Margin of safety is **-28%**.
**Refresh due:** 2027-01-04. Do it sooner if Q2 FY2027 results come out in late December 2026.

> **What changed since the 2026-09-18 thesis:** NIKE reported Q1 FY2027 on 2026-10-01. Revenue fell 4%. China fell 22%. Management guided full-year revenue **down high-single digits** and adjusted EPS to **$1.15–$1.35**, and announced a five-year restructuring called "Pace". The guidance means several of our sell triggers will probably be hit this year. We do not own the stock, so there is nothing to sell, but the case for buying is weaker than it was.

## 1. What the company does

NIKE sells athletic shoes, clothing and equipment under the NIKE, Jordan and Converse brands. Outside contractors make almost all of it. NIKE designs the product, pays athletes and teams to make people want it, and keeps the gap between what a shoe costs to make and what the swoosh lets it charge.

Q1 FY2027, the three months to 31 Aug 2026 ([Q1 FY27 8-K, Exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000184/q1fy27exhibit991er.htm)):

| Split | Q1 FY2027 revenue | Change vs a year ago |
|---|---|---|
| **Total NIKE, Inc.** | **$11,213M** | **-4%** (-5% currency-neutral) |
| North America | $5,127M | +2% |
| EMEA | $3,176M | -5% |
| Greater China | $1,180M | **-22%** (-26% c-n) |
| Asia Pacific & Latin America | $1,463M | -2% |
| Converse | $263M | **-28%** |
| Wholesale (Foot Locker, Dick's, JD…) | $6,804M | -1% |
| NIKE Direct (nike.com, apps, own stores) | $4,142M | -8% (digital **-13%**, stores -5%) |

**The strategy story so far.** Before CEO Elliott Hill came back in October 2024, NIKE pulled away from wholesale partners and pushed people to buy direct. That over-relied on a few lifestyle shoes and on heavy digital discounting. Hill reversed course. He is rebuilding wholesale, cutting back discounted digital sales, and putting "performance" sport product first (running, football, basketball). On 1 Oct 2026 he went further:
- a **"Pace" restructuring**: about **$1.0B** of charges through FY2031, mostly people costs, for about **$2.5B** of cumulative savings;
- a cut from **four regions to three** (Americas; Asia Pacific & Greater China; EMEA);
- a new campus in India ([Reuters via GBAF](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/nike-quarterly-sales-miss-estimates-china-weakness/)).

## 2. Moat (a lasting advantage that keeps rivals away): **narrow**

**What still works:**
- **Pricing power.** Gross margin is what's left of each sales dollar after the direct cost of the product. It *rose* 60 basis points to **42.8%** in Q1 even though sales fell. FY2026 was 42.9%. Customers still pay up for the brand.
- **Performance product is winning.** NIKE Performance grew high-single digits and Running grew double digits in Q1 ([call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/02/nike-nke-q1-2027-earnings-call-transcript/)).
- **Returns on capital.** FY2026 ROIC (profit per dollar invested in the business) was about **22%**. This is our own calculation and leaves out leases, so it flatters the real figure a little.

**What is being worn down:**
- **China:** down 22% in Q1, which by our count is the ninth quarter of decline in a row. The 10-Q says the problems are expected to **"extend beyond fiscal 2027."**
- **Lifestyle brand heat:** Jordan fell mid-teens, Sportswear fell low double digits, the Dunk shoe fell nearly 50%, and Converse fell 28%.

Pricing power is the one thing that stops this from being "no moat." If gross margin breaks below 40%, we downgrade.

## 4. Fair value: how we got $21.19 / $26.68 / $33.35

We computed it with `code/valuation.py`, giving equal weight to two methods:

1. **Earnings (P/E) method.** We used the midpoint of management's FY2027 *adjusted* EPS guidance, **$1.25**, times 15x / 20x / 25x. That gives $18.75 / $25.00 / $31.25.
   - We moved off last year's $2.10. That figure included a one-time $0.52 tariff refund, and management has now told us FY2027 will be much lower.
   - The multiples are *higher* than last time (13/17/21x) because FY2027 may be the low point. 15x means earnings stay here. 20x means a slow recovery. 25x means the Pace savings and the performance turnaround both work.
2. **Free-cash-flow yield method.** Trailing FCF of **$1.418/share** divided by 6% / 5% / 4% gives $23.63 / $28.36 / $35.45. We ask for high yields because FCF is falling and does not cover the dividend.

**Sensitivity checks (not used for the headline band):**
- *Bear case:* EPS at the low end of guidance ($1.15) at 13/17/21x, and FCF falling to $1.00/share (our assumption). Band: **$15.81 / $19.77 / $24.57**.
- *Recovery case:* EPS gets back to $2.10 within a few years (our assumption, not guided) at 16/19/22x. Band: **$33.60 / $39.90 / $46.20**.

**What this means.** Today's $34.25 price is about what the recovery case's *low* end implies. The market is already paying for much of a turnaround that management has not promised.

**Data caveats:**
- The P/E method rests on company *guidance*, not reported results.
- The ~$986M tariff refund booked in Q4 FY2026 has not been confirmed as collected. Its status is **unknown**. It would be worth about $0.66/share and is *not* in the band.

## 6. Bull case
- Gross margin is rising despite falling sales, so the brand still has pricing power.
- Running is growing double digits and North America is growing again. The performance-first plan is working where it started.
- Operating overhead fell 6%, and Pace targets $2.5B of savings.
- Net cash of about $475M and no buybacks to fund.
- The CFO said that "under all scenarios" the dividend will be maintained and grown over time. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.41.
- Expectations are already very low. LVMH's Alexandre Arnault joined the board on 2026-09-16 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000170/a091626pressrelease.htm)).

## 7. Risks (stated as hard as the bull case)
- **China is getting worse.** Q1 revenue fell 22% and China EBIT fell from $377M to $248M. The CFO said China "actually gets worse from a revenue perspective for the balance of this year."
- **Earnings are collapsing.** Guided adjusted EPS of $1.15–$1.35 compares with about $1.58 in clean FY2026 earnings. At $34.25 the stock is about 27x the guidance midpoint, which is a high price for a shrinking business.
- **Lifestyle franchises are fading:** Jordan, Sportswear, Dunk and Converse.
- **The dividend is not covered** by free cash flow and is being paid out of a shrinking net-cash cushion.
- **A long restructuring on top of a turnaround.** Pace runs to FY2031, and the regions are being reorganised at the same time.
- **Inventory rose 5%** while sales fell. Mix is the stated reason. One quarter of this is not a red flag; two would be.
- Rivals (On, HOKA, New Balance, adidas) are taking share. Tariffs remain a cost. The tariff refund status is unknown.

Any future BUY must first re-check triggers 2, 3, 4 and 7 in a refreshed thesis.

## 9. What would make us look again
- A price in the low $20s (inside our band, with real margin of safety), **and**
- evidence that China and Sportswear have stopped getting worse, **and**
- FCF covering the dividend again.
