## 1. What the company actually does, in plain English

Eli Lilly makes prescription medicines. Doctors prescribe them; the cash comes from insurers,
employers, pharmacy-benefit managers, Medicare/Medicaid, and — increasingly — straight from
patients through Lilly's own website, LillyDirect.

Today Lilly is, in practical terms, **an obesity and diabetes company with a good side business.**
One molecule, **tirzepatide**, is sold under two names:

- **Mounjaro** (type-2 diabetes) — **$9,943M** in Q2 2026, up 91% year over year
- **Zepbound** (obesity) — **$4,928M** in Q2 2026, up 46%

Together that is **$14,871M of the quarter's $22,974M of revenue — about 65% of the company**
([10-Q, three months ended 2026-06-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/59478/000005947826000081/lly-20260630.htm)).
For full-year 2025 the same two brands were **56% of revenue**
([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/59478/000005947826000013/lly-20251231.htm)).

In April 2026 the FDA approved **Foundayo (orforglipron)** — the first *pill* GLP-1 for weight
management, taken any time of day with no food or water restrictions. It did **$98M** in its first
partial quarter
([Lilly IR](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-approves-lillys-foundayotm-orforglipron-only-glp-1-pill)).
A pill matters because it can be prescribed in ordinary primary care and shipped anywhere — the
injectable business is limited by cold chain and by fill-finish capacity.

Everything else is real but secondary: **Verzenio** (breast cancer, $1,474M in Q2 2026),
**Jardiance** ($1,232M), **Trulicity** ($1,219M), **Taltz** ($856M), plus Ebglyss, Omvoh, Jaypirca,
Inluriyo and Kisunla (Alzheimer's).

## 2. Why we think the moat is wide

A **moat** is a lasting advantage that keeps competitors out. Lilly has four:

1. **Patents.** Tirzepatide's core composition-of-matter patent runs to roughly **January 2036**,
   with formulation and device patents claimed beyond that
   ([DrugPatentWatch](https://www.drugpatentwatch.com/p/alphasignals/tradename/MOUNJARO) — a
   secondary source, not a filing).
2. **Proven best-in-class efficacy.** In a head-to-head Phase 3 trial reported February 2026,
   Novo Nordisk's CagriSema delivered ~20% weight loss over 84 weeks versus **~24% for Zepbound**;
   CagriSema also lost to tirzepatide on blood-sugar control
   ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/novo-nordisk-cagrisema-zepbound-head-to-head-results/812810/)).
   You cannot buy that evidence base; you have to spend a decade generating it.
3. **Manufacturing you cannot rent.** Lilly spent **$9.9B of capex in the last twelve months**
   against only **$2.1B of depreciation**, and committed **another $4.5B** to Indiana plants and a
   first genetic-medicine facility alongside Q2 results
   ([Q2 2026 release](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-raises-full)).
   Peptide fill-finish capacity is the real bottleneck in this market.
4. **Distribution control.** LillyDirect (and the TrumpRx channel) lets Lilly sell to self-pay
   patients without a pharmacy-benefit middleman.

Two honest caveats on that table:
- **ROIC and net-debt/EBITDA are our own arithmetic**, built from line items in the filings, not

  makes the leverage ratio slightly *worse* than reality — conservative on purpose.
- **Free cash flow is much smaller than net income** ($18.2B vs $26.7B TTM) because Lilly is in the
  middle of a huge factory build. That is investment, not weakness — but it is real cash out the door.

## 4. Fair value: how we got the band

We used two methods and let `code/valuation.py` do the arithmetic.

**Method 1 — price-to-earnings on guided FY2026 earnings.**
Lilly guides FY2026 EPS to **$35.50–$36.50** (midpoint **$36.00**), after a **$3.03** hit from
acquired in-process-R&D charges. We applied multiples of **24× / 29× / 34×** → **$864 / $1,044 / $1,224**.
Why those: a wide-moat business compounding earnings above 30% a year with ~a decade of patent
protection deserves a clear premium to the market's ~22×, so 34× at the high end. But this is still
pharma — the tirzepatide cliff around 2036 sits inside a long-term owner's holding period, and US
and international prices are already falling — so we refuse to pay a perpetual growth multiple; 24×
at the low end.

**Method 2 — free-cash-flow yield.**
TTM FCF per diluted share is **$20.33**. At target yields of **3.5% / 2.8% / 2.2%** that gives
**$581 / $726 / $924**. This leg is deliberately harsh and acts as a **floor**: today's FCF is
suppressed by growth capex, so it understates what the business will throw off once the plants are built.

**Averaging the two: low $722 · base $885 · high $1,074.**

At **$1,185.96** the stock is **34% above our base fair value** and above the top of the band. On
guided earnings that is roughly **33× forward**. The trend is strong (price sits above both the
50-day $1,157.72 and 200-day $1,033.38 averages), but momentum is not a reason to overpay.

## 5. The risks — written as hard as the bull case

- **We would be paying up.** −34% margin of safety. Everything has to go right for a decade.
- **One molecule is the company.** Tirzepatide is ~65% of Q2 2026 revenue. A safety signal, a
  manufacturing failure or a lost patent case would hit the majority of Lilly at once.
- **Price is falling while volume booms.** Q2 2026 revenue grew 48% on **+60% volume and −13%
  realized price**; US prices fell ~9% and international realized prices fell **36%** (largely
  Mounjaro joining China's NRDL). Volume can offset price for a while — not forever.
- **Government is now setting the price.** Under the November 2025 agreement with the US
  administration, injectable tirzepatide is **$245/month for Medicare** with $50 copays, and about
  **$346/month average via TrumpRx**, versus a former list price above **$1,080**
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/06/trump-eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-deal-obesity-drug-prices.html)).
  Further most-favored-nation extension or eventual IRA negotiation would compress this more.
- **Generic filers have already knocked.** The FDA accepted **two Sandoz ANDAs** for generic
  tirzepatide (announced 2026-06-29) and China's **Hybio** announced accepted ANDAs plus a
  **Paragraph IV** challenge to Lilly's patents in July 2026
  ([Drug Topics](https://www.drugtopics.com/view/fda-accepts-sandoz-applications-for-generic-tirzepatide)).
  Lilly should win with patents to 2036 — but litigation is never a certainty.
- **Cash conversion is weak while the build-out runs**, and $3,486M of in-process-R&D purchases in
  H1 2026 alone shows how lumpy business-development spending is.
- **Litigation tail**: the 10-Q discloses consolidated Mounjaro/Zepbound product-liability cases in
  Pennsylvania federal court, Cipla's ANDA challenge to Verzenio, the Emgality/Teva dispute, 340B
  litigation and the insulin-pricing MDL.
- **Competition is well funded**: Novo Nordisk, Amgen, Roche/Zealand, AstraZeneca and several
  Chinese biotechs are all in incretins.

## 6. Sell triggers — the exact rules

These are written now, in calm conditions, so we do not improvise later.

1. Combined **Mounjaro + Zepbound revenue declines year over year for two consecutive quarters**.
2. **Gross margin falls below 75%** for two consecutive quarters (from 85.8%).
3. **Total revenue declines year over year for two consecutive quarters**, or full-year revenue shrinks.
4. **Net-debt-to-EBITDA rises above 2.5×** (from ~1.3×).
5. **Free cash flow negative for a full fiscal year**, or TTM FCF below 40% of TTM net income for four straight quarters.
6. **ROIC falls below 15%** on a trailing-twelve-month basis.
7. A court **invalidates or holds not-infringed a core tirzepatide patent**, or Lilly settles to allow US generic entry **before 2033**.
8. An **FDA safety action** on tirzepatide or orforglipron — boxed warning, materially narrowing label restriction, or withdrawal.
9. A **retatrutide or orforglipron pivotal failure / FDA rejection**.
10. **US net realized price for the incretin franchise falls more than 25%** year over year in any single year.
11. Management **withdraws or cuts full-year revenue guidance by more than 10%**.
12. A **serious accounting restatement**, disputed auditor change, or other governance red flag.

## 7. Catalysts we are watching

- **Retatrutide BLA planned Q1 2027** (obesity, obstructive sleep apnea, knee osteoarthritis pain)
  ([CNBC, 2026-07-23](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/23/eli-lilly-will-file-for-approval-of-retatrutide-obesity-drug-in-2027.html)).
- **Foundayo (orforglipron) ramp** through LillyDirect, retail and telehealth; a possible type-2 diabetes label.
- **Broader Medicare/Medicaid obesity coverage** under the November 2025 pricing agreement — volume in exchange for price.
- **New capacity coming online**, relieving supply constraints.
- **Resolution of the Sandoz / Hybio ANDA challenges** in Lilly's favour.
- **A price pullback toward $1,074, or better $885** — which is the only thing that turns this from a watch into a buy.

## 8. Bottom line

Lilly is one of the highest-quality businesses we have looked at: 84%+ gross margins, ~34% ROIC,
50%+ revenue growth, a wide moat built on patents, evidence and factories, and a pipeline behind the
current franchise. That is why conviction is not lower.

But conviction is not permission to overpay. At $1,185.96 we are 34% above our base fair value, and
the two biggest structural risks — a single molecule carrying 65% of revenue, and a government that
has already cut the price of that molecule by roughly two-thirds — are exactly the kind of risk that
punishes a full price. **Watch it. Set an alert near $1,074, and get serious near $885.**
