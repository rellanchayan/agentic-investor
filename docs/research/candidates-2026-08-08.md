clear holes stand out:

## Candidate 1 — SPGI: S&P Global Inc.

**Sector:** Financials (financial data and analytics)
**Market cap:** ~$121 billion | **Stock price:** ~$410 (as of 2026-08-07)
**Exchange:** NYSE | **Ticker:** SPGI

### Business in two sentences
S&P Global owns four interlocking franchises: credit ratings (the S&P brand sits alongside
Moody's as one of only two ratings anyone on earth truly trusts), the S&P Dow Jones Indices
business (it licenses the S&P 500 and thousands of other benchmarks to ETF managers and
derivative traders), Platts commodity price benchmarks, and Market Intelligence (financial
data terminals and research). Every time a company issues a bond, every time an ETF tracking
the S&P 500 takes in a dollar, and every time a commodity trader checks a price, SPGI collects
a royalty.

### Why the moat is durable
The ratings franchise is an NRSRO-designated oligopoly: regulators require NRSRO ratings for
capital adequacy calculations at banks and insurers worldwide, and only a handful of agencies
hold that designation. S&P and Moody's together rate the overwhelming majority of global
issuance — a position that has been unbroken for decades and cannot be replicated by a new
entrant. The index business is stickier still: the S&P 500 is not just a benchmark, it is
financial infrastructure. Trillions of dollars of ETFs, futures, and derivatives are legally
linked to it. Switching is not a business decision any fund manager can make unilaterally.
Asset-linked fees grow automatically as equity markets rise, creating a structural tailwind
unrelated to the number of transactions SPGI has to process.

### Recent revenue and earnings trajectory
Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-28): pro-forma revenue $3.68 billion, up 11% year-over-year; adjusted
EPS $4.83, up 23%; adjusted operating margin 54.3% (expanded 200 basis points). Ratings revenue
grew 17% (68.5% segment margin) on a 25% surge in transaction revenue, driven by $169 billion
in hyperscaler-related debt issuance in H1 2026. The Indices segment grew 20% at a 71.5%
operating margin, boosted by the S&P 500 ETF crossing $1 trillion in AUM for the first time
and total index-linked ETF AUM hitting $6.35 trillion. Full-year 2026 guidance: adjusted EPS
$17.50-17.75; second-half free cash flow (post Mobility spin) projected at $2.9-3.1 billion.
The company authorized $7 billion in share repurchases (more than 5% of market cap).

Source: S&P Global Q2 2026 earnings call highlights via BigGo Finance and GuruFocus (see links
below).

### Why it belongs on the watchlist
The stock is down roughly 28% from its 2025 high (~$529 in August 2025 to ~$410 today), partly
due to the Mobility Global spin-off and some multiple compression. That creates a potential
entry opportunity for the research analyst to study. The core franchises — ratings and indices
— are arguably more durable than almost anything else in our watchlist. Free cash flow is real
and growing. No technology disruption can replace NRSRO-certified ratings without regulatory
change, and no passive fund manager can simply switch away from the S&P 500. We have no
financial data or ratings name on the watchlist; this fills that gap cleanly.

## Candidate 2 — MCO: Moody's Corporation

**Sector:** Financials (financial data and analytics)
**Market cap:** ~$84 billion | **Stock price:** ~$478 (as of 2026-08-07)
**Exchange:** NYSE | **Ticker:** MCO

### Business in two sentences
Moody's earns money in two ways: its Moody's Investors Service (MIS) segment charges issuers
a fee every time they want a credit rating, and its Moody's Analytics (MA) segment sells
risk-assessment data, models, and software on subscription to banks, insurers, and asset
managers. MIS is a government-licensed oligopoly; MA is a sticky, high-retention SaaS
business built on top of MIS's century-old database of credit defaults.

### Why the moat is durable
The NRSRO designation is a legal prerequisite for ratings to count in regulatory capital
calculations globally — and that designation has been granted to only a handful of agencies
over four-plus decades. Moody's century-old proprietary default database, built from
continuous data since the 1900s, cannot be replicated at any price. This history of defaults
is the raw material for every model regulators, banks, and pension funds rely on. The
analytics segment compounds this moat by turning MIS data into software that becomes embedded
in client risk systems, yielding a 95% retention rate. Warren Buffett has owned Moody's
continuously since the Dun & Bradstreet spin-off in 2000 and has described it as one of his
favorite businesses — a useful sanity check.

### Recent revenue and earnings trajectory
Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-22): consolidated revenue $2.19 billion, up 15%; adjusted diluted
EPS $4.68, up 31%; adjusted operating margin 55.3% (expanded 440 basis points). MIS revenue
surged 25%, with transaction revenue up 34% — the market-making environment for corporate
debt was exceptionally active, with over $2 trillion rated for the second consecutive quarter.
MA segment organic revenue grew 8%, annualized recurring revenue reached $3.7 billion (up 9%),
and retention held at 95%. Q2 free cash flow: $688 million, up 47% year-over-year. Full-year
2026 guidance: adjusted EPS $16.50-17.00; free cash flow $2.7-2.9 billion.

Source: Moody's Q2 2026 earnings press release and BigGo Finance earnings call summary (see
links below).

### Why it belongs on the watchlist
Moody's is one of the few businesses in the world with 55%+ operating margins, a structurally
protected position, and real free cash flow ($2.7-2.9 billion guidance this year). The
business is simpler and more concentrated than SPGI — primarily ratings and risk analytics —
which makes it easier to model. MCO complements SPGI on the watchlist: together they
represent both halves of the ratings duopoly, both of which our research analyst should be
familiar with. Seventeen sell-side analysts rate it Buy; none rate it Sell. Note that the
ratings business is cyclical in a narrow sense — issuance volume falls in risk-off markets —
but the underlying franchise has never lost its dominant position in any credit cycle.

## Candidate 3 — TMO: Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.

**Sector:** Health care (life-sciences tools and instruments)
**Market cap:** ~$216 billion | **Stock price:** ~$578 (as of 2026-07-31)
**Exchange:** NYSE | **Ticker:** TMO

### Business in two sentences
Thermo Fisher sells the instruments, reagents, consumables, and contract services that
pharmaceutical and biotech companies, hospitals, and academic labs need to do their science —
everything from gene sequencing machines to mass spectrometers to single-use bioprocessing
bags. Once a lab has built its workflows around Thermo Fisher instruments and software, the
cost and disruption of switching to a competitor's platform is enormous, so customers rarely
leave.

### Why the moat is durable
Three reinforcing advantages: high switching costs (lab instruments are embedded in validated
workflows; a pharma company cannot change a critical instrument without re-running expensive
validation studies for regulators), proprietary technology (annual R&D near $1.5 billion,
with leading positions in high-resolution mass spectrometry and genomic sequencing), and
unmatched global distribution through the Fisher Scientific channel (the largest scientific
supply chain in the world, reaching virtually every lab on earth). These combine to produce
a business where customers are essentially locked in and where scale compounds over time —
the more instruments installed, the more consumables and service revenue flows in each year
without additional selling effort. GuruFocus rates the TMO moat score at 8 out of 10.

### Recent revenue and earnings trajectory
Q1 2026 (reported 2026-04-23): revenue $11.01 billion, up 6% year-over-year, adj EPS $5.44
(beat estimates of $5.25). Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-23): revenue $11.99 billion, up 10%,
another guidance raise. Full-year 2026 guidance (as of Q2): revenue $47.4-48.1 billion, adj
EPS $24.93-25.33, free cash flow $6.8-7.3 billion. Management has raised guidance twice this
year. The life-sciences tools sector has been recovering from a 2023-2024 inventory
destocking cycle in pharma, and TMO is one of the first to show clear re-acceleration.

Source: Thermo Fisher Q2 2026 earnings call transcript via Investing.com; Q1 earnings press
release via IR page (see links below).

### Why it belongs on the watchlist
Our healthcare exposure today is entirely in drug companies (JNJ, LLY, ABBV) and one managed
care insurer (UNH). We have nothing upstream in the tools and instruments segment, which tends
to be more durable than drug development because it supplies ALL drug developers regardless of
which drug wins. Thermo Fisher is the dominant company in that upstream role. At a $216 billion
market cap it is large and liquid. Free cash flow guidance of $6.8-7.3 billion is real and
growing. The pharma spending recovery is still in early innings after 2023-2024 destocking,
giving the research analyst a reason to study valuation now rather than after the recovery is
fully priced in.

## Names considered but not proposed this week

**META (Meta Platforms, NASDAQ)** — The network-effect moat across Facebook, Instagram, and
WhatsApp covering 3+ billion daily users is genuine. Q2 2026 revenue was $60.8 billion, up
28%. However, free cash flow collapsed 91% to $784 million in Q2 2026 as the company guided
capital expenditures to $130-145 billion for the full year. The underlying franchise is
excellent but the near-term FCF test fails under our current criteria. We will revisit when
the AI capex cycle shows evidence of FCF normalization.

**AMZN (Amazon, NASDAQ)** — AWS grew 37% to $42.2 billion quarterly revenue in Q2 2026, with
a $496 billion contracted backlog. AWS is the dominant cloud platform with genuine switching
costs. However, free cash flow turned negative (trailing 12-month outflow of $7.6 billion)
due to $220 billion in guided 2026 capex. Same issue as META — extraordinary business,
temporarily impaired FCF. Will revisit when FCF recovers.

**AMT (American Tower, NYSE)** — A well-structured business (150,000 cell towers globally,
long-term leases with mobile carriers) but revenue growth is modest (+5% in Q2 2026) and
the REIT structure requires distributing most earnings as dividends. Not a clear addition
over the three names above at this time.
