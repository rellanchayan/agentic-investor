## Candidate 1 — SPGI (S&P Global)

## Candidate 2 — MCO (Moody's Corporation)

**Sector gap filled:** Financials — credit ratings and analytics (paired duopoly with SPGI)

**Business (2 sentences):** Moody's operates two segments: Moody's Investors Service (credit ratings) and Moody's Analytics (risk data, software, and research for financial institutions). Together with SPGI's Ratings division, it forms a globally recognized duopoly where bond issuers must obtain ratings to access capital markets — the ratings are mandated by regulation in most jurisdictions.

**Moat type:** Regulatory oligopoly + switching costs. Bond issuers legally need ratings from a Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization (NRSRO); there are only a handful and SPGI/MCO dominate with ~80% combined share. Once financial institutions embed MCO's Analytics products into risk workflows, switching costs are extremely high.

**Quality check:**
- Revenue (TTM): $8.16 billion (+11.7% YoY). Source: [StockAnalysis MCO](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mco/)
- Net income (TTM): $2.80 billion (+31.1% YoY). EPS $15.74 (+34%). Source: [StockAnalysis MCO](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mco/)
- Adjusted operating margin Q2 2026: 55.3% (+440 bps YoY). Source: [Moody's Q2 2026 Form 8-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001059556/000162828026049104/a2q26earningsrelease.htm)
- ROE (5-year average): 57.2%. Source: [StockStory MCO Research](https://stockstory.org/us/stocks/nyse/mco)
- Average daily volume: ~773K shares (~$367M/day). Highly liquid.

**Current price & valuation:** $474.95 (Sep 11, 2026 close). P/E ~30.2x trailing. Analyst consensus target: $561.90 (+18% implied upside). Source: [StockAnalysis MCO](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mco/). A 30x PE for a business with 55% operating margins, 11.7% revenue growth, and a near-monopoly regulatory position is not a bargain, but it is plausibly fair. The 18% gap to the analyst consensus target suggests the market may be somewhat cautious vs the Street.

**Key risks:** Cyclical — debt issuance slows in recessions, hurting Ratings revenue sharply. Regulatory risk (governments could alter how ratings work). Competition in Analytics from specialized fintech vendors.

**Verdict:** One of the best moats in finance. A full thesis is warranted. The cyclical nature means the best entry prices tend to come during credit-market stress events, so the thesis should be built now to be ready.

## Candidate 3 — MSCI (MSCI Inc.)

**Sector gap filled:** Financials — index and analytics (no comparable on watchlist)

**Business (2 sentences):** MSCI creates and licenses equity, fixed income, and real-estate indices (e.g., MSCI World, MSCI Emerging Markets) that serve as benchmarks for over $16.5 trillion in global AUM. It also sells analytics, ESG data, and real-estate data tools — all on subscription contracts with 93–95% annual retention rates.

**Moat type:** Network effects + switching costs + data asset. Once an ETF, pension fund, or asset manager benchmarks to an MSCI index, switching is contractually and operationally nearly impossible. MSCI earns asset-based fees that grow automatically as AUM grows, creating a toll-road on rising global markets.

## Candidate 4 — SYK (Stryker Corporation)

**Business (2 sentences):** Stryker makes orthopedic implants (hips, knees, spinal), surgical equipment, neurotechnology, and the Mako robotic surgical system. **Moat type:** Switching costs + installed base + product complexity. The Mako robotic platform creates a razor/blade model: once a hospital installs it, replacement implants must also be Stryker. Surgeons develop trained proficiency on specific systems and resist changes. Stryker's sales force provides deep clinical support that builds multi-year hospital relationships.

## Candidate 6 — ITW (Illinois Tool Works)

Its 80/20 management philosophy — focusing each business unit on the top 20% of products/customers that generate 80% of profits — has driven consistently high and improving margins for over a decade.

**Moat type:** Switching costs + niche scale. ITW's products are often custom-engineered components embedded in customers' production lines — switching suppliers means re-qualification and downtime risk, so customers rarely switch. The 80/20 model lets ITW charge premium prices in niches where it has dominant share.

## Candidate 7 — ODFL (Old Dominion Freight Line)

**Sector gap filled:** Industrials — LTL (less-than-truckload) freight, a niche with very high barriers to entry

**Business (2 sentences):** Old Dominion operates the second-largest less-than-truckload freight network in the United States, with ~260 service centers, and is widely considered the industry's quality benchmark. Unlike full-truckload carriers, LTL requires a dense hub-and-spoke network that took ODFL decades to build and cannot be replicated cheaply or quickly by a new entrant.

**Moat type:** Scale + network density + brand. An LTL carrier must have enough density on each lane to fill trailers profitably; ODFL's decades-long network investment gives it structural cost and coverage advantages over smaller competitors. Its industry-leading service quality (damage rates, on-time delivery) allows premium pricing.

**Quality check:**
- Current price: ~$200 (per multiple sources as of early Sep 2026; range $185–$206 across sources). 52-week range: $126.01–$252.03. Source: [StockAnalysis ODFL](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/odfl/statistics/)
- ROIC: 27.2% (Jun 2026). Source: [GuruFocus ODFL ROIC](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/roic/ODFL)
- Revenue (TTM): ~$5.6 billion. Net income TTM: ~$1.09 billion (~19.5% net margin). Source: [StockAnalysis ODFL](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/odfl/statistics/)
- FCF per share (Q2 2026): $2.62/quarter. Source: [MacroTrends ODFL FCF](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/ODFL/old-dominion-freight-line/free-cash-flow-per-share)
- P/E: ~36x trailing (at ~$200). Market cap ~$38B. Source: [Old Dominion Freight Line Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/stocks/ODFL/)
- NASDAQ-listed, large-cap. Liquid.

**Current price & valuation:** At ~$200 vs. 52-week high of $252, the stock is ~21% below its peak. A 27% ROIC business at 36x trailing earnings is still a premium — ODFL almost never trades cheaply. But this is the type of business worth owning when the price is right, and the pullback from $252 is meaningful. The full thesis must establish a fair-value range and identify what entry price provides a margin of safety.

**Key risks:** LTL volumes are highly cyclical; a freight recession (as seen in 2022-2023) can sharply reduce revenue and earnings. Amazon Freight and digital brokers could erode pricing power over time. Labor (Teamsters) is a recurring risk in the trucking sector, though ODFL is non-union.

**Verdict:** The best-run LTL carrier. ROIC of 27% is exceptional for an asset-heavy business. Good bench addition for when freight recessions create entry opportunities.
