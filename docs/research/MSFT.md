## What changed since the last thesis (June 2026)

The last thesis was written on FY2025 numbers. Microsoft has since reported a full fiscal
year 2026 (ended 30 June 2026), and the picture has changed in two big, opposite ways:

| | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $281.724B | **$331.839B** | +17.8% |
| Operating income | $128.528B | **$155.237B** | +20.8% |
| Net income (GAAP) | $101.832B | **$133.749B** | +31.3% |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $13.64 | **$17.95** | +31.6% |
| Capex (property & equipment) | $64.551B | **$115.948B** | **+79.6%** |
| Free cash flow | $71.611B | **$66.987B** | **−6.5%** |
| Gross margin | 68.8% | 67.9% | −0.9 pt |
| ROIC (our calc) | ~31% | **~26%** | falling |

**The good news:** growth got *faster*, not slower, at a $330B revenue base. Azure passed
$100 billion of annual revenue for the first time and still grew 41%.

**The bad news:** profits are growing but cash is not. Microsoft spent $115.9B on data
centres and chips in one year — more than it spent in FY2023, FY2024 combined — and free
cash flow actually went *down*. Price went up 25% since our last look (from $393.54 to
$493.13) while the cash the business produces went down.

All figures above from the
[FY2026 Form 10-K via SEC XBRL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json)
and the [FY26 Q4 earnings release (8-K Ex-99.1)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789019/000119312526323632/msft-ex99_1.htm).

## What the business does (plain English)

Microsoft sells software and rented computing power, mostly on subscription, to businesses,
governments and consumers. In FY2026 it reported three segments
([source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)):

- **Productivity & Business Processes — $139.996B.** Microsoft 365 (Office), Teams, LinkedIn,
  Dynamics. This is the "everyone at work uses it" business.
- **Intelligent Cloud — $137.791B.** Azure (renting servers and AI chips over the internet),
  server software, GitHub. This is the growth engine: Azure grew **41% for the year and 43%
  in Q4**, passing **$100B**.
- **More Personal Computing — $54.052B.** Windows, Xbox, Surface, search advertising. This
  part is **shrinking** (−4% in Q4).

Microsoft Cloud overall passed **$214B, up 27%**.

**A structural note:** from FY2027 Microsoft reports in only **two** segments — "Agents and
Infra" (FY2026 restated: $268.1B revenue, $136.4B operating income) and "Devices and
Consumer" ($63.7B / $18.9B)
([8-K, September 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789019/000119312526380280/d291965dex991.htm)).
We will see *less* detail going forward, which makes tracking Azure specifically harder.

### Is the demand real?
Two figures say yes, at least for the next couple of years:
- **Remaining performance obligation (contracted revenue not yet booked): $684B** at
  2026-06-30, versus $375B a year earlier ([10-K XBRL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json)).
  That is signed business waiting to be delivered.
- **Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats**, and as of Q3 FY2026 the AI business
  passed a **$37B annual run rate, up 123%**
  ([Q3 FY26 release](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q3/press-release-webcast)).

## Why it has a moat (still wide)

- **Switching costs.** Companies run email, documents, identity (Entra ID), databases and
  finance on Microsoft. Moving off is a multi-year, expensive project. Almost nobody does it.
- **Network effects.** Teams, LinkedIn and GitHub get more valuable as more people use them.
- **Scale in cloud.** At $214B of cloud revenue, Microsoft spreads enormous fixed costs over
  a base that few rivals can match.
- **Distribution for AI.** Microsoft does not have to *find* customers for AI — it already
  bills them every month. That is why Copilot reached 30 million paid seats so quickly.

**Rating: wide.** Nothing in FY2026 weakened the moat. What weakened is the *capital
efficiency* of the business, which is a different problem.

| Diluted shares | 7,453M | [10-K XBRL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json) |

**How we computed ROIC:** operating income $155.237B × (1 − 19.4% effective tax rate, itself
$32.185B tax / $165.934B pre-tax income) = NOPAT $125.1B. Invested capital = equity $442.387B
+ debt $40.294B + finance leases $66.594B − cash and short-term investments $76.843B =
$472.4B. $125.1B / $472.4B = **26.5%**. On the identical basis, FY2025 was **31.3%**.
Still excellent — but heading the wrong way.

**How we computed net debt:** debt $40.294B + finance lease liabilities $66.594B − cash
$20.935B − short-term investments $55.908B = **$30.0B net debt**. EBITDA ≈ operating income
$155.237B + depreciation $34.3B + intangible amortisation $4.7B + finance-lease amortisation
$5.403B ≈ **$199.6B**. So 0.15x — trivially small either way. We flag finance leases
explicitly because they grew from $27.1B (FY24) → $46.2B (FY25) → **$66.6B (FY26)**, and
the popular "Microsoft has net cash" line ignores them.

## Fair value: $336 (low) / $392 (base) / $456 (high)

Arithmetic done by `code/valuation.py`, not by hand. Two methods, averaged:

**1) Price-to-earnings on non-GAAP EPS $17.28, at 24x / 28x / 32x → $414.72 / $483.84 / $552.96.**
We use non-GAAP EPS rather than the GAAP $17.95 because GAAP includes a **$4.963B net gain
from Microsoft's OpenAI investments** (plus a $3.2B Anthropic gain in Q4) — real, but not
operating profit and not repeatable. 28x is the centre: a business growing revenue ~18% with
a 47% operating margin and a fortress balance sheet deserves a premium multiple, but the
rising capital intensity argues against the 32-35x it has sometimes carried.

**2) Free-cash-flow yield on $8.99 per share, at 3.5% / 3.0% / 2.5% → $256.86 / $299.67 / $359.60.**
($66.987B FCF ÷ 7,453M shares. Cross-checked at $9.02/share by
[StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/MSFT/statistics/).)

**Blended band: low $335.79, base $391.75, high $456.28. At $493.13 the margin of safety is
−25.9% and the code's verdict is "expensive (above the high end of fair value)."**

### Being honest about the disagreement
These two methods are **$184 apart at the base**, and that gap *is* the investment question.

- If you believe the $115.9B of capex is *growth* spending that will convert into Azure and
  Copilot revenue, then free cash flow today is artificially depressed, the P/E method is
  right, and fair value is nearer **$484** — i.e. the stock is roughly fairly priced.
- If you believe a meaningful chunk of that capex will not earn its cost, then cash flow is
  telling the truth, and fair value is nearer **$300**.

We cannot resolve this from the filings — Microsoft does not split maintenance capex from
growth capex, and we refuse to guess. So we keep both methods and accept a wide, humble band.
**Even under the generous P/E-only reading ($553 high end), $493 leaves no cushion.** The
answer at today's price is the same either way: wait.

## The risks — stated as hard as the bull case

1. **We would be paying ~26% above our base fair value.** That is the whole objection.
2. **The capex cycle is enormous and its return is unproven.** Property-and-equipment
   additions went $44.5B → $64.6B → **$115.9B** over three years, and FY27 Q1 *alone* is
   guided to **"over $50 billion"**
   ([8-K guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789019/000119312526380280/d291965dex991.htm)).
   Free cash flow already fell 6.5% while net income rose 31%. Management's public guidance
   is only that Microsoft will remain free-cash-flow *positive* in FY2027 — a very low bar
   for a company that produced $71.6B of it two years ago.
3. **Depreciation is a bill that arrives later.** Depreciation went from $22.0B to $34.3B in
   one year and will keep climbing as those data centres come online. If AI revenue does not
   scale to match, *reported earnings* get hit in FY2028-29, not just cash flow. Microsoft has
   also adjusted asset useful lives, which pushes costs further out — that deserves scrutiny,
   not applause.
4. **Returns are eroding.** ROIC 31% → 26%; gross margin 68.8% → 67.9%. Microsoft is
   gradually becoming a capital-heavy infrastructure business rather than a capital-light
   software business. Those deserve lower multiples.
5. **Earnings quality is noisier.** GAAP EPS now swings on mark-to-market gains and losses on
   OpenAI and Anthropic stakes (−$3.1B in Q1 FY26, +$7.6B in Q2 FY26). Headline EPS growth of
   31% overstates the underlying business.
6. **Everything depends on cloud now.** More Personal Computing shrank 4% in Q4. There is no
   second engine.
7. **Less disclosure ahead.** The two-segment FY2027 structure will make it harder to see
   Azure's health on its own.
8. **The usual:** antitrust scrutiny of cloud bundling and the OpenAI relationship; an AI
   demand air-pocket; price competition from AWS and Google Cloud; enterprise IT slowdown.

## Sell triggers — the exact rules

If we ever own MSFT, we sell (or at least reduce) when any of these is true:

1. Azure (or the successor "Agents and Infra" segment) revenue growth falls **below 15% YoY
   for two consecutive quarters**.
2. Free cash flow turns **negative for a full fiscal year**, or falls **below $40B** in a
   fiscal year, without a credible, stated capex-normalisation plan.
3. Total revenue **declines year-over-year for two consecutive quarters**.
4. Company-wide **gross margin falls below 60%** for two consecutive quarters.
5. **Operating margin falls below 35%** for two consecutive quarters (AI depreciation
   overwhelming the P&L).
6. **Net-debt-to-EBITDA, including finance leases, rises above 1.5x.**
7. **ROIC** (computed the same way as above) **falls below 15% for two consecutive fiscal years**.
8. A **large impairment or write-down of AI/data-centre assets**, or a useful-life change used
   to mask deteriorating economics.
9. A serious **accounting restatement** or material governance red flag.

## What would make us buy

- A pullback toward the **$392 base** or the **$336 low end** of the band — especially a
  drawdown driven by AI-capex fear rather than by the operating business breaking. That is
  the setup we are waiting for.
- **FY2027 Q1 earnings, expected late October 2026** — the first look at whether $50B+ of
  quarterly capex is being matched by revenue, and the first report under the new segments.
  Guidance is revenue of **$89.85-$90.95B** (~+16% on FY26 Q1's $77.673B) with operating
  margin "roughly flat year over year."
- Any explicit disclosure of **return on AI investment**, or evidence of capex normalising,
  which would let the cash-flow view converge upward toward the earnings view.

**Conviction 4/5** — this is a business we would happily own for a decade. The 4 rather than 5
reflects the rising capital intensity, the falling ROIC, and the fact that the cash-flow and
earnings pictures now tell different stories. **Conviction is about the business; the price is
a separate question, and today the price says no.**
