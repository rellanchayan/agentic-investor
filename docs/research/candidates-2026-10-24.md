## Proposed candidate 1: SPGI — S&P Global Inc.

**What it does.** S&P Global provides credit ratings (S&P Ratings), financial data and analytics (Market Intelligence), commodity and energy data (Platts), and — most famously — it owns and licenses the S&P 500 index franchise.

**Moat.** S&P and Moody's together hold roughly 80% of the global credit-ratings market, a near-duopoly embedded in bond covenants, regulations, and investor mandates worldwide. Credit ratings are almost impossible to displace: issuers need them to access debt markets, buyers need them for compliance, and regulators rely on them. On top of that, the S&P 500 index name is used in trillions of dollars of passive funds, ETFs, and derivatives — generating licensing fees that are essentially permanent. The data and analytics segment (formerly IHS Markit, merged 2022) has high renewal rates because financial professionals build workflows around these tools.

**Financials (approximate, from aggregator data — analyst must verify from filings).**
- Trailing-twelve-month revenue: ~$16.1B (FY2025: ~$15.3B, ~8% growth)
- TTM free cash flow: ~$5.5B
- FCF margin: ~35% — exceptional for a business of this size
- Operating margin: ~44%
- Market cap: ~$132B (based on ~293M shares at current price)

**Price data (from Alpaca paper feed, Oct 23 close).**

- Current price is roughly in the middle of the 52-week range — not obviously cheap or expensive until a full valuation is done.

**Why it belongs on the watchlist.** We have payment networks (V, MA), a large bank (JPM), and an insurer (PGR), but nothing that captures the financial data and index infrastructure layer. SPGI earns extraordinary returns on invested capital from a position that regulators and markets have effectively cemented in place over decades. The research analyst should build a DCF and stress-test the ratings business against the next credit cycle.

## Proposed candidate 2: CME — CME Group Inc.

**What it does.** CME Group operates the world's largest futures and options exchange, covering interest rates, equity indices (S&P 500 futures, NASDAQ futures), energy, metals, agricultural commodities, and foreign exchange. Its venues include the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, CBOT, NYMEX, and COMEX.

**Moat.** Exchange businesses have some of the deepest network effects in finance: traders, hedgers, and speculators all need to be in the same place for liquidity to exist, so a dominant venue becomes self-reinforcing. CME's interest rate futures (Treasury bonds, Eurodollars, SOFR) are literally the global benchmark for hedging and pricing fixed-income risk — replacing them is not a decision any single institution can make unilaterally. Switching costs are structural: clearing relationships, systems integration, and margin netting all lock participants in. CME also has almost no significant U.S. competitor in its core products.

**Financials (approximate, from aggregator data — analyst must verify from filings).**
- FY2025 revenue: ~$6.5B
- FY2025 free cash flow: ~$4.2B
- FCF margin: ~64% — among the highest of any major business in the index
- Market cap: ~$110B
- Note: next earnings report expected October 28, 2026 — analyst should factor in fresh results.

**Price data (from Alpaca paper feed, Oct 23 close).**
- Price: $305.73
- 52-week range: $218.56 – $326.41
- Currently near the upper end of its 52-week range (up ~40% from the 52-week low), which may compress near-term margin of safety.
- Avg 20-day dollar volume: ~$29.6M — liquid.

**Why it belongs on the watchlist.** CME is the kind of toll-road business that is hard to find: capital-light, regulated but not rate-regulated, with pricing power tied to trading volume that grows with financial market activity over time. It is entirely absent from our watchlist today. The research analyst needs to build a thesis on normalized volume assumptions and the interest rate cycle, since CME's volumes tend to be higher in volatile rate environments.

## Proposed candidate 3: TMO — Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.

**What it does.** Thermo Fisher makes and sells the equipment, consumables, and services that scientists use every day: analytical instruments, reagents, laboratory plasticware, cold-chain logistics, and contract manufacturing for biopharma. It serves pharmaceutical companies, hospitals, academic research labs, government agencies, and industrial customers worldwide.

**Moat.** The moat here is deep switching-cost stickiness at the workflow level. Once a lab validates a protocol using a specific instrument and reagents from Thermo Fisher, revalidating with a competitor's products takes months of scientist time and risks regulatory scrutiny — so customers almost never switch. The consumable / reagent stream that flows from an installed instrument base creates high-margin recurring revenue year after year. Scale matters too: with ~$45B in revenue, Thermo Fisher can negotiate supplier pricing and fund R&D that smaller peers cannot match.

**Financials (approximate, from aggregator data — analyst must verify from filings).**
- Trailing revenue: ~$44-45B (FY2026 guidance ~$47-48B)
- TTM free cash flow: ~$6.1-6.75B
- FCF margin: ~14-15% — solid for a business at this scale and in this industry
- Market cap: ~$218B
- Noted risk: the company carries elevated financial leverage; the analyst should check net debt and coverage ratios carefully.
- Note: next earnings report expected around October 28, 2026.

**Price data (from Alpaca paper feed, Oct 23 close).**
- Price: $673.84
- 52-week range: $438.15 – $696.50
- Currently near the top of the 52-week range — the stock has recovered strongly from a May 2026 low around $438. This does not preclude watchlist addition, but the analyst should assess whether current valuation leaves an adequate margin of safety.
- Avg 20-day dollar volume: ~$104.4M — highly liquid.

**Why it belongs on the watchlist.** Our health care coverage spans insurance (UNH), pharma (LLY, ABBV, JNJ), and surgical devices (SYK), but we have nothing in life sciences tools and services — the picks-and-shovels layer that supplies every pharma company, academic lab, and biotech on the planet. TMO is the dominant player in that layer, with decades of consistent compounding. The research analyst should model normalized volumes (the pandemic-era diagnostics windfall has wound down), assess leverage, and determine whether the current valuation supports a margin of safety.
