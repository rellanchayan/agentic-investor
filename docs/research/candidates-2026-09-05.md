## Candidate 1 — PLD (Prologis, Inc.)

**Sector gap filled:** Real estate (REIT) — currently zero names in this sector.

### Business in two sentences
Prologis is the world's largest industrial real estate investment trust, owning and operating
approximately 1.3 billion square feet of logistics warehouses across 20 countries on four
continents. Its customers are the e-commerce, retail, and manufacturing companies that need
well-located distribution space near population centers — space that is expensive and slow to
replicate.

### Moat
Location is the moat. A modern logistics warehouse 30 minutes from a major city port or
population center cannot simply be built elsewhere — land zoning, permitting timelines, and
infrastructure proximity make the incumbents' properties effectively irreplaceable. Prologis
compounds this with scale: it is 10x larger than its nearest REIT competitor, giving it
procurement advantages, data on market rents, and the ability to offer multinational tenants a
single global relationship. As of mid-2026, embedded rent spreads on rolling leases were
approximately 17% (meaning existing leases are still roughly 17% below current market rents,
which will convert to higher revenue as leases renew). The company has also begun leasing
portions of its portfolio to data center operators tied to AI infrastructure — roughly 10% of
new contracts in H1 2026 — which adds an optionality layer.

### Key financials (approximate, sourced from public releases)
- **2025 full-year revenue:** $9.19 billion (up ~7.5% year-over-year). Source: StockAnalysis.
- **Core FFO (H1 2026):** ~$3.0 billion, annualizing to ~$6 billion. (REITs are valued on
  Funds From Operations, not GAAP net income, because GAAP depreciation is non-cash for
  real estate.) Source: Prologis Q2 2026 earnings release.
- **Market cap:** approximately $132.9 billion as of July 2026. Source: StockAnalysis.
- **Liquidity:** $7.6 billion available as of June 30, 2026. Average debt interest rate of
  3.3% with nearly 8-year average maturity — a healthy position for a large REIT.
  Source: Prologis Q2 2026 release.
- **Approximate price (late July 2026):** ~$150. Some analysts estimate the stock is
  approximately 4% undervalued at this price relative to intrinsic NAV, with an average
  analyst price target of ~$153. Source: Yahoo Finance / CNN Markets.
- **Trailing P/FFO (approximate):** Given Core FFO annualizing ~$6B and a market cap of
  ~$133B, the implied P/FFO multiple is roughly 22x, which is within normal range for a
  premier industrial REIT. The reported trailing GAAP P/E (35x) is less meaningful for
  REIT valuation.

### Why it might be a quality business
E-commerce is structurally rewriting how goods move, requiring more, not less, warehouse
space near consumers. Supply of prime urban logistics space is constrained by land and
zoning. Prologis collects rents on long-term leases with built-in escalators, has 17% of
embedded rent upside as older leases roll, and is diversified across geographies and
tenants. The balance sheet is strong, and the 3.3% average debt cost — locked in over
nearly 8 years — provides protection if rates stay elevated.

### Key risks
- Rising interest rates increase the cost of new debt and may compress REIT valuations
  generally (cap rate expansion).
- A global economic slowdown that reduces goods trade would reduce logistics demand.
- The stock trades at a premium to book value; if the real estate cycle turns, NAV
  compression is possible.
- Data center expansion is new and unproven at scale for PLD.

## Candidate 2 — SPGI (S&P Global, Inc.)

**Sector gap filled:** Financial data infrastructure — the watchlist has payment networks
(V, MA) and a bank (JPM) but no financial-data or ratings infrastructure business.

### Business in two sentences
S&P Global provides credit ratings, financial market data, benchmarks, and analytics to
banks, asset managers, corporations, and governments around the world. Its ratings business
and its index business (the S&P 500 family) are embedded so deeply into global capital
markets that they function as regulatory and contractual requirements, not discretionary
purchases.

### Moat
Wide moat from two reinforcing sources. First, regulatory oligopoly: the U.S. SEC designates
only ten Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organizations (NRSROs), and S&P and
Moody's together command roughly 80% of global rated-debt issuance. Issuers must pay for a
rating from one of these two to access the bond market at competitive rates — demand is
effectively mandated. Second, index network effects: more than $20 trillion in global assets
under management is benchmarked to S&P indices. A fund manager benchmarked to the S&P 500
has no practical alternative — the index is the market standard. These advantages do not
erode easily; they tend to compound as capital markets grow.

### Key financials (approximate, sourced from public filings and releases)
- **Q1 2026 revenue:** $4.2 billion, +10.4% year-over-year. Source: Yahoo Finance / Q1
  earnings release.
- **Q1 2026 adjusted EPS:** $4.97, beating consensus by 3.1%, up 13.7% year-over-year.
  Source: Yahoo Finance earnings recap.
- **Full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance:** $19.40–$19.65 (midpoint ~$19.52). Source:
  company guidance.
- **Free cash flow:** 2025 annual FCF was $5.456 billion. Q1 2026 FCF was $919 million.
  FCF per share as of March 2026: $18.34. Source: MacroTrends, GuruFocus.
- **Capital returns:** Returned $1.2 billion to shareholders in Q1 2026 alone, including
  $1.0 billion in buybacks. Source: Q1 2026 earnings release.
- **Approximate price (Sep 4, 2026):** ~$451. Source: GuruFocus / FullRatio.
- **Forward P/E:** approximately 22.6x (GuruFocus, as of Aug 11, 2026), which is roughly
  24% below the 10-year historical average P/E of ~32x. GuruFocus GF Value estimate:
  ~$545, suggesting modest undervaluation at the current price.
- **Operating leverage:** Revenue grew 10% in Q1 2026 while operating profit grew 27% —
  showing the high-incremental-margin character of this business. Source: earnings recap.
- **Long-term debt:** $10.6 billion as of Q1 2026, manageable against $5+ billion annual FCF.

### Why it might be a quality business
Capital markets grow over time as the global economy grows; S&P sits in the middle of that
flow, earning a toll every time debt is issued or an index-tracking fund trades. The business
has near-zero incremental cost of adding another rating or another data query — margins expand
as volume grows. At a forward P/E of ~23x versus a historical average of ~32x, the market
appears to be pricing SPGI at a meaningful discount to its own history, which could represent
an entry opportunity for the analyst to explore.

### Key risks
- A sustained slowdown in debt issuance (higher-for-longer rates dampen refinancing activity)
  directly reduces ratings revenue; issuance is lumpy and cyclical.
- Regulatory scrutiny of the rating-agency oligopoly is a permanent risk; any structural
  change to NRSRO rules could reduce pricing power.
- The Indices segment's revenue depends on AUM levels; a bear market reduces management fees
  and index licensing revenue.
- The IHS Markit integration (completed 2022) was large; integration risks and debt from
  that deal ($10.6B remaining) are ongoing considerations.

## Candidate 3 — WM (Waste Management, Inc.)

**Sector gap filled:** Essential services / industrials with pricing power. CAT, HON, and
UNP are already on the watchlist; WM adds a fundamentally different industrial sub-type —
a locally defensive, essential-service monopoly.

### Business in two sentences
Waste Management is the largest solid-waste collection and disposal company in North America,
operating a network of collection routes, transfer stations, landfills, and recycling
facilities that is nearly impossible to replicate at meaningful scale. It collects, processes,
and disposes of garbage and recyclables for millions of residential, commercial, and
industrial customers under long-term contracts.

### Moat
The moat is a set of overlapping local monopolies. A landfill permitted and operating in a
metropolitan area is irreplaceable: the permitting process takes years, neighbors oppose new
facilities, and regulators rarely grant new landfill capacity near population centers. A
company that already owns the local landfill, the collection routes with decades of customer
relationships, and the transfer stations between them effectively controls the economics of
that market — a new entrant would have to replicate all three to compete. This structure
generates predictable, recurring revenue with built-in contract escalators tied to inflation
or CPI. Renewable energy from landfill gas provides an additional growing revenue stream.

### Key financials (approximate, sourced from public releases)
- **Q2 2026 revenue:** $6.68 billion, +4.0% year-over-year. Source: Yahoo Finance / WM
  earnings release.
- **Full-year 2026 revenue guidance:** $26.275–$26.475 billion (trimmed slightly due to
  softer volumes). Source: WM Q2 2026 earnings release.
- **Q2 2026 FCF:** $1.10 billion, up 35% year-over-year. Source: Yahoo Finance / WM release.
- **Full-year 2026 FCF guidance:** $3.75–$3.85 billion. Source: WM guidance.
- **Adjusted EBITDA margin (Q2 2026):** 30.9%, expanding 40 bps year-over-year. Source: WM
  Q2 2026 highlights.
- **Net income (Q2 2026):** $785 million ($1.95/diluted share), up from $726M a year earlier.
  Source: WM earnings release.
- **Approximate price (Sep 4, 2026):** ~$218–$220. Source: StockAnalysis / Robinhood.
- **Trailing P/E:** ~31x; forward P/E: ~25.8x. Both modestly above the 10-year historical
  average of ~28.9x. Source: FullRatio.
- **Annual dividend:** $3.78/share (~1.75% yield). Consistent dividend payer with a long
  history of increases.
- **Average analyst price target:** ~$256, implying ~18% upside from current price. Source:
  StockAnalysis consensus.

### Why it might be a quality business
Trash volumes correlate with GDP but are far less cyclical than, say, capital goods orders.
WM's landfill network took decades to build, has essentially no new direct competition in its
core markets, and generates large, predictable free cash flows that it returns via dividends
and buybacks. At ~25.8x forward earnings with FCF guidance of $3.75–3.85B, the valuation is
not cheap, but it is not extreme either — the analyst may find an acceptable entry after
market volatility. The company is also developing renewable-energy infrastructure (landfill
gas-to-energy) that could generate incremental, higher-margin revenue over the coming decade.

### Key risks
- Volume softness: Q2 2026 showed weaker commercial volumes and some national account losses,
  suggesting the moat does not fully prevent customer attrition. The company trimmed revenue
  guidance.
- Regulation: Environmental regulations can increase operating costs (landfill liner
  requirements, emissions controls); landfill capacity could face political or regulatory
  limits.
- Automation capital spend: WM is investing heavily in automation of recycling facilities,
  which is depressing near-term FCF vs. the headline. Capital intensity could remain elevated.
- At ~31x trailing earnings, a significant economic downturn could compress both multiples
  and volumes simultaneously.
