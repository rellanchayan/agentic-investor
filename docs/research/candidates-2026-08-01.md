### NEE — NextEra Energy, Inc.

**Sector gap filled:** Utilities (previously zero coverage)

**Business in two sentences:**
NextEra Energy is the largest electric utility in the United States. It operates
Florida Power & Light (FPL), a regulated electric utility serving roughly 12 million
people in Florida, alongside NextEra Energy Resources (NEER), the world's largest
generator of wind and solar power.

**Moat:**
Two overlapping moats. FPL is a state-regulated monopoly with guaranteed returns
on its rate base — competitors cannot legally build a competing grid in its service
territory. NEER has a scale advantage in renewable-energy development: it is the
biggest buyer of wind turbines and solar panels in North America, giving it
negotiating power on equipment cost that smaller developers cannot match. The AI
data-centre buildout is driving a step-change in US electricity demand, and NEE's
pipeline of clean-power projects positions it as a primary beneficiary.

**Revenue trend:**
- FY2024 revenue: $24.75 billion
- FY2025 revenue: $27.41 billion (+10.7% year-over-year)

Source: [NextEra Energy Q4 2025 Earnings Release](https://www.investor.nexteraenergy.com/~/media/Files/N/NEE-IR/reports-and-fillings/quarterly-earnings/2025/Q4%202025/2026-0127%20NEEQ42025News%20Release%20vF.pdf); [companiesmarketcap.com NEE revenue](https://companiesmarketcap.com/nextera-energy/revenue/)

**EPS trend (adjusted):**
- FY2023: $3.17 per share
- FY2024: $3.43 per share (+8.2%)
- FY2025: $3.71 per share (+8.2%)
- FY2026 guidance: $3.63–$4.00 per share
- FY2027 guidance: $3.85–$4.32 per share

Source: [NEE Q4 2025 news release (same link above)](https://www.investor.nexteraenergy.com/~/media/Files/N/NEE-IR/reports-and-fillings/quarterly-earnings/2025/Q4%202025/2026-0127%20NEEQ42025News%20Release%20vF.pdf)

**Free cash flow note:**
GAAP free cash flow is negative due to enormous capital expenditure building out
the regulated rate base and new clean-energy projects. This is normal and expected
for a capital-intensive regulated utility in a growth phase; the economically
relevant metric is adjusted EPS (which compounds at ~8% annually) and the
rate-base growth that underpins future earnings. The research analyst should
confirm the dividend coverage ratio from the full thesis.

**Valuation:**
Forward P/E ~22–24x; EV/EBITDA ~17x (as of mid-2026). The company trades at a
premium to utility sector peers (sector median EV/EBITDA ~10x) but the premium
reflects 8–10% annual EPS growth vs. sector average of 3–5%.

Source: [GuruFocus NEE EV/EBITDA](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/enterprise-value-to-ebitda/NEE); [StockAnalysis NEE statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nee/statistics/)

**Main risk / thesis-break trigger:**
Rising long-term interest rates compress regulated utility valuations (rate-base
returns are set years ahead). Florida regulatory risk: FPL's allowed return on
equity is reset periodically; a politically driven rate reduction would lower
earnings. Heavy total debt (~$95 billion as of 2025), typical for a regulated
utility, is manageable while the business has guaranteed cash flows — but it
amplifies any rate shock.

**Why we want it:**
NEE is the quality leader in a sector we have no exposure to. It compounds EPS at
~8% annually in a fully regulated, predictable business — closer to a bond with
growth than a cyclical. The AI-driven electricity demand surge gives NEER an
additional secular tailwind. Dividend growth is guided at ~10% per year.

### LIN — Linde plc

**Sector gap filled:** Materials (previously zero coverage)

**Business in two sentences:**
Linde is the world's largest industrial gas company, producing and delivering
oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, argon, and specialty gases to manufacturers, hospitals,
semiconductor fabs, and oil refineries in more than 100 countries. It controls
roughly 31% of the global industrial gas market, alongside Air Liquide (~23%) and
Air Products (~13%).

**Moat:**
Exceptional, multi-layer moat:
1. *Infrastructure lock-in*: Linde builds gas-production plants directly on customer
   sites (steel mills, semiconductor fabs, refineries). The customer's entire process
   depends on a continuous, on-site supply; switching is not just expensive — it often
   requires rebuilding part of the production facility.
2. *Long-term contracts*: On-site supply agreements run 15–20 years with take-or-pay
   terms, providing visible, recurring cash flows.
3. *Route density cost advantage*: A driver servicing five cylinder customers on the
   same street has drastically lower cost per delivery than a competitor serving one;
   the incumbent's density is essentially impossible to replicate without losing money
   for years.
4. *Oligopoly structure*: The top three players control ~67% of the global market.
   Building the network of pipelines, liquefaction plants, and local routes takes
   50–100 years and billions in capital; there has been no meaningful new entrant in
   decades.

Source: [Linde moat analysis — BizModelMastery](https://bizmodelmastery.substack.com/p/inside-linde-how-20-year-contracts); [Compounding Titans — Linde profile](https://compoundingtitans.substack.com/p/linde-plc-the-invisible-giant)

**Revenue trend:**
- FY2024 revenue: $33.0 billion
- FY2025 guidance (adjusted EPS): $16.15–$16.55 per share (implies continued mid-single-digit growth)

Source: [Linde Q4 2024 Earnings Release](https://www.linde.com/news-and-media/2025/linde-reports-full-year-and-fourth-quarter-2024-results)

**EPS and cash flow (FY2024):**
- GAAP EPS: $13.62 per diluted share
- Free cash flow: $4.9 billion (strong, positive)
- EBITDA: $12.4 billion (29.5% operating margin — among the highest in any industrial)
- Return on capital: 25.9%

Source: [Linde FY2024 earnings release tables](https://assets.linde.com/-/media/global/corporate/corporate/documents/press-releases/2024/linde-4q24-earnings-release-tables.pdf)

**Balance sheet:**
Cash $4.8 billion vs. long-term debt $15.3 billion (net debt ~$10.5 billion against
$12.4 billion EBITDA — roughly 0.85x net leverage). Very manageable.

Source: [StockTitan LIN financials](https://www.stocktitan.net/financials/LIN/)

**Valuation:**
Trailing P/E ~34x; forward P/E ~28x; EV/EBITDA ~19x. Linde trades at a premium to
Air Liquide (forward P/E ~25x) and Air Products. The premium reflects Linde's higher
returns on capital and more consistent margin expansion.

Source: [FinanceCharts LIN P/E](https://www.financecharts.com/stocks/LIN/value/pe-ratio); [ad-hoc-news.de LIN valuation](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/ueberblick/linde-plc-stock-ie000s9ys762-valuation-metrics-put-premium-pricing-in/69524580)

**Main risk / thesis-break trigger:**
Global industrial production slowdown cuts volumes (Linde's volumes track steel,
semiconductor, and chemical output). Hydrogen energy transition is a long-promised
tailwind that has been slow to materialise; large bets on green hydrogen projects
carry execution risk. Currency headwinds from a strong dollar compress reported
earnings (though most contracts are in local currency).

**Why we want it:**
Linde is arguably one of the five or six highest-quality industrial businesses in
the world. It has pricing power, locked-in long-term contracts, capital-light
repeat revenues, industry-leading margins, and ~8–10% annual EPS growth. It fills
the materials gap with a business that is essentially disruption-proof (oxygen and
nitrogen are not going away). The analyst should check whether the current 28x
forward P/E offers an adequate margin of safety before any purchase.

### PLD — Prologis, Inc.

**Sector gap filled:** Real estate (previously zero coverage)

**Business in two sentences:**
Prologis is the world's largest owner and developer of logistics real estate:
warehouses, distribution centres, and last-mile fulfilment hubs. It operates
roughly 1.3 billion square feet of space across 20 countries, serving approximately
6,500 tenants including Amazon, UPS, FedEx, and most major retailers.

**Moat:**
Scale and location scarcity. The best logistics real estate sits adjacent to
major ports, rail yards, and dense urban populations — and cannot be easily
replicated. Once a tenant builds out a warehouse (racking, conveyor systems,
IT infrastructure) and trains staff at a specific location, the cost of moving is
enormous relative to the rent saved. Prologis's global scale also lets it offer
customers the same lease terms and credit standards across dozens of markets — a
real advantage for multinational tenants managing global supply chains from a
single real-estate partner. Its development platform (land bank, relationships,
construction expertise) creates additional barriers.

Source: [Alpha Spread — PLD economic moat](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/nyse/pld/qualitative/block/economic-moat)

**Revenue and FFO trend:**
- FY2024 total revenue: $8.20 billion
- FY2025 total revenue: $8.79 billion (+7.2%)
- FY2024 Core FFO per share: $5.56
- FY2025 Core FFO per share: $5.81 (+4.5%)

Source: [Prologis Q4 & FY2025 Results Press Release](https://ir.prologis.com/press-releases/detail/1029/prologis-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)

**Free cash flow and balance sheet:**
- TTM free cash flow (Sep 2025): ~$5.2 billion — strong, covers dividends comfortably
- Debt/Adjusted EBITDA: 5.3x (within the REIT sector norm of 5–6x)
- Weighted-average interest rate on debt: 3.3% (locked in at low fixed rates)
- Weighted-average debt maturity: 8.2 years (limited near-term refinancing risk)
- Total available liquidity: $7.6 billion

Source: [Prologis FY2025 results press release (same link above)](https://ir.prologis.com/press-releases/detail/1029/prologis-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)

**Valuation:**
P/AFFO approximately 20–22x — in line with or slightly above REIT sector peers,
justified by Prologis's scale premium and consistent FFO growth.

Source: [AlphaSpread PLD summary](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/nyse/pld/summary)

**Main risk / thesis-break trigger:**
E-commerce growth slowing or reshoring supply chains (which would change the
locations tenants need) could reduce demand for Prologis's existing asset base.
Rising interest rates hurt REIT valuations directly (cap rate expansion lowers
asset values) and raise refinancing costs over time. Oversupply of new logistics
space — which spiked in 2022–2023 — could compress rents if developers overbuild
again.

**Why we want it:**
Prologis is the blue-chip choice for real-estate exposure — the only logistics REIT
large enough, liquid enough, and diversified enough to fit our mandate. E-commerce
structurally requires 3x the logistics square footage of traditional retail,
providing a long-duration demand tailwind. The balance sheet is conservatively
managed for a REIT (3.3% average rate, 8-year maturity), and FCF covers the
dividend with room to grow. The analyst should pay close attention to near-term
rent spread trends and occupancy rates when writing the full thesis.
