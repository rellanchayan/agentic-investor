## Proposed additions

### 1. PGR — The Progressive Corporation (Insurance / Financials)

Progressive is the largest personal-auto insurer in the United States and one of the most
profitable underwriters in the industry. The business model is straightforward: take in
premiums, pay claims, invest the float. Progressive's edge is in the third word — "pay
claims." The company has spent 30 years building a proprietary telematics and pricing
engine (Snapshot and the more recent Product Model 9.0) that lets it price individual
driver risk more accurately than peers. The result is a combined ratio of 86.4 in Q1 2026
— meaning for every $100 in premium collected, Progressive kept about $14 before investment
income. Most competitors are near or above 100 (breakeven). That data and pricing moat is
hard to replicate because it compounds: more policies generate more driving data, which
sharpens the model, which lets Progressive win better risks at cheaper prices. Progressive
is large, liquid, NYSE-listed, and consistently profitable with real free cash flow.

**Moat type:** Data/analytics cost advantage + scale in telematics.
**Gap filled:** Insurance is entirely absent from our watchlist.
**Why watch now:** The sector sold off broadly during the 2025 cat-loss cycle. Progressive
navigated it better than peers, and the combination ratio improvement in early 2026 suggests
the pricing engine is working. Worth having a fair-value framework ready.

### 2. SYK — Stryker Corporation (Medical Devices / Health Care)

Stryker makes orthopedic implants (knees, hips), surgical instruments, hospital beds, and
neurovascular devices. The Mako robotic surgery platform is now the centerpiece: once a
hospital buys a Mako robot, surgeons train on it, outcomes data accumulates in Stryker's
ecosystem, and the hospital becomes a captive buyer of Stryker implants for the robot's
lifetime. That razor-and-blade dynamic, combined with clinical evidence barriers (changing
implant vendors mid-career is career risk for a surgeon), creates exceptionally high
switching costs. Stryker reported 11% organic revenue growth and gross margins of ~64% in
recent quarters, with Q1 2026 net income up 14% year-over-year. Market cap is approximately
$104 billion. Large, liquid NYSE stock.

**Moat type:** Switching costs (surgeon preference + robotic ecosystem lock-in) + patents +
regulatory barriers.
**Gap filled:** We track four pharma/biotech names in health care but zero medical-device
companies. Devices have a different economic profile — recurring implant volume rather than
patent cliffs — and Stryker is the clearest compounder in the space.
**Why watch now:** Stryker's price has lagged the broader market at times when consumer
confidence in elective surgery weakens. Any such softness would be worth examining against
the long-term structural demand from aging demographics.

### 3. CSX — CSX Corporation (Railroads / Industrials)

CSX operates roughly 20,000 miles of rail in the eastern United States, connecting the
Southeast, Midwest, and Northeast. No one is building a competing railroad. The economics
are a natural oligopoly: two Class I railroads (CSX and Norfolk Southern) divide most of
the eastern US, with barriers to entry that are functionally infinite. CSX earns operating
margins around 37% and a net margin above 20%, which is elite for an industrials business.
Revenue is diversified across merchandise (chemicals, agricultural products, metals),
intermodal containers, and coal. The coal exposure is a long-term headwind worth monitoring,
but merchandise and intermodal are structurally growing. Balance sheet carries debt, as all
railroads do — capital-intensive infrastructure is debt-funded by design — but debt is
serviced comfortably by operating cash flow. NASDAQ-listed, large-cap (~$74B), liquid.

**Moat type:** Irreplaceable physical infrastructure (impossible to replicate a rail network
corridor, and land easements make it doubly impossible).
**Gap filled:** UNP covers the western US; adding CSX gives us an eastern railroad for
comparison and diversifies the industrials bench.
**Why watch now:** Railroads were range-bound in mid-2026 amid intermodal softness. Any
further weakness in the cycle creates the opportunity to think hard about entry.

### 4. NOW — ServiceNow, Inc. (Enterprise Workflow Software / Technology)

ServiceNow runs the operating system for large enterprises' IT, HR, finance, and operations
workflows. It started as an IT service-management ticketing platform and has expanded into
every department that runs on process. The moat is one of the strongest in software: once a
company's IT operations, HR approvals, procurement flows, and security incident response all
run through the ServiceNow platform, removing it is a multi-year program that no CIO wants
to sign. The evidence is in the renewal rate: 98-99% consistently across a $13 billion
trailing revenue base. In 2026 the company guided to 20% revenue growth and a ~35% free
cash flow margin. That is a business generating roughly $4-5 billion in real cash per year
while growing fast. NYSE-listed, large-cap, very liquid.

**Moat type:** Deep switching costs from enterprise-wide workflow integration.
**Gap filled:** We have ADBE (creative tools) and CRM (sales CRM) but no pure-play enterprise
workflow platform. ServiceNow occupies a different, stickier niche.
**Why watch now:** At CAPE ~40x the market is expensive broadly, and software multiples
compress hardest in a valuation reset. NOW is likely priced for perfection today, but the
research analyst should establish a fair-value model and entry threshold now so we are ready
if and when the multiple contracts.

## Names considered and rejected

**Applied Materials (AMAT):** Strong financials (50% gross margins, 33% operating margins,
ROE ~56%), and a genuine quasi-monopoly in some advanced-node deposition equipment. However,
the moat analysis flagged it as "wide but not absolute" — unlike ASML's near-total monopoly
on EUV lithography, AMAT competes with Lam Research and KLA in several segments. The China
mature-node exposure also introduces an idiosyncratic geopolitical risk. Not rejected
permanently, but the moat case is less clean than the four names above. Recommend revisiting
if the analyst wants a semiconductor equipment name.

**Norfolk Southern (NSC):** The obvious eastern railroad alternative to CSX. Both serve
similar geographies and have similar economics. CSX is preferred here because its operational
turnaround (under the precision scheduled railroading model) is more advanced, and its
Southeast port exposure (Savannah, Jacksonville) to reshoring-driven import volume is
potentially stronger. NSC is not junk — if the analyst prefers it, it would pass the smell
test too.

**Oracle (ORCL):** Massive installed base, extreme database switching costs, growing cloud
backlog. Not proposed because the balance sheet carries heavy acquisition-driven debt and
the business is harder to understand cleanly (legacy licenses, cloud transition, Cerner
integration). The quality is real but the complexity makes it a lower-priority addition to
the bench.

## Analyst priority for next week

The playbook records an explicit cash-drag cost of ~1 pp per month, with a standing lesson
that bench expansion is an obligation with a compounding price. Given that, the two names
most worth moving immediately to a full thesis are:

1. **PGR** — Insurance is completely absent from our coverage. Progressive is one of the
   highest-quality underwriters in US markets with a well-understood business model, and the
   pricing engine is genuinely differentiated. A thesis could be completed in one session.

2. **SYK** — Healthcare devices is our only major health-care sub-sector gap. Stryker's Mako
   robotics moat is straightforward to model, and the demographic tailwind (aging population,
   more hip and knee procedures) makes the demand side easy to understand. Also one session.

CSX and NOW are high quality but less urgent: CSX requires a coal run-off model to do it
properly, and NOW is almost certainly priced for perfection today (meaning the thesis work
is right but the entry threshold is far away). PGR and SYK are more likely to yield an
actionable entry price sooner.
