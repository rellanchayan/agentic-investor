## Candidate 1 — SPGI (S&P Global Inc.)

### What the business does
S&P Global provides financial intelligence through three main segments: **Ratings** (credit
ratings for bonds and structured products), **Market Intelligence** (data, analytics, and
research sold to financial institutions on a subscription basis), and **Indices** (it created
the S&P 500 and charges asset-based licensing fees to the ETFs and funds that track its
benchmarks). A fourth segment, Commodity Insights (Platts), supplies energy and commodity data.
The company completed the acquisition of IHS Markit in 2022, roughly doubling its size, and
spun off its Mobility unit in 2025.

### The moat
The Ratings segment benefits from a **regulatory oligopoly**: the SEC designates only a small
number of Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organizations (NRSROs), and S&P and Moody's
together account for roughly 80% of the global rated-issuance market. Issuers need ratings to
access public debt markets; they cannot easily route around S&P. The Indices segment has a
powerful **network effect and brand moat**: the S&P 500 is the world's most-referenced equity
benchmark; the more money tracks it, the more licensing fee revenue S&P earns, and the less
likely investors are to switch to a competing benchmark. Market Intelligence clients face high
**switching costs** — financial workflows, models, and risk systems are built around S&P's data.
Gurufocus rates the moat score at 8/10 as of July 2026. (Source: gurufocus.com)

### Revenue, earnings, and free cash flow
All figures from S&P Global's SEC filings and stockanalysis.com, cited below.

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | FCF |
|-------------|---------|------------|-----|
| 2022 | $11.2B | $3.2B | $2.5B |
| 2023 | $12.5B | $2.6B | $3.6B |
| 2024 | $14.2B | $3.9B | $5.6B |
| 2025 | $15.3B | $4.5B | $5.5B |

Revenue has grown at approximately 8% per year. The 2023 net-income dip reflected integration
costs from the IHS Markit deal; underlying cash generation rose strongly every year. FCF reached
$5.5B in 2025, a level that easily covers the dividend and a $5.0B share-buyback programme.
Adjusted diluted EPS grew 19% in 2025 to $14.66 (company press release, February 2026).

### Balance sheet
S&P Global carries approximately $13.9B in gross debt (taken on to finance the IHS Markit
acquisition) against $1.8B in cash, leaving net debt of roughly $12.1B. That is substantial
but well-covered: the net-debt-to-FCF ratio is approximately 2.2x, and the debt is
investment-grade with long maturities. The company returned $6.2B to shareholders in 2025
($1.2B dividends, $5.0B buybacks), evidence of high confidence in cash generation.

### Approximate valuation (July 25, 2026)
- Price: approximately $427
- Trailing P/E: approximately 27x (EPS $14.66 GAAP 2025 → implied P/E = 29x; the ~27x
  figure from stockanalysis.com likely uses a slightly different earnings base)
- Forward P/E: approximately 22x (2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $19.40–$19.65 per company)
- EV/FCF: approximately 25–26x
- Market cap: approximately $130B

The forward P/E of 22x is well below S&P Global's longer-term average (the stock has
historically traded at 25–35x forward earnings). For the quality of the franchise, this
seems like at least a fair entry zone, though the research analyst will need to build a
proper discounted-cash-flow model.

### Key risks
- **Debt-issuance cyclicality**: Ratings revenue falls sharply when bond markets seize up
  (as in 2022–2023). This is partly offset by the subscription businesses.
- **Regulatory risk**: Further tightening of NRSRO rules or the introduction of new
  government-backed ratings frameworks could erode the duopoly.
- **AI disruption**: Competitors could use AI to undercut proprietary data products in
  Market Intelligence, though S&P's own Kensho AI unit and deep proprietary datasets are
  a potential offset.

### Sector gap filled

(V, MA) and the bank (JPM) already on the watchlist.

## Candidate 2 — ISRG (Intuitive Surgical Inc.)

### What the business does
Intuitive Surgical designs, manufactures, and sells the **da Vinci robotic surgical system**
and related instruments, accessories, and service contracts. Surgeons operate the robot from a
console to perform minimally invasive procedures (prostatectomy, hysterectomy, colorectal,
thoracic, and more). Revenue has three components: one-time system sales (~$650M per quarter
in Q2 2025), recurring instruments and accessories (~$1.6B/quarter, consumed every procedure),
and service contracts on the installed base (~$433M/quarter). Recurring revenue makes up
roughly 80% of total revenue.

### The moat
- **Installed-base lock-in**: Intuitive had 11,106 da Vinci systems installed worldwide at
  the end of 2025, up 12% from 9,902 a year earlier. Each system costs $1–2M+ to purchase;
  hospitals build operating-room layouts, capital budgets, and OR-staff schedules around their
  robots. Removal and replacement with a competing system is a multi-year disruption.
- **Surgeon-training switching costs**: Most robotic surgeons learn on da Vinci during
  residency. Credentialing on a new platform means retraining the surgeon and the entire
  OR team, creating powerful inertia.
- **Patent thicket and clinical data**: Two decades of patents and the largest body of
  peer-reviewed clinical data for robotic soft-tissue surgery; FDA-cleared indications took
  years to accumulate.
- **Flywheel**: More installed systems → more trained surgeons → more procedures → more
  accessory revenue → more R&D budget to extend the lead.
- Market share in soft-tissue robotic surgery is estimated at 70%+ globally (Motley Fool,
  July 1, 2026). Procedure volumes grew 18% in 2025.

### Revenue, earnings, and free cash flow
All figures from Intuitive Surgical SEC filings and stockanalysis.com, cited below.

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | Gross margin | Op. margin |
|-------------|---------|------------|--------------|------------|
| 2022 | $6.2B | $1.3B | ~66% | ~25% |
| 2023 | $7.1B | $1.8B | ~66% | ~25% |
| 2024 | $8.4B | $2.3B | ~67% | ~28% |
| 2025 | $10.1B | $2.9B | ~66% | ~29% |

Revenue has compounded at roughly 18–20% per year for the past three years. Net income
compounded at similar rates. FCF reached $2.5B in 2025 (per Macrotrends), up from $1.3B
in 2024, reflecting improving capital efficiency as the installed base matures.

### Balance sheet
Exceptional: net cash of $5.2B as of mid-2026 (stockanalysis.com). No meaningful financial
debt. This gives management flexibility to invest in the next-generation da Vinci 5 system
(launched 2024) and the Ion bronchoscopy platform without balance-sheet risk.

### Approximate valuation (July 25, 2026)
- Price: approximately $337 (July 25, 2026 per investing.com)
- Trailing P/E: approximately 43x (net income $2.9B / $7.87 EPS; at $337 → 42.8x)
- Forward P/E: approximately 33x (FinanceCharts / GuruFocus)
- Market cap: approximately $122B

The stock is down roughly 38% from its 52-week high, driven by general growth-stock
de-rating and China headwinds (domestic Chinese robotics competition and government
pricing pressure on tenders). The valuation is still elevated in absolute terms; this
is not a cheap stock. However, the business quality and growth rate are among the best
in health care. The research analyst should model whether the growth runway (global
penetration of robotic surgery is still low) justifies the multiple.

### Key risks
- **Valuation**: Even after a 38% decline, the stock trades at 33x forward earnings.
  Any growth-rate slowdown would compress the multiple meaningfully.
- **Competitive entry**: Johnson & Johnson's Ottava system is in late-stage development;
  Medtronic's Hugo is gaining CE mark clearances in Europe. Neither has replicated
  ISRG's clinical data volume or surgeon-training network yet, but the threat is real.
- **China**: Government-mandated tenders and domestic robot makers (Tinavi, Microport) are
  gaining share in the Chinese market, which has historically been a growth driver.
- **Procedure concentration**: Half of da Vinci revenue still comes from a small number of
  high-volume procedure types; any clinical guidance changes could slow adoption.

### Sector gap filled
Health care — medical devices, an area entirely absent from the current watchlist (which
covers managed care, pharma, and biotech only).

## Candidate 3 — TJX (The TJX Companies Inc.)

### What the business does
TJX is the world's largest off-price apparel and home fashions retailer. It operates
approximately 4,900 stores across 9 countries under the brands T.J. Maxx, Marshalls, and
HomeGoods in the United States; Winners and HomeSense in Canada; TK Maxx and HomeSense in
Europe; and TK Maxx in Australia. The off-price model works differently from department
stores: TJX buys excess inventory, cancelled orders, and overruns from over 21,000 vendors
at substantial discounts and passes part of the savings to shoppers. Customers come for the
"treasure hunt" experience, not a predictable catalogue.

### The moat
- **Scale and sourcing network**: With $56B in annual revenue and 21,000+ vendor relationships,
  TJX receives first call on the best off-price deals. A newcomer with 50 stores cannot access
  the same deal flow. This is a genuine cost/scale advantage.
- **Operational expertise**: The logistics of buying opportunistically (deal size, timing,
  price, and assortment all vary unpredictably) requires decades of institutional knowledge.
  TJX has been refining this system since 1976. It is genuinely difficult to copy.
- **Counter-cyclical resilience**: Off-price retail gains share during recessions as
  consumers trade down from full-price department stores and specialty retailers. TJX grew
  through the 2008–2009 and 2020 downturns.
- **Private-label insignificance**: TJX sells primarily branded goods at a discount, which
  drives brand-name shoppers into its stores. Customers who feel they got a good deal return.

TJX's ROIC has exceeded WACC by 14%+ in recent years per the 2026 analysis on artificall.com,
consistent with a genuine moat.

### Revenue, earnings, and free cash flow
Fiscal years end in late January / early February.
All figures from TJX investor relations and stockanalysis.com, cited below.

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | EPS | FCF |
|-------------|---------|------------|-----|-----|
| FY2022 (Jan 2022) | $48.6B | $3.3B | $2.70 | $2.0B |
| FY2023 (Jan 2023) | $49.9B | $3.5B | $2.97 | $2.6B |
| FY2024 (Jan 2024) | $54.2B | $4.5B | $3.86 | $4.3B |
| FY2025 (Feb 2025) | $56.4B | $4.9B | $4.26 | $4.2B |

Revenue has grown modestly (TJX is already enormous) but earnings and FCF have grown faster
as margins improved. The company generated $6.1B of operating cash flow in FY2025 and ended
the year with $5.3B in cash. Management plans to buy back $2.0–$2.5B of stock in FY2026
and increase the dividend by 13%.

### Balance sheet
Healthy. TJX had $5.3B in cash at fiscal year end (February 2025). The company does carry
lease obligations (as all retailers do), but net debt is manageable relative to its cash flow.

### Approximate valuation (July 25, 2026)
- Price: approximately $155 (July 24–25, 2026 range $152–$155 per MarketBeat)
- Trailing P/E: approximately 30x
- Forward P/E: approximately 29x
- Market cap: approximately $172B

TJX's 5-year average trailing P/E has been approximately 27x, so the current valuation is
modestly above its recent norm. Shares are up approximately 31% over the past year
(financecharts.com), reflecting strong FY2025 results and continued market share gains.
The research analyst should assess whether the forward growth rate (mid-single-digit revenue,
double-digit EPS due to buybacks) justifies a premium to historical multiples.

### Key risks
- **Tariff exposure**: TJX sources a significant portion of goods from outside the US.
  Tariff increases (especially on China-origin goods) could compress merchandise margins.
  Management has historically managed this through vendor renegotiation, but a sustained
  tariff regime is a genuine headwind.
- **Consumer spending slowdown**: If shoppers pull back broadly, even off-price retail can
  be affected, though TJX historically fares better than full-price peers.
- **Inventory availability**: The off-price model depends on a steady supply of excess
  inventory from brands. A shift to tighter inventory management by brands (as happened
  post-COVID) could temporarily reduce the supply of attractive deals.
- **International execution**: Expanding TK Maxx in Europe and Australia carries integration
  and currency risk.

### Sector gap filled
Consumer Discretionary — a large, profitable, structurally advantaged retailer with a
different business model and cycle profile from Home Depot (home improvement), McDonald's
(fast food), and Nike (footwear/apparel at full price).
