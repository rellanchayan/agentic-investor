# The Coca-Cola Company (KO) — Research Thesis

**As of:** 2026-Q2 (quarter ended 2026-07-03) · **Written:** 2026-09-11 · **Refresh due:** 2026-12-10
**Price used:** $88.01 (Alpaca) · **Fair value band:** $64.76 / **$76.67** / $87.58
**Verdict: quality business, full price. WATCH, do not buy at $88.**

---

## 1. What the company actually does

Coca-Cola mostly does not make Coca-Cola. It sells **concentrate** — the syrup base — to a network
of independent, authorised **bottlers**. They add the water and sweetener, put it in cans and
bottles, drive the trucks, and stock the shelves. Coca-Cola keeps the brand, the recipe and the
advertising. The bottlers keep the factories and the working capital.

That is the whole point. It means Coca-Cola collects something close to a royalty on every drink
sold, without owning the heavy assets. The numbers show it: in FY2025 the company produced
**$47.941B of revenue** on only **$2.112B of capital spending** and **$1.050B of depreciation**
([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21344/000162828026010047/ko-20251231.htm),
[cash flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21344/000162828026010047/R7.htm)).

Scale, from the 10-K: products are sold in **more than 200 countries**, at a rate of
**2.2 billion servings per day**. The Coca-Cola system sold **33.8 billion unit cases in 2025**
(33.7 billion in 2024). **Sparkling soft drinks are 69%** of worldwide volume and **Trademark
Coca-Cola alone is 47%**.

There is also a smaller **Bottling Investments** segment — bottlers Coca-Cola has temporarily taken
back onto its own books before selling them on ("refranchising"). The current example is
**Coca-Cola Beverages Africa**, carried as **$5.342B of assets held for sale** at 2025-12-31 and
expected to be sold in the second half of 2026.

**Leadership:** James Quincey handed the CEO job to **Henrique Braun on 2026-03-31** and became
Executive Chairman ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21344/000162828026010052/ko-20260219.htm)).

## 2. The moat: **wide**

A "moat" is a lasting advantage that keeps competitors out. Coca-Cola's has three legs:

1. **Brand.** Trademark Coca-Cola is 47% of all volume, worldwide, and has been for decades. You
   cannot buy that with a marketing budget.
2. **Distribution.** The bottler system reaches roughly every corner shop, restaurant and stadium
   on earth. A rival with a better-tasting cola still cannot get onto those shelves.
3. **Scale in marketing.** Advertising costs the same per campaign whether you sell 1 billion or
   33.8 billion cases. Coca-Cola spreads it over the biggest base in the industry.

The evidence that the moat is real is **pricing power**: a **61.6% gross margin** in FY2025
(61.9% over the trailing twelve months) and a **28.7% operating margin**. Companies without a moat
do not keep 62 cents of every sales dollar after the cost of the product.

### How we got ROIC (~17.7%)
ROIC is "profit per dollar the business has tied up." Above 10-15% and steady is good.
- **NOPAT** = operating income $13.762B × (1 − 17.88% tax rate) = **$11.301B**. The tax rate is
  income taxes $2.861B ÷ pre-tax income $15.998B, both from the FY2025 filing.
- **Invested capital** = total equity $34.275B + total debt $45.492B − cash, short-term investments
  and marketable securities $15.806B = **$63.961B**.
- $11.301B ÷ $63.961B = **17.7%**.

*Honest caveat:* about **$20.235B** of that invested capital is stakes in bottlers accounted for by
the equity method. Their profit shows up *below* operating income as "equity income," so it is in
the denominator but not the numerator. True operating ROIC is higher than 17.7%.

### The free-cash-flow footnote you must read
GAAP free cash flow looks weak two years running, and both times it was a **one-off payment**:
- **FY2025:** operating cash flow $7.408B − capex $2.112B = **$5.296B**, because Coca-Cola paid
  **$6.1B** of the final fairlife acquisition milestone in Q1 2025. Excluding it, the company's own
  non-GAAP figure is **$11.4B**.
- **FY2024:** depressed by a **$6.0B deposit** paid to the IRS (see risks).

There is still a genuine quality gap even after normalising: FY2025 net income was $13.107B but
normalised FCF was ~$11.4B, partly because $1.038B of 2025 equity income from bottlers was profit on
paper that was *not* received as cash.

### Recent momentum is genuinely good
Q2 2026: **unit case volume +5%**, organic revenue +6%, operating margin **34.9%** (from 34.1%),
comparable EPS **+11% to $0.97**. Coca-Cola *raised* FY2026 guidance on 2026-07-28 to ~5% organic
revenue growth and **9-10% comparable EPS growth**. That is a real acceleration — in 2025 unit case
volume grew only 0.3%.

## 4. Valuation — and why we are not buying today

Computed with `code/valuation.py` so the arithmetic is checkable.

**Method 1 — P/E.** Trailing twelve-month diluted GAAP EPS is **$3.33** ($3.04 FY2025 − $1.65 H1
2025 + $1.94 H1 2026). We apply multiples of **20 / 24 / 27**:
- 24x base — what we judge a wide-moat staples compounder growing mid-single-digit organic revenue
  and high-single-digit EPS is worth.
- 20x low — a de-rating if volume growth stalls or the tax case is lost.
- 27x high — if 5%+ organic growth persists.
→ **$66.60 / $79.92 / $89.91**

**Method 2 — FCF yield.** FY2025 free cash flow excluding the fairlife one-off, $11.4B ÷ 4,313M
shares = **$2.643 per share**, capitalised at **4.2% / 3.6% / 3.1%**.
→ **$62.93 / $73.42 / $85.26**

**Blended fair value: low $64.76 · base $76.67 · high $87.58.**

> ⚠️ The multiples and yields above are **our assumptions, not cited facts.** We did not verify
> Coca-Cola's historical average P/E, so we do not quote one.

**Sensitivity:** if you use FY2026 *guided* FCF of $12.4B ($2.875/share) instead of the FY2025
actual, the band rises to **$67.53 / $79.89 / $91.33** — the base is *still* below today's price.

### What $88.01 is asking us to pay
- **26.4x** trailing GAAP earnings of $3.33
- **~26.7x** the midpoint of FY2026 guided comparable EPS (~$3.27-$3.30)
- a **3.0%** free-cash-flow yield (3.3% on 2026 guidance)
- a **2.41%** dividend yield

**Margin of safety: −12.9%** using `(fair value base − price) / price`, or **−14.8%** using the
codebase's `(base − price) / base` convention. Either way the number is negative: **we would be
paying above what we think the business is worth.** With `min_margin_of_safety = 0.12`, our
**buy trigger is roughly $67.50 or below.**

Trend, from Alpaca: last close **$88.03**, 50-day average **$86.55**, 200-day average **$78.78**,
250-day range **$65.65-$91.97**. The stock is in an uptrend near its high — which is precisely why
it is expensive.

## 5. The risks — written as hard as the bull case

1. **Price is the biggest risk.** We are paying 26x earnings for a business whose *volume* grew
   0.3% in 2025. Most of the growth is price and mix. If the multiple reverts to 20x, that is a
   ~24% loss before the business does anything wrong.

2. **The IRS case — a near-binary event, pending right now.** The US Tax Court sided predominantly
   with the IRS on Coca-Cola's transfer pricing for 2007-2009 (~$3.3B of tax plus interest;
   Coca-Cola already paid **$6.0B** against IRS invoices in 2024). Coca-Cola appealed, and the
   **11th Circuit heard oral argument on 2026-06-25** — a ruling could land any day. The FY2025
   10-K states the potential **aggregate remaining incremental tax and interest for 2010-2025 could
   be approximately $14 billion** (~**$3.25 per share**), and that continued application of the Tax
   Court's method would raise the effective tax rate by about **3.5 percentage points** every year
   thereafter. That last part is the one that matters most: it is a permanent ~4% haircut to EPS.

3. **Leverage removes optionality.** Gross debt of **$43.543B** against $16.371B of cash and
   securities at 2026-07-03. Net debt/EBITDA of 1.71x is fine on its own — but combined with a
   possible $14B tax bill and a dividend that cannot be cut, there is less slack than the AA credit
   rating suggests.

4. **The dividend is a rigid claim on cash.** FY2025 dividends of **$8.779B** consumed ~77% of
   normalised free cash flow. Buybacks were only $746M gross. A 64-year streak means management
   will protect the dividend ahead of almost anything else.

5. **Currency flatters the 2026 numbers.** FY2025 EPS carried an **8-point currency headwind**;
   FY2026 guidance assumes a **~3% currency tailwind**. A dollar rally turns that around and the
   growth story looks much duller.

6. **Secular pressure on sugary drinks.** Sugar taxes, health regulation, GLP-1 weight-loss drugs
   reducing consumption occasions, and packaging litigation (a Baltimore environmental suit is
   disclosed in the 10-K). Sparkling soft drinks are still 69% of volume.

7. **A brand-new CEO.** Henrique Braun took over on 2026-03-31 after Quincey's long tenure.
   Unproven over a full cycle.

8. **Noisy comparisons.** The pending CCBA sale creates a 2-3% revenue headwind in FY2026 and makes
   reported numbers harder to read.

## 6. Sell triggers (the rules we follow, written while calm)

1. Gross margin below **55%** for two consecutive quarters (FY2025: 61.6%).
2. Global unit case volume **declines year-over-year for two consecutive quarters**.
3. Organic revenue growth below **2%** for two consecutive quarters, **or** reported revenue shrinks
   year-over-year for two consecutive fiscal years.
4. Net debt / EBITDA rises **above 3.0x** (1.71x today), or debt is raised to fund the dividend.
5. Coca-Cola **loses the 11th Circuit appeal AND** the ruling is extended to the 2010+ years near
   the ~$14B the company itself disclosed — or the ongoing effective tax rate rises **3+ points**
   on a sustained basis without an offsetting operational gain.
6. The dividend is **cut or frozen** for a full year (ending the 64-year streak), or dividends paid
   exceed normalised free cash flow for two consecutive years.
7. Normalised free cash flow falls **below $9B** in a full fiscal year ($11.4B in FY2025).
8. Comparable operating margin below **28%** for two consecutive quarters (Q2 2026: 35.6%).
9. Any serious **accounting red flag**, restatement, or auditor change.

## 7. What we are watching for

- **The 11th Circuit ruling.** A win removes a ~$14B disclosed overhang. A loss is the main downside
  catalyst — and, paradoxically, the most likely source of the cheaper price we want.
- **Q3 2026 earnings (expected late October 2026)** — does the +5% volume hold, or was it a FIFA
  World Cup marketing effect?
- **The CCBA sale closing** in H2 2026.
- **A pullback to $65-$70**, which would put Coca-Cola inside our band with the margin of safety our
  rules require.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Coca-Cola is one of the few genuinely **wide-moat** businesses we can own: 62% gross margins, ~18%
ROIC, capital-light, 64 straight years of dividend increases, and — right now — real operating
momentum with volume up 5%. **Conviction 4** reflects that quality, and every number above comes
from an SEC filing.

But conviction is not a buy signal. **At $88.01 the stock trades roughly 13% above our base fair
value of $76.67**, offering no cushion at all, while a pending court decision carries a disclosed
$14B tail risk. A missed buy costs nothing; an overpriced buy costs money.

**Action: add to the watch list. Buy trigger at approximately $67.50 or below.**
