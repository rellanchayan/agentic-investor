## New candidates proposed

### 1. SPGI — S&P Global Inc.

**Business in two sentences:** S&P Global runs four businesses: Ratings (credit ratings on bonds for issuers and investors), Market Intelligence (subscription data and analytics), Commodity Insights (energy and commodities data), and Indices (licensing the S&P 500 and thousands of other benchmarks). The indices unit alone earns fees every time a passive fund manager or ETF provider uses an S&P benchmark.

**Moat:** Regulatory entrenchment and century-old brand credibility make the Ratings business a near-duopoly with Moody's (MCO) — the two together hold roughly 80% of global credit ratings market share, with bond mandates often citing specific agencies by name. The S&P 500 index license is a true toll road: every passive dollar tracking that benchmark pays a fee. Switching costs in Market Intelligence are high because client workflows are deeply embedded.

**Smell test:**
- Revenue (FY 2025): $15.3 billion, +8% year-over-year. Source: [S&P Global FY 2025 earnings release](https://s29.q4cdn.com/690959130/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/S-P-Global-4Q-FY-2025-Earnings-Release-Exhibits-2-10-2026.pdf)
- 2025 GAAP EPS: $14.66 (+19%); adjusted EPS: $17.83 (+14%).
- 2026 guided adjusted EPS: $19.40–$19.65.
- Trailing twelve-month free cash flow (through Jun 2026): ~$5.6 billion. Source: [GuruFocus SPGI FCF](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/total-free-cash-flow/SPGI)
- Net margin: 29% (FY 2025).
- Current price (Aug 10, 2026): ~$411. Forward PE ~21x. Five-year average PE: ~38x — current valuation is roughly 45% below the historical norm. Source: [FinanceCharts SPGI PE](https://www.financecharts.com/stocks/SPGI/value/pe-ratio)
- Market cap: large-cap, NYSE-listed, highly liquid.
- Risk flag: AI tools may compete with the Market Intelligence data-analytics segment over time; the Ratings business is better insulated due to regulatory mandates. Source: [Seeking Alpha MCO vs SPGI](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4866537-moodys-vs-s-and-p-global-ai-concerns-have-hit-safe-havens-but-one-is-better-protected)

**Sector gap filled:** Financial data — distinct from payments and banking already on watchlist.

**Verdict:** Passes the smell test. High quality, very profitable, clear multi-part moat, trading at an unusually low valuation relative to history. Warrants a full thesis.

### 2. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical Inc.

**Business in two sentences:** Intuitive Surgical makes the da Vinci robotic surgical system, which surgeons use to perform minimally invasive procedures in urology, gynecology, colorectal, and thoracic surgery. It earns money three ways: selling or leasing the robot system, licensing instruments and accessories that are consumed with each procedure, and servicing installed machines — a classic razor-and-blades model.

**Moat:** Once a hospital installs a da Vinci system and trains its surgeons and operating room staff on it, the switching cost is enormous — retraining staff, replacing capital equipment, and disrupting established surgical workflows. Intuitive had roughly 9,500 da Vinci systems installed globally as of early 2026. The consumables attach rate grows with each procedure, making revenue highly recurring.

**Smell test:**
- Q2 2026 revenue: $2.89 billion, +19% year-over-year. Q1 2026 revenue: $2.77 billion, +23% YoY. Source: [Intuitive Q1 2026 earnings](https://isrg.intuitive.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-announces-first-quarter-earnings-7)
- TTM free cash flow (through Mar 2026): ~$2.8 billion. Source: [GuruFocus ISRG FCF](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/total-free-cash-flow/ISRG)
- Non-GAAP gross margin guidance (2026): 67–68%.
- Price (Jul 16, 2026): ~$402; forward PE ~30x. Market cap ~$119 billion. Source: [StockAnalysis ISRG](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/isrg/)
- Year-to-date performance: down approximately 30%, which is what puts it on the scout's radar.
- Risk flag: reports of "slowing growth in da Vinci procedures and increased competition" cited in recent news. A full thesis must assess whether competition from Medtronic's Hugo system and others is a temporary headwind or a structural moat-erosion event. Source: [StockAnalysis ISRG overview](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/isrg/)

**Sector gap filled:** Health care equipment/devices — distinct from pharma (LLY, ABBV), managed care (UNH), and diversified pharma (JNJ) already on watchlist.

**Verdict:** Passes the smell test on quality and liquidity. The 30% YTD decline makes it potentially interesting on valuation, but the competition concern must be investigated before building a thesis. Flag for analyst: determine whether competing systems are genuine substitutes or remain clearly inferior.

### 3. ODFL — Old Dominion Freight Line Inc.

**Business in two sentences:** Old Dominion is the largest independent less-than-truckload (LTL) freight carrier in the United States, moving partial loads for businesses that do not need a full trailer. It earns its premium not by being cheapest but by having the lowest cargo damage rate and best on-time delivery record in the industry.

**Moat:** LTL is a network business: more service centers and more daily shipments reduce cost-per-unit and improve delivery times, which allows ODFL to charge a premium over competitors. That premium funds further network investment, creating a flywheel. The company's operating ratio (operating costs as a share of revenue) is consistently the best among US LTL carriers, reflecting genuine operational advantage rather than luck.

**Smell test:**
- FY 2025 revenue: $5.5 billion (down ~5.5% due to cyclical freight market weakness). Source: [StockAnalysis ODFL revenue](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/odfl/revenue/)
- Q2 2026 EPS: $1.68, +32.3% year-over-year. Q2 2026 operating ratio: 70.1%, improved 450 basis points year-over-year — the operating leverage is working. Source: [Old Dominion Q2 2026 press release](https://ir.odfl.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/342/old-dominion-freight-line-reports-first-quarter-2026)
- H1 2026 operating cash flow: $646.3 million.
- Price (Aug 17, 2026): ~$212; PE ~41x; market cap ~$44 billion. Source: [Morningstar ODFL](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/xnas/odfl/quote)
- The PE looks elevated but is common for cyclical businesses early in a freight recovery — earnings are recovering from a trough. An analyst should build a normalized-earnings valuation.
- Risk flag: freight is economically sensitive. Revenue declined in 2025 and early 2026 due to weak industrial demand. The thesis must assess whether Q2's improvement represents a durable freight recovery or a one-quarter bounce.

**Sector gap filled:** Freight/logistics within industrials — different moat type from heavy equipment (CAT), conglomerates (HON), or railroads (UNP).

**Verdict:** Passes the smell test. Best-in-class franchise in a durable industry, profitable throughout the cycle, large and liquid. Cyclical nature means an analyst needs to build a through-the-cycle normalized earnings estimate before setting an entry price.

## Existing watchlist names to prioritize for thesis-building this week

The screen data shows several quality names that have pulled back materially while the market has risen. These are already on the watchlist — they need a thesis, not scouting.

### Priority 1: MCD — McDonald's Corp. ($271.07, -19.0% six months)

McDonald's is a franchise-and-real-estate business masquerading as a restaurant chain: ~95% of its locations are franchised, so it earns royalties and rent rather than bearing food or labor costs directly. The brand and the real estate ownership create a durable two-layer moat. A -19% six-month decline is meaningful for a business of this stability. An analyst should check whether recent weakness reflects temporary consumer-spending pressure or a structural issue with the brand. This is the most attractive existing watchlist candidate for a thesis this week.

### Priority 2: PG — Procter & Gamble Co. ($144.72, -12.4% six months)

Procter & Gamble owns brands (Tide, Pampers, Gillette, Dawn) with decades of shelf-placement leverage over retailers, enabling consistent pricing power. A -12.4% decline takes it closer to historical valuation norms for a defensive staples name. Consumer staples companies with PG's pricing power tend to look most attractive during periods of consumer spending anxiety, which may be what's driving the pullback.

### Priority 3: HON — Honeywell International Inc. ($215.99, -11.1% six months)

Honeywell is an industrial/aerospace conglomerate with strong positions in building controls, industrial automation, and aerospace components. The company has been undergoing a portfolio restructuring. The -11.1% decline warrants investigation: is this multiple compression from the restructuring uncertainty, or has something changed in the underlying businesses? Either answer is useful for thesis-building.
