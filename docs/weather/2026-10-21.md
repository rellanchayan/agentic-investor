# Market Weather — Wednesday, October 21, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** September CPI came in hotter than expected at 3.7%, the Fed meets in
seven days with the door open to another rate hike, the 10-year Treasury yield sits near 5.28%
making bonds a genuine competitor to overpriced equities, and all our thesis candidates still
trade well above fair value — conditions require a larger discount before buying.

## What is SPY doing?

SPY is at **$770.35**, which is **6.1% above** its 200-day moving average of $726.25.

The 200-day average is the benchmark for the market's long-run direction. When the price sits
above it, the broad trend is up — there is no crash underway. SPY being 6.1% above that line
means the market has drifted higher without pulling back to the average. This is not a signal
to buy in panic; it is a signal that the market is not on sale.

**Premarket futures today:** S&P 500 futures are up about **+0.24%** and Nasdaq futures are up
about **+0.70%** this morning. The rally is tech-led — SpaceX reported Q3 earnings that beat
analysts' expectations overnight, and Tesla is also trading higher ahead of its report after
today's close. Wells Fargo's Sentiment Indicator reportedly moved into "buy" territory after a
recent mini-selloff, suggesting retail investors may be returning to equities.

A modest futures bounce after a single earnings beat is not a change in the structural backdrop.
The market was expensive yesterday and it is expensive today.

## Interest rates and the Fed

This remains the most important factor shaping our posture.

- **Fed funds rate: 3.75%–4.00%.** The Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points at its
  September 16 meeting, restarting its tightening cycle after a pause.
- **10-year Treasury yield: approximately 5.28%.** This was confirmed on October 15; no fresh
  quote was available for today, and nothing in the available news points to a significant move
  since then. A 5.28% risk-free yield is one of the highest in over two decades.
- **Next FOMC meeting: October 27–28, decision Wednesday October 28 at 2:00 PM ET.** That is
  seven days away. The September CPI print (discussed below) gives the Fed grounds to hike
  again if it chooses. Markets are not pricing in a high probability of a hike, but the risk
  is real and the meeting is imminent.
- **White House pressure on rates:** A Fortune article from this morning notes that the White
  House is publicly pressuring Fed Chair Warsh to cut rates, citing rising debt-service costs.
  This political noise does not change actual policy, and the Fed has historically defended its
  independence. We note it only as a sign that rate expectations are contested.

What this means in plain English: when a 10-year government bond pays 5.28% with no risk of
default, every stock must earn a meaningfully higher return to justify the extra risk. At current
equity prices, most large-cap stocks do not clear that bar. This is the mathematical reason all
our thesis candidates have fair-value estimates far below their current prices.

## Inflation

**September CPI (released October 14):**
- Headline: **3.7% YoY** — above the 3.6% consensus
- Core: **2.6% YoY** — above the 2.5% consensus
- Month-over-month headline: +0.6%, driven partly by a record September petrol price increase

This is the second data point in a row suggesting inflation has stopped falling. Hot CPI makes
it harder for the Fed to cut and keeps the door open to another hike on October 28.

**Oil:** Brent crude is trading near **$110 per barrel** this morning. High oil prices feed
directly into gasoline and transportation costs, which shows up in future CPI readings. This is
an inflationary headwind, not a tailwind.

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Expensive by historical standards.

- **Forward P/E: approximately 19x** earnings expected over the next 12 months (First Trust,
  data as of late September 2026). That is near the long-run average, but at a time when the
  risk-free rate is 5.28%. Historically, a 19x P/E was sustainable when 10-year yields were
  2–3%. At 5%, it implies a thin or negative equity risk premium.
- **Shiller CAPE: approximately 41x** (last confirmed reading, October 2026). The long-run
  average is about 17x. The market has been above CAPE 40 since mid-2026. This does not
  predict an imminent crash, but it does mean a buyer today is paying a premium that leaves
  little room for error.

The market being "not crashing" is not the same as the market being "fairly priced." We are
long-term investors and we do not panic about daily swings, but we also do not pay 41x
smoothed earnings when a Treasury bond yields 5.28%.
