they are worth buying.

## What is the VIX saying?

The VIX — the market's "fear gauge," measuring expected near-term volatility — was **15.31 on
October 2**, which is calm by historical standards. A VIX below 20 typically signals relaxed
investor sentiment, not panic or heightened caution. No October 8 reading was available, but
nothing in the available news suggests a sudden spike.

A low VIX combined with an expensive, rising market is a classic condition where complacency is
highest. It is worth noting that the VIX was similarly low in early 2000 and late 2021 — both
times stocks were near major peaks.

## Interest rates and the Fed

This is the most important factor in today's weather.

- The **10-year US Treasury yield hit 5.34% on Monday, October 5** — its highest level since
  2002, over 20 years ago. By October 6 it had pulled back slightly to ~5.27-5.28%, but the
  direction of travel is up.
- The **Federal Reserve raised its overnight rate by 25 basis points on September 16** to the
  3.75-4.00% range — restarting its rate-hiking cycle after a multi-year pause.
- Markets are pricing roughly a **25% chance of another hike on October 28** (the next FOMC
  meeting) and treat a 25bp hike by December as nearly certain.
- The **ISM Services Prices Paid index hit 74.0** on October 6 — its highest reading since July
  2022. This measures how fast service businesses are paying more for inputs. A reading above 70
  is historically associated with persistent inflation. It suggests the Fed has not yet finished
  its job.

What this means in plain terms: when the 10-year Treasury pays 5.28%, a risk-free government bond
earns a competitive return. Stocks at current prices no longer hold a wide advantage over bonds.
Every business we evaluate must now earn a higher return to justify the risk premium. That makes
us more demanding about the price we pay, not less.

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Very expensive by historical standards.

The **Shiller CAPE ratio** — which smooths 10 years of earnings against the current price — sits
near **41.1x**. The long-run average is about 17x. The only time it has been higher was the
brief dot-com peak of 44.2x in December 1999, which preceded a multi-year market decline of
roughly 50%. The market has been continuously above CAPE 40 since May 2026.

This does not predict a crash. Expensive markets can stay expensive for years. But it does mean:
a buyer at today's prices has a thin margin for error. The right response is patience and
discipline, not buying whatever is available at current prices.
