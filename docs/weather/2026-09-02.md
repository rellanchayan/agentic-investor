# Market Weather — Wednesday, September 2, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is technically healthy but fundamentally expensive, a live US-Iran conflict is pushing oil higher and stoking inflation risk, and three major macro events arrive over the next two weeks — all of which argue for demanding a bigger discount before buying anything.

---

## What the market is doing right now

**SPY** (our benchmark, the 500 largest US companies) closed yesterday at **$766.02**.

Its **200-day average** is **$711.10**, meaning SPY is roughly 7.7% above that line. When a market is above its 200-day average it is generally in an uptrend — the price is higher today than the average of the last ~200 trading days. That is a mild positive signal.

**Futures this morning** (pre-market): S&P 500 futures are down a slim **0.07%**, pointing to an essentially flat open. Prediction markets put the probability of a higher open at 53% — a coin flip. There is no strong directional signal from overnight trading.

## Is the market cheap or expensive?

**Expensive by most measures.**

- **Trailing P/E ~29.6x** — that means investors are paying about $29.63 for every $1 of earnings the S&P 500 companies produced over the last year. The long-run average is roughly 16-17x.
- **Forward P/E ~21.5x** — based on estimated future earnings. The Fed's own stability report flagged this as "close to the upper end of its historical range."
- **Shiller CAPE ~39x** — a long-term smoothed measure of valuation. At 39x, the market is priced at roughly 91% above its modern-era average. This is not a crash predictor, but it does mean future long-run returns are historically lower from this starting point.

In plain words: good companies are being priced for perfection. There is little margin for error.

## The geopolitical wildcard: US-Iran conflict

Since early 2026, the US and Iran have exchanged military strikes targeting energy infrastructure and shipping near the Strait of Hormuz — a narrow waterway through which about 20% of the world's oil flows. Escalation renewed in late August, and as of this morning:

- Crude oil is up roughly **2%** on fresh exchange of strikes.
- Analysts at BNEF warn oil could reach **$91/barrel** if the conflict prolongs.
- Higher oil = higher gasoline and transport costs = hotter inflation = the Fed has less room to cut rates.

This is a genuine background risk, not a headline to ignore. It is unlikely to derail the whole economy alone, but it adds noise and uncertainty to an already crowded calendar.

---

## What this means for us today

We are a long-term investor. We do not panic about daily moves. But we do calibrate how much discount we demand before buying a stock.

**Today's posture is cautious.** In practice that means:

1. **Demand a wider margin of safety** — we want to pay meaningfully less than our estimate of a company's true worth, not just a little less. The playbook uses an 18% margin of safety in cautious periods vs. a 12% normal bar.
2. **Prefer holding cash** — with a $1,000,000 account and $5,000 daily budget, being patient costs roughly 1 percentage point per month vs. SPY (as our own journal records). That is real, but it is less costly than buying an overpriced asset ahead of a bad data print.
3. **No thesis candidate clears the bar today** — AAPL is 67% above our fair-value estimate, MSFT is 32% above, and V is 28% above. None are close to a buy.

The weather is not a storm. It is an overcast day with a busy forecast. Keep the umbrella handy and wait for a better price.
