## What the numbers say

**SPY and the trend**

SPY closed yesterday at 774.00. Its 200-day average — the line that separates a general uptrend from a downtrend — sits at 701.93. SPY is 10.3% above that line. By this measure the market is in a solid uptrend and not in distress.

Premarket this morning, S&P 500 futures are up roughly 0.39%, adding to Tuesday's 1.79% gain that pushed the index to a fresh record close. Sentiment is positive and earnings have been broadly strong this season.

**Market valuation — is it cheap or expensive?**

Cheap means a fund like SPY holds companies at prices that leave room for gains. Expensive means prices already reflect a lot of optimism. Right now the market looks expensive by most measures:

- Trailing P/E (what investors pay for each $1 of actual earnings) is roughly 26–30. The long-run historical average is closer to 16–17.
- Forward P/E (using expected future earnings) is approximately 19.5 — more reasonable, but still above the norm.
- Shiller CAPE (a 10-year smoothed version that irons out cycles) is 36.4 — 76% above its modern-era average of 20.7. This is the most conservative measure, and it is flashing expensive.

None of this means the market will fall tomorrow, but it does mean we are paying a high price for a dollar of earnings relative to history. A long-term value buyer demands a bigger discount in this environment, not a smaller one.

**Interest rates and the Fed**

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate at 3.50%–3.75% at its July 28–29 meeting. That rate (set by the Fed) affects borrowing costs throughout the economy and has a direct bearing on what stocks are worth. Higher rates mean future earnings are worth less in today's dollars — one reason expensive markets are more vulnerable when rates stay high.

What is unusual is the vote: 9 to 3 to hold, the most divided Fed decision since 2016. Three regional Federal Reserve presidents dissented — some pushing for a rate increase. This suggests real internal disagreement about whether inflation is tamed enough. That is a source of uncertainty.

**Inflation**

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) — the broadest measure of how fast prices are rising — came in at 3.5% year-over-year for June 2026, down from 4.2% in May. The trend is improving, but 3.5% is still well above the Fed's 2% target. Energy costs remain elevated (though easing after US-Iran ceasefire progress). The Fed is unlikely to cut rates aggressively until CPI gets closer to 2%, which means rates stay higher for longer than the market might hope.

## What this means for us today

We hold $1,000,000 in cash and zero equity positions. No candidate meets our margin-of-safety test. The market is expensive by historical measures, the Fed leans hawkish with internal disagreement, and a major jobs report arrives in two days. This is a day to watch, not a day to buy.

Hold full cash. Continue monitoring prices daily. If any thesis candidate falls meaningfully toward its fair-value range in the coming days or weeks — particularly after a data-driven market pullback — we reassess. The discipline is in waiting for prices to come to us, not chasing them higher.

The playbook principle applies directly: "A missed buy costs nothing; an overpriced buy costs money."
