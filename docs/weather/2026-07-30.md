# Market Weather — 2026-07-30

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

One-sentence reason: Geopolitical escalation intensified overnight (US intercepted an Iranian attack on US troops; drones struck Saudi oil facilities), the S&P 500 is down 1.52% early, Brent crude is rising again to $92.65, and the FOMC delivered a hold with three hawkish dissenters favouring a rate hike — none of our thesis names trade at a required margin of safety.

## What the signals say

### SPY and the 200-day average
SPY is at approximately $738.10, above its 200-day average of $699.92 (+5.5%). The broad market remains technically in an uptrend. However, today's session is negative: the S&P 500 is down 1.52% (-112.63 points to 7,316). The bullish 200-day signal is intact, but the near-term price action is negative.

### FOMC — July 29 decision (yesterday)
The Federal Open Market Committee voted **9-3** to hold the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75%, its fifth consecutive hold. The dissent was hawkish: three regional bank presidents (Hammack/Cleveland, Kashkari/Minneapolis, Logan/Dallas) preferred a quarter-point rate hike at this meeting. The Fed cited solid economic activity and productivity growth, but elevated uncertainty from the Middle East conflict. The hawkish dissent signals the Fed is tilted toward higher-for-longer if inflation re-accelerates — which oil-driven inflation now risks.

### Oil and geopolitics — escalating
This is today's primary driver. Overnight, the US military intercepted what it described as a surprise Iranian attack targeting US troops stationed across the Middle East. Separately, Iran-backed militias launched drones at oil facilities in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Region. These events represent a meaningful escalation from last week (when Strait of Hormuz tankers were being targeted). Brent crude rose ~8% in the previous session and was at $92.65 this morning, up $3.12 from yesterday. Oil remains well below last week's $98 peak but is trending higher again. A further escalation could push it back toward or above $100.

The Strait of Hormuz situation has not resolved. Geopolitical risk is the single largest factor weighing on markets today and driving our CAUTIOUS posture.

### Earnings season — MSFT and V reported
**Microsoft (MSFT)** reported Q4 FY2026 results on July 29 after the close:
- EPS: $4.74 (beat of $0.41 vs $4.33 estimate, a ~9.5% beat)
- Revenue: $90.0 billion (slight beat of ~$633M)
- Azure grew 43%; Azure surpassed $100 billion in annual revenue for the first time
- Microsoft 365 Copilot reached 30 million paid seats
- Stock surged ~14.8% today to approximately $448–$452, its best single-day gain since October 2008

**Visa (V)** reported Q3 FY2026 results on July 28:
- Net revenue: $11.6 billion (+14% YoY)
- Non-GAAP EPS: $3.32 (beat by $0.03)
- GAAP EPS: $2.97 (+10% YoY)
- Payments volume: +10% on constant-dollar basis
- Visa repurchased shares at $330.71 average during the quarter — they see value in the stock below $354

Both companies reported strong quarters. However, as discussed in the Portfolio Decision, neither trades at a required discount to fair value.

### Market valuation context
The S&P 500 forward P/E remains elevated at approximately 20x. Earnings growth is strong — MSFT's results are exemplary — but valuations leave little room for error. In a CAUTIOUS environment with rising oil prices and hawkish Fed dissenters, a higher margin of safety is required before committing capital.

### Interest rates
The 10-year Treasury yield remains near recent highs (~4.64%). The hawkish FOMC dissent did not push it meaningfully higher, but the direction of risk is upward if inflation re-accelerates. Higher long rates reduce the present value of future earnings.
