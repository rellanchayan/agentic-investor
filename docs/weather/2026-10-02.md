---

## What these words mean (for new readers)

- **SPY**: a fund holding the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — "the market."
- **200-day average**: SPY's average price over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it means the market is in a general uptrend.
- **Shiller CAPE**: a measure of how expensive the stock market is relative to long-run earnings. The historical average is about 17. At 40.58, stocks cost roughly 2.4 times what history says is "normal." Only the dot-com bubble peak (44.19 in December 1999) has been higher in 150 years of data.
- **10-year Treasury yield**: the interest rate the US government pays to borrow for 10 years. At 5.18%, this is elevated. Higher yields mean you can earn more just by lending to the government — making stocks comparatively less attractive.
- **Nonfarm Payrolls (jobs report)**: how many net new jobs the US economy added. A miss well below forecast (as today) hints the economy is slowing.
- **Fed / FOMC**: the US central bank sets short-term interest rates. Its next meeting is October 27-28. A weaker jobs report reduces the odds of another hike.

## Why the market is rallying on bad economic news

Markets are interpreting the weak jobs print as making it less likely the Fed will raise rates again at its October 27-28 meeting. Market-implied probability of no rate change at that meeting is now 74%. If the Fed pauses or eventually cuts, that lowers the discount rate applied to future earnings — which in theory supports higher stock prices.

This is sometimes called a "bad news is good news" market. It is real in the short term. But it does not change what stocks are actually worth: CAPE at 40.58x is still the second-highest valuation in 150 years of history. A temporarily lower expected rate does not justify permanently elevated multiples.

## Coming up: FOMC meeting October 27-28

The next rate decision arrives in 3.5 weeks. The Fed raised rates by 25bp on September 16, bringing the target range to 3.75-4.00%. With today's weak jobs data and the earlier softer PCE reading (3.0% YoY core, below the 3.3% forecast), the market now leans toward a pause. The December meeting is the next likely decision point.

This is event risk. We will watch for any Fed communications (speeches, minutes) between now and then.
