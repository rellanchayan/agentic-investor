# Market Weather — 2026-07-27

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

One-sentence reason: The Iran military standoff has paused and oil has fallen sharply, but the FOMC meets tomorrow with a rate decision on Wednesday, MSFT and AAPL report earnings this week, and valuations at ~20x forward P/E remain above the historical average — too many near-term unknowns to relax our discount requirement.

---

## What the signals say

### SPY and the 200-day average
SPY was quoted at $737.29 this morning. Its 200-day moving average (the average closing price over the last ~200 trading days) is $698.86. SPY is 5.5% above that average, which means the broad market is in a healthy uptrend. This is the same picture as last week: the long-term trend is intact. On that one measure alone, the market looks fine.

### Futures this morning
US stock index futures are strongly positive before the open. S&P 500 futures are up approximately 0.8-1.0% and Nasdaq futures are up approximately 1.3-1.65%. Prediction markets put an 88% probability on an "Up" open for the S&P 500. The driver is clear: oil prices fell sharply over the weekend as the US and Iran paused their military conflict (see below). Risk appetite has returned sharply.

### Interest rates / the Fed
The Fed's current short-term interest rate is 3.50-3.75%. The FOMC meeting starts tomorrow (Tuesday July 28) and the rate decision comes Wednesday July 29 at 2:00 PM ET, followed by a press conference at 2:30 PM ET by Chair Kevin Warsh. The market currently prices roughly a 65% probability of no change — meaning about a 35% chance of a surprise move. This is not a Summary of Economic Projections meeting, so there will be no updated dot plot.

Higher rates make borrowing more expensive for companies and make bonds more attractive relative to stocks. With September rate hike odds now at 82%, the Fed's tone Wednesday will matter a great deal.

The 10-year Treasury yield is at approximately 4.63-4.64%, essentially unchanged from last week's two-month high. This level is still elevated and remains a headwind for stocks.

### Oil and geopolitics
This is the main change since last Wednesday. After 13 consecutive nights of US airstrikes against Iran that pushed Brent crude to approximately $98 per barrel, the US announced a suspension of the airstrike campaign over the weekend to allow space for diplomatic negotiations. Iran confirmed it would also halt its retaliatory strikes under an "attack-for-attack" framework as long as the US pause holds.

Brent crude fell roughly 7.6% to approximately $89.43 per barrel this morning. WTI crude is near $83.37/barrel. This is real and meaningful relief — the acute energy shock of the past two weeks has partially unwound.

The key word is "pause." There is no diplomatic agreement, no permanent ceasefire, and no guarantee the pause holds. The Strait of Hormuz remains a flashpoint. The situation improved materially but has not resolved.

### Market valuation
The S&P 500's forward price-to-earnings ratio has fallen below 20x for the first time in an extended period, reaching approximately 19.8x as of July 24. This is because earnings have been unusually strong: the S&P 500 is tracking toward back-to-back quarters of over 20% earnings growth. Strong earnings growth pulls the P/E ratio down even when prices rise. This is a genuine positive.

However, 20x (or 19.8x) is still well above the long-run historical average of roughly 15x. The market is not cheap by any objective measure. A careful long-term investor still demands a meaningful discount before buying.

### Earnings season
We are in peak Q2 earnings season. This week is exceptionally busy: Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta all report. MSFT and AAPL are both live thesis candidates — companies we have priced and have a written case for. We want to see their reported earnings before making any buy decision near current prices, because a big earnings miss (or a cautious forward guide) could move prices quickly in either direction.

## What this means for us today

Hold cash. The Iran news is genuinely good, and the strong futures this morning are welcome. But the decision to buy should not be made on a single morning's news when: (a) the FOMC is deciding Wednesday, (b) MSFT and AAPL both report earnings this week, and (c) none of our three thesis candidates are close to their buy thresholds in any case. The right move is to watch carefully, let the FOMC and earnings results land, and then reassess.

If MSFT falls sharply on earnings (say, below $333), it would cross the normal-posture threshold and we would revisit the posture. We are not there today.
