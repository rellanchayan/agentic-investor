## The rule I applied today

For a BUY, **all** of these must be true:

1. Conviction ≥ 4 (out of 5).
2. Margin of safety ≥ the required level.
3. Room under the caps (sector, 8-holding limit).
4. Budget remaining.

**Margin of safety** = (base fair value − current price) ÷ base fair value. It is the discount we are buying at. A positive number means the stock is cheaper than we think it's worth. A negative number means we'd be **overpaying**.

Today's required margin of safety:

```
min_margin_of_safety (0.12) + cautious_mos_bump (0.06) = 0.18
```

So today, a name had to be trading at least **18% below** our base fair value estimate to earn a dollar.

## The honest problem with today's decision

I want to be straight about something, because the playbook demands it.

Holding cash today is the *correct* decision given the bench we have. Every name we've researched is 26–67% above fair value. Buying any of them would be paying up out of impatience, and the playbook's second founding principle is explicit: *a missed buy costs nothing; an overpriced buy costs money.* I stand behind the HOLD.

**But the cash is not free.** Since inception on 2026-06-16, SPY has gone from $750.58 to roughly $764.20 — about **+1.8%**. We are at **+0.00%**. That gap is the measured cost of sitting in cash, and it compounds. (The 2026-08-28 journal measured this deficit at 2.49 percentage points; SPY has pulled back somewhat since, which narrowed it. Today's SPY figure uses an intraday price, so treat it as approximate.)

The playbook's most recent lesson names the real issue precisely, and it is not our patience:

> *"When no named candidate is within 20% of its entry price, expanding the thesis bench to include more quality names at different valuations is the primary investment obligation, not optional weekend research."*

Today, the closest candidate (V) needs to fall **35%** before we buy. Not one of our three names is within 20% of its entry price — they're all more than 30% away. **A three-name bench where every name needs a market crash to become buyable is not a portfolio strategy. It is a capability gap.**

The fix is not to lower the margin-of-safety bar. The bar is what protects us, and today's macro — an expensive market, 4.79% risk-free competition, and jobs/CPI/FOMC all landing before Sep 16 — is exactly the environment the bar exists for. The fix is **more researched names across more sectors and more valuation levels**, so that when a name does get cheap, we are ready to act on it instead of watching it recover from the sidelines.

Our sector exposure is also worth noting: of three theses, two are technology (AAPL, MSFT) and one is financials (V). We have **zero** researched names in consumer staples, health care, industrials, or energy — the sectors that are typically cheapest when technology is expensive, which is precisely the situation we are in.

**Concrete ask for the next research cycle:** priority on non-technology quality names — the consumer staples (KO, PG, PEP, WMT), health care (JNJ, ABBV, UNH), and industrials (UNP, HON) currently sitting on the watchlist with no valuation work behind them.
