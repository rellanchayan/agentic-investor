## What I did and why

Here is the whole argument in one table. "Margin of safety" is just how far below our estimate of fair
value the stock trades. A positive number means it's on sale. A negative number means we'd be
**overpaying**.

Prices are live bid/ask midpoints pulled from Alpaca this morning (MSFT 498.85/499.64, V 373.36/373.60,
AAPL 329.63/329.74). All three theses were checked and are **fresh and valid** — none are stale, none
are invalid. So this is not a "we don't know" day. We know exactly what these businesses are worth to
us, and the market is asking far more than that.

**The bar today.** Our normal rule is to buy only with at least a 12% margin of safety. The cautious

value. Instead, all three trade 26–69% *above* it. The gap isn't close — MSFT would have to fall 37.9%
to clear today's bar, Visa 34.8%, Apple 51.5%.

**A word on the cautious bump.** It's worth being honest that the bump didn't decide anything today.
Even under a normal posture at a 12% bar, all three names fail by enormous margins. The macro backdrop

**AAPL has a second, independent problem.** Its conviction is 3 out of 5, and our rule is to only buy

**On the other 26 tickers in the watch file** (GOOGL, NVDA, AVGO, JPM, COST, WMT, PG, KO, UNH, JNJ, XOM,

## The honest problem worth naming

This is the pattern our own playbook has flagged repeatedly, and I'm not going to paper over it: we have
been holding 100% cash while every named candidate sits 26–69% above its entry bar. As of 2026-08-28 the
measured cost of that inaction was a **2.49 percentage point deficit versus SPY**, roughly 1 point per
month at the pace observed. SPY is at $757.26, still above its 200-day average. The cash drag is real,
it compounds, and it does not get recovered.

**The fix is not to lower the bar.** Buying Microsoft at a 32% premium to fair value because we feel
behind is exactly how patient investors turn a quality strategy into an expensive one. The fix is the one
the playbook already identified as an *obligation*: **expand the thesis bench.** A bench of three names,
all deeply overvalued, is a capability gap — it structurally guarantees "hold cash" no matter how
diligent the morning process is. With 26 unresearched names on the watch list spanning staples,
health care, energy, and industrials — sectors that have not run like mega-cap tech — it is entirely
possible something there is fairly priced today.

That is research work, and it belongs to the weekly research cycle, not to a morning decision made with
live capital. **Flagging it as the highest-priority item for the next research run.** Today's answer
stands on its own merits regardless: at these prices, holding cash is the correct decision, not merely
the default one.
