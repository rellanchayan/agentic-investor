## 2. Market context — why the posture is cautious

Three things are true at the same time, and they pull in different directions:

**The trend is up.** SPY is at 771.08 versus its 200-day average of 706.71 — about **9.1% above**
its long-term trend line. Momentum-wise, this is a healthy market. That is the bullish part, and we
are not going to pretend it isn't there.

**But the price of the market is extreme.** The Shiller CAPE ratio (a smoothed, 10-year-average
earnings multiple used to judge whether the whole market is cheap or dear) sits at **41.2x**. That is
the **98.9th percentile of every month since 1881** — only 18 months in nearly 150 years have been
higher, and every one of them was in 1999–2000. The plain trailing P/E on the S&P 500 is ~27x against
a historical median around 18x. This does not tell us what happens next month. It does tell us that
buyers today are paying a historically extreme price for a dollar of earnings.

**And the "risk-free" alternative is now genuinely competitive.** The 30-year Treasury yields
**~5.20%**, after touching 5.33% yesterday — near a **19-year high**. Higher long rates matter to us
directly: they are the discount rate in every valuation. When you can earn 5.2% risk-free for 30
years, a stock at 30x earnings (a ~3.3% earnings yield) has to grow a great deal just to break even
against a government bond. High rates *lower* the fair value of exactly the kind of long-duration,
high-multiple compounders we like to own.

**The Fed is not coming to the rescue soon.** Fed funds are held at 3.50–3.75% after the July 29
meeting. Inflation is still above target — July CPI at **+3.4% headline / +2.5% core** year over
year. Notably, **three FOMC members wanted to *hike* at the July meeting**. Today's 2:00 PM ET
release of the July minutes could reveal more hawkish detail than the market expects. We are not
trading the minutes — we don't do that — but it is one more reason not to reach for a full-price
stock on a morning when the afternoon has a known binary event in it.

Elevated Middle East tensions are also pushing oil higher, which feeds back into inflation.

**What this means for us in practice:** the cautious posture raises our required discount from 12%
to **18% below base fair value**. That is not a prediction that stocks fall. It is a statement that
when the whole market is priced at the 99th percentile and rates are at 19-year highs, we want to be
paid more for taking equity risk.

for low-single-digit growth while a 30-year Treasury pays 5.2% is not an investment we can defend.

**Two independent reasons we cannot buy this today:** the margin of safety is deeply negative, *and*
conviction is **3**, below our buy threshold of 4. Even if the price halved tomorrow, the conviction

**Decision: no action. Not a buy at any price we expect to see soon.**

## 5. What would change our mind — and what to watch

**Things that would make us buy:**
- Any of the three trading at or below its cautious entry price in the table above.
- A September thesis refresh that materially *raises* fair value on genuinely better fundamentals —
  for example, evidence that Microsoft's $64.6B of AI capex is producing real, high-return revenue.
- The posture shifting from cautious back to normal (which lowers the required discount from 18% to
  12%), which would need a meaningfully less stretched market: a lower CAPE, cooler long rates, or
  inflation moving convincingly toward 2%.
- An Apple conviction upgrade from 3 to 4, which would require evidence on the AI/Services
  re-acceleration — not merely a lower price.

**Things that would make us more cautious still, or trigger a sell if we owned these:**
- *MSFT thesis breaks if:* Azure/Intelligent Cloud growth falls below 10% for two consecutive
  quarters; gross margin below 60% for two consecutive quarters; FCF negative for a full year;
  net-debt/EBITDA above 2.0x; ROIC below 15% sustained.
- *V thesis breaks if:* GAAP operating margin below 50% for two consecutive quarters; net revenue
  shrinking YoY for two consecutive quarters outside a recession; a regulatory ruling capping
  interchange in the US or EU expected to cut net revenue more than ~15%; net-debt/EBITDA above 2.0x.
- *AAPL thesis breaks if:* gross margin below 42% for two consecutive quarters; Services revenue
  growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters; revenue shrinking YoY for two straight
  fiscal years; net-debt/EBITDA above 1.5x.

We own none of these, so no sell trigger is live today. They are recorded so that the standard is
