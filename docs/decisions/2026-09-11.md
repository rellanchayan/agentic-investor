## Honest assessment of today's bench expansion

On 2026-08-26 this journal recorded a blunt criticism of itself: *"Requiring a 35-48% decline in all
three of our only ideas is not a strategy — it is a bench-coverage failure."* The playbook has carried
that lesson since 2026-08-19, and on 2026-08-28 it was given a price tag: roughly 1 percentage point
per month of cash drag.

Today we acted on it by adding **KO and JNJ**, taking the bench from 3 names to 5, and deliberately
reaching outside technology into consumer staples and health care.

**What genuinely improved.** The best available opportunity moved from *"V needs -34.2%"* to
*"KO needs -28.5%."* We now have names in three sectors instead of one-and-a-half, which means a
tech-led drawdown is no longer the only market event that could ever give us an entry. KO in
particular is a different *kind* of bet — a defensive staple with a binary legal catalyst — rather
than another expensive megacap compounder.

**What did not improve, and I should say so clearly.** A 28.5% required decline is still not a
plausible near-term entry. Adding two names that are 15% and 24% overvalued has not solved the
underlying problem; it has only made the gap slightly less absurd. And one of the two new names
(JNJ) arrived with conviction 3, which means it cannot be bought at *any* price until the talc
liability is bounded well enough to justify a 4. So today's expansion delivered **one** genuinely new
actionable candidate, not two.

**The uncomfortable truth.** Our screening universe is 29 tickers of large, famous, widely-owned
US megacaps, in a market at a 25.9x trailing P/E. It should not be surprising that all of them look
expensive — we are fishing in the most picked-over pond there is. The honest conclusion is that the
next bench expansion should not simply add more household names; it should deliberately look at
quality businesses that are currently *out of favour* for identifiable, temporary reasons. That is
the weekend research task, and I am naming it here so it is a commitment rather than an intention.

**A correction to the record, in our favour.** The playbook's 2026-08-28 lesson cited a 2.49pp cash
drag versus SPY. Measured today, SPY is **+1.78%** since our 2026-06-16 inception ($750.58 → $763.93)
against our 0.00%, so the deficit is now roughly **1.8pp**, not 2.5pp — the market pulled back in
early September and narrowed the gap. Meanwhile our max drawdown is 0.00% against SPY's -2.24%.
None of that makes holding cash *correct*; it just means the cost of patience has been smaller than
feared so far. Cash drag is real and I am not dismissing it. But the fix for cash drag is finding a
genuinely cheap wonderful business, not lowering the bar for an expensive one.

## FOMC risk note

**The FOMC decides on Wednesday, 2026-09-16 at 2:00 PM ET — three trading days from today.** Market
pricing puts roughly a **30-40% probability on a 25bp rate hike**, against a hold. That is unusually
high uncertainty: it is not a case where the market has already made up its mind and the meeting is a
formality.

This matters for us in a specific way, and I want to be precise rather than hand-wavy about it.
We are not trying to predict the decision — we cannot, and this journal does not claim to. The point
is narrower: **when a genuinely two-sided event sits three days away, the reward for waiting three
days is unusually high and the cost is unusually low.** If the Fed holds, our five names may get more
expensive and we will have missed nothing that was ever within 28% of our buy price. If the Fed
hikes into a market at 25.9x trailing earnings, we would very much rather be holding $1,000,000 of
cash than a position bought on Friday. The asymmetry points one way.
