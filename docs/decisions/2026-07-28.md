## Decision: HOLD everything. No orders written. $0 deployed.

This is a deliberate decision, not an oversight. We own nothing, and not one of the three names
with a valid, fresh thesis is anywhere near cheap enough to start a position. All three trade
*above* our base fair value — we would be paying a premium, not taking a discount. Every other
name on the watchlist has **no thesis**, and the rules are explicit: no thesis, no buy.

The temptation on a red-tape day like this is to "put some money to work" because the semis are
down 7.5% and it feels like a sale. It is not a sale for us. The names that are down are names we
have not researched. The names we have researched are not down. Buying the former would be
speculating on a headline; buying the latter would be overpaying. Neither is our job.

A missed buy costs nothing. An overpriced buy costs money. Patience is the position.

## Watchlist candidates worth researching next
This is the real gap in the portfolio, and it is worth stating plainly: we have only **three**
researched names, and all three are expensive. That is not a market problem, it is a coverage
problem. A wider bench of vetted businesses gives us more chances that *something* is on sale
when the market gets nervous. Priority for the next research cycle:

1. **GOOGL** ($328.29, communication) — the most obvious hole. A wide-moat, cash-generative
   business in a sector where we currently have zero coverage. Also gives us a non-technology
   sector slot.
2. **UNH** ($430.50, health care) — health care is completely uncovered, and UNH has been
   through a significant de-rating, which is exactly when a fresh valuation is most useful.
   Worth a careful look precisely because it is out of favor.
3. **COST or WMT** (consumer staples) — durable, recession-resistant compounders that would
   diversify us away from a portfolio that would otherwise be all mega-cap technology and
   payments. COST is likely expensive; WMT at $115.78 is worth pricing properly.
4. **AVGO / NVDA** (technology) — today's semiconductor selloff (NVDA -4.92%, AMD -8.31%) is the
   kind of event that eventually creates opportunity, but we cannot act on it because we have no
   thesis for either name. We should build one during a calm period so we are ready next time
   rather than researching in a panic. To be explicit: today's drop is **not** a reason to buy
   them. It is a reason to research them.

Note the sector limit: `max_per_sector` is 3, and MSFT and AAPL already occupy two technology
slots on the researched bench. Broadening into communication, health care, staples, and
industrials is more valuable to us right now than a fourth technology name.
