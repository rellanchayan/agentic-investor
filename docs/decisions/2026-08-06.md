### A note on NVDA, the one name that looks tempting

NVDA is the closest to our range, and it deserves an explicit rejection rather than a silent one,
because there is a seductive-but-wrong argument for buying it: *"$218.78 is below the high end of
fair value ($233.97), so it's still inside the band."*

That reasoning is backwards, and I want it on the record as rejected. The high end of a fair-value
range is not a buy signal — in our process it is a **trim** trigger for something we already own.
The buy test is measured against the **base** case ($183.83), and against that NVDA is 19% too
expensive. Buying at the top of a valuation band means every optimistic assumption has to come true
just for us to break even. That is not a margin of safety; it is the absence of one.

NVDA also fails independently on conviction: 3 out of 5, below our floor of 4. The thesis itself
explains the reservation honestly — heavy customer concentration in a handful of hyperscalers, and
a valuation resting on trailing earnings that may prove to be near a cycle peak. The business
quality is not in question (74% gross margin, 91% ROIC, a genuine CUDA moat). The price and our
certainty are. Two independent disqualifiers, either of which alone is enough.
