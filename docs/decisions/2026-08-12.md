## 2. Why no buy is warranted

Five independent reasons, any one of which alone is sufficient:

**1. All three eligible names fail the margin-of-safety test — badly.** Not by one or two percent
where you might argue about the model. The *best* of them is 21% above base fair value. To buy today
I would have to believe our valuation work is off by 20-55%, and if I believed that, the right move
would be to fix the theses, not to override them.

**2. Two of the three trade above even the *high* end of fair value.** MSFT +13.5%, V +3.9% above
their optimistic cases. Our own rules say a holding whose price rises above the high band is a
**trim** candidate. It would be incoherent to buy at a price where our rulebook tells us to sell.

**3. AAPL additionally fails the conviction gate.** Conviction 3 versus a threshold of 4. Price is
not even the binding constraint there.

**4. The posture is cautious for real reasons, not vibes.** Inflation at 3.4% is still 1.4 points
above the Fed's target. A 10-year at 4.7% mathematically compresses what any long-duration equity is
worth. A ~48% chance of a rate *hike* on Sep 16 is a genuine risk to multiples. And the market is
paying 26.4x trailing earnings while the historical norm is 18.5x. Meanwhile VIX at 14.9 and a
Fear-Greed reading of 61 (Greed) tell us nobody is scared — which is precisely when bargains do not
exist. Cheap prices come from fear, and there is none on offer today.

**5. Everything else is unbuyable by rule.** GOOGL, NVDA, AVGO, ADBE, CRM, MA, JPM, COST, WMT, PG,
KO, PEP, HD, MCD, NKE, UNH, JNJ, LLY, ABBV, CAT, HON, UNP, XOM, CVX, DIS and SPY have **no thesis**.
No thesis = no buy. That is a hard limit and I am not going to route around it by "just buying a
little SPY" — that would be an untheses'd purchase dressed up as prudence.

### The honest counter-argument, stated fairly

I should not pretend this decision is free. It is not.

We have now held **100% cash since 2026-06-16 — 57 calendar days — and have never executed a single
trade.** As of the most recent measured close (2026-08-10, per `state/runs/2026-08-10-summary.json`)
SPY had returned **+2.99%** since our 2026-06-16 inception while we returned **0.00%**. That is the
last figure actually computed — today's gap has not been measured yet and I will not estimate it. We
are trailing our benchmark by roughly three percentage points, and the reason is that we have been standing still. Our
Sharpe ratio is not even calculable because equity has never moved. A 0.00% max drawdown looks
excellent on paper but reflects inactivity, not resilience.

There is also a process failure in the record that I will not paper over: **twelve consecutive
trading days between 2026-07-24 and 2026-08-10 had no morning decision file.** We cannot say from
the record whether any candidate crossed its entry threshold during that window. Today's file breaks
that streak, but the gap is real and it is a genuine risk of missed entries.

So: is the discipline wrong, or is the *coverage* wrong?

My judgment is that the **discipline is right and the coverage is wrong.** The failure here is not
that we demand an 18% discount — that is exactly what a cautious posture in a 26x-earnings market
should demand. The failure is that we have built theses on only **three** names, all of them
large-cap technology-adjacent compounders (two are literally the same sector), all of them among the
most-loved and most-expensive stocks in the index. Of course they are expensive. We picked from the
part of the market where bargains are least likely to be found.

The fix is **more research breadth, not a lower bar.** Lowering the hurdle to force a trade today
would convert a temporary opportunity cost into a permanent capital loss. Widening the opportunity
set gives patience something to actually act on. I have listed specific candidates for the research
desk in section 5.

**What I am explicitly *not* recommending:** lowering `min_margin_of_safety`, dropping the cautious

goalposts to manufacture activity. The third would be an untheses'd buy that violates a hard rule,
and it would also mean buying the index at 26.4x trailing earnings — the very valuation we are
