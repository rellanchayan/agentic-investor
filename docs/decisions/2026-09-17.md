## The bar I had to clear today

Two gates, both from `state/config.json`, and I am not allowed to waive either:

1. **Conviction ≥ 4** out of 5 (`conviction_buy_threshold`).
2. **Margin of safety ≥ 18%** — the normal `min_margin_of_safety` of 12% **plus** the
   `cautious_mos_bump` of 6%, because today's posture is cautious.

"Margin of safety" is simply how far below our estimate of fair value a stock trades:
`(base fair value − price) ÷ base fair value`. A positive number means it's on sale. A **negative**
number means we would be **overpaying** from day one.

### The three names, one at a time

**Apple (AAPL) — $332.55, no buy.** A genuinely wide-moat business having a great year: 9-month FY2026
revenue $364.4B, up 16.2%, with Services at $30.7B in the June quarter. But at $332.55 it trades at
**38.1x trailing earnings** and a **2.79% free-cash-flow yield** — while cash in a money-market fund
yields about 4%. Our own thesis contains an explicit price-discipline rule: *"do not initiate or add
above $233; a first tranche is only justified below ~$210."* We are at $332.55. Conviction is also
only 3/5, below the threshold of 4. This is momentum supporting the price, not value. No buy.

**Microsoft (MSFT) — $497.58, no buy.** The best business on the list and the thesis says so plainly:
FY2026 gross margin 67.9%, operating margin 46.8%, ROIC ~31%, Azure guided to 44–45% growth. The
honest problem is capex. Cash capex of $115.9B is now **3.4x depreciation**, and free cash flow has
*fallen* year over year ($71.6B FY2025 → $67.0B FY2026). On earnings alone the stock is roughly fair
(27.7x trailing GAAP); on cash flow it is expensive. Our band deliberately blends both, which is why
conviction was cut to 3/5 — we cannot yet know whether that AI capex earns a return. When we can't
tell, we don't pay up. No buy.

**Visa (V) — $369.51, no buy — and this is the closest call.** Conviction 4/5 clears the first gate.
Near-duopoly network, 59.1% GAAP operating margin, minimal leverage. On **non-GAAP** earnings ($12.78
TTM) the same multiples give $307/$358/$409, which would make the stock roughly *fair*. So there is a
defensible case that Visa is merely fully priced rather than expensive. But "roughly fair" is not the
bar. The bar is an 18% discount, and on the conservative GAAP measure we are **27% above** base fair
value. A fairly-priced great business bought with a rate-hiking cycle just resumed is a fine way to
earn nothing for three years. **I will not waive the margin-of-safety requirement for a name simply
because I like the business.** No buy.

### Why not just buy something small — a $1,000 "starter"?

Because a missed buy costs nothing and an overpriced buy costs money. The daily budget is a **ceiling,
not a quota**. There is no penalty for ending the day flat, and deploying $5,000 into a stock 27% above
fair value would be buying to feel busy. Patience is a position, and today it is the one we hold.
