Apple is a genuinely wide-moat business: 46.9% gross margin in FY2025, $98.8B of free cash flow, a
~2.4B active-device installed base, and a share count shrinking from 15.408B to 15.004B diluted. None

price collapsed to our entry level, this thesis as written would not authorise a buy without a
conviction upgrade first. Two independent gates, both shut. The thesis itself names the level that
would interest us: a pullback into the **$200-$230** zone.

### MSFT — the best business on the bench, at the worst price
Microsoft is the highest-quality name we track: 68.8% gross margin, ~36% ROIC, a **net cash** balance
sheet, ~$71.6B FCF, and Azure past $75B growing 34%. Conviction 4 — it clears the quality bar

above base fair value and 17.3% above the high end**. Worth noting that the thesis was written on

### V — closest to qualifying, still not close

## The honest problem: this is a bench gap, not patience

I want to separate two things that look identical from the outside.

**Disciplined patience** is holding cash because good candidates are temporarily a bit expensive.
**A capability gap** is holding cash because we only track three names and all three are 29-64%
overvalued with no plausible path to our entry price.

Today is the second one, and the playbook already says so. The lesson dated **2026-08-19** reads:
*"A thesis bench of only three deeply overvalued names is not a valid position; it is a capability
gap. When no named candidate is within 20% of its entry price, expanding the thesis bench... is the
primary investment obligation, not optional weekend research."*

Nine days later the bench is still three names, and the gap has **widened** — MSFT has moved further
away, not closer. Meanwhile the context file lists **26 other tickers** across health care, energy,
consumer staples, industrials and communication services, every one of them marked *"NO THESIS —
research before any buy."* Some of those may well be cheap right now. **I cannot know, and I am not
permitted to guess**, because a name with no thesis cannot be bought — correctly so. NKE at $39.16,
WMT at $103.10, KO at $89.69, DIS at $107.70 and PG at $143.62 are names where the question is at
least worth asking; asking it is research work, not a morning-decision call, and I will not
pre-judge the answer here.

So the correct read on today's zero-trade outcome is: **the decision was right, and the position we
are in is not good enough.** Holding $1,000,000 in cash at a 0% drawdown while SPY sits 8.9% above
its 200-day average is not a costless victory — it is a real opportunity cost, and it is not being
paid down by watching the same three overvalued tickers each morning. The fix is not a looser
margin of safety. **Lowering the bar to make the bench work would be exactly the wrong lesson.** The
fix is more quality names under coverage, at a range of valuations, so that the discipline has
something it can actually say yes to.

**Action for the weekend research cycle (Saturday 2026-08-29):** expand the thesis bench beyond
technology and payments. Priority on sectors where a cautious, rate-sensitive, inflation-running-hot
tape may already have marked down quality — consumer staples, health care, energy — rather than
adding a fourth expensive mega-cap tech name to a bench that already has three.
