## Two things worth flagging for the record

**1. The cost of waiting is real and I am not hiding it.** The playbook's 2026-08-28 lesson put
a number on it: sustained inaction had already cost roughly 2.49 percentage points versus SPY,
at a pace of about 1 point per month. SPY sits at $766.99, 7.6% above its 200-day average, and
has kept climbing while we have held cash. That is a real, compounding cost.

The right response is *not* to lower the bar and buy MSFT 59% above its trigger. It is to widen
the bench. Our entire investable universe is three names, all of them 25-62% overvalued on our
own numbers. That is not patience — patience implies you have candidates that might get cheap.
It is a coverage gap. **Expanding the thesis bench to quality names trading at more reasonable
valuations is the highest-value work available right now**, and it sits with research, not with
me. I am escalating it rather than papering over it with a bad trade.

**2. Standing review obligations from today's named catalysts.** Per the 2026-07-30 playbook
lesson, naming a dated event in a decision file creates a commitment to show up for it:

- **Thursday, Sept 11 — August CPI.** A hot print with Brent near $98 could reprice both bonds
  and equities.
- **Wednesday, Sept 16 — FOMC decision and dot plot.** The single biggest scheduled mover in
  the next fortnight.
- **All three theses come due for refresh in that same window** — MSFT and V on Sept 15, AAPL on
  Sept 16. They should be refreshed on schedule, and if either macro event materially moves the
  business outlook, the fair-value estimates themselves need re-checking, not just the prices.

A sharp repricing around those dates is the most plausible path to a genuine entry. That is a
reason to be *ready*, not a reason to pre-position.
