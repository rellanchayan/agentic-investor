### MSFT — HOLD. Do not buy. **Thesis refresh required (HIGH PRIORITY).**

**What happened:** Microsoft reported **FY2026 Q4 on July 29 after the close**, and the stock gapped
from a $391.00 close to a $438.47 open — it now trades ~**$450.69** (bid $450.42 / ask $450.61),
about **+15%** overnight and +19% in a week. The results were genuinely strong:

- Q4 revenue **$90.0B, +18%**; FY2026 revenue **$331.8B, +18%**
- FY2026 diluted EPS **$17.95 GAAP** (+32%) / **$17.28 non-GAAP** (+22%)
- **Azure +43%** in Q4 and surpassed **$100B** of revenue for the full year
- Microsoft 365 Copilot passed **30 million paid seats**; commercial RPO **+84% to $678B**
  (Source: Microsoft IR, FY26 Q4 press release, July 29 2026)

**Why this makes the thesis stale.** Our thesis on file is `MSFT-2025Q4`, built on **FY2025** data
(EPS $13.64, FCF/share $9.59) with a base fair value of **$378.07**. An entire fiscal year has since
been reported. The `refresh_due` field says 2026-09-15 and the staleness checker therefore calls it
"fresh," but that is a calendar test, not a truth test: **the numbers underneath the fair value have
been superseded.** Per our rules, **no buy on a stale valuation.** This is queued for research.

**The important part — a beat does not automatically mean it got cheaper.** The tempting read is
"EPS jumped 32%, so fair value must jump too, so $450 is a bargain." I checked that, and it is wrong.
Our thesis values MSFT by **blending a P/E leg and a free-cash-flow-yield leg** (I reproduced the
method exactly against the thesis's own published FY2025 output — it returns $327.96 / $378.07 /
$437.32 to the cent, so the reconstruction is sound). Running the **same method** on FY2026 actuals:

| Leg | FY2025 (thesis) | FY2026 (actual) |
|---|---|---|
| EPS | $13.64 | $17.28 non-GAAP / $17.95 GAAP |
| Operating cash flow | $136.2B | **$182.9B (+34%)** |
| Capital expenditure | $64.6B | **$115.9B (+80%)** |
| **Free cash flow** | **$71.6B** | **$67.0B (-6.5%)** |
| **FCF per share** | **$9.59** | **$8.99 (-6.3%)** |

**Microsoft's free cash flow went DOWN this year**, because it spent **$115.9B** on property and
equipment ($35.8B in Q4 alone, versus $17.1B a year earlier) building AI capacity. So the P/E leg
soars while the cash-flow leg falls, and the blend lands at roughly:

- **low ~$370 | base ~$426 | high ~$491** (on non-GAAP EPS)
- **low ~$380 | base ~$437 | high ~$503** (on GAAP EPS, which includes a $3.2B Anthropic investment gain)

At **$450.69, MSFT is still 3–6% ABOVE base fair value**, i.e. a **negative** margin of safety.
We need **18%** today. Even on the most generous GAAP read, we would want MSFT below roughly
**$358 (18% MoS)** or **$385 (12% MoS)**. It is nowhere near. **No buy.**

**Thesis-break check — nothing broke.** Revenue +18% (trigger: two consecutive down years). Azure
+43% (trigger: sub-10% growth for two quarters). Gross margin **67.9%** FY2026 (trigger: below 60%).
FCF still strongly positive at $67B and the balance sheet is still net cash (trigger: negative FCF
year / net-debt-to-EBITDA above 2.0x). Conviction stays **4**.

**But flag this for research:** our own thesis listed the risk *"AI capex is exploding... if AI demand
disappoints, returns on that spend may be poor and FCF growth stalls."* FCF growth did not merely
stall — **it went negative** while the market paid up 15%. That is exactly the tension the refresh
must resolve: is $116B/yr of capex a growth investment that earns its return, or is it the reason
owner earnings are shrinking? Until that is answered with real work, we do not put money in.

**Budget used: $0.**
