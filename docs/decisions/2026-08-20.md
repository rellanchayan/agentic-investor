## What I did and why

We hold zero positions, so there is nothing to trim or sell — no thesis-break trigger can fire on
a position we do not own. The only live question this morning was whether to *start* a position,
and the answer was no for every name on the bench.

Here is the test a buy has to pass today, and how each candidate failed it.

Under our settings, a BUY needs **conviction ≥ 4** and a **margin of safety ≥ 12%**. Because
today's posture is **cautious**, we add the `cautious_mos_bump` of 6 points, so the real bar this
morning is a **margin of safety of 18% or better** — the price must be at least 18% *below* our
base estimate of what the business is worth.

Margin of safety = (base fair value − current price) ÷ base fair value.

| Name | Price now | Our base fair value | Margin of safety | Bar today | Conviction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AAPL | $316.60 | $195.00 | **−62.4%** | +18% | 3 | **HOLD — no buy** |

| V    | $367.18 | $296.91 | **−23.7%** | +18% | 4 | **HOLD — no buy** |

A *negative* margin of safety means the stock costs **more** than we think the business is worth.
We are not being offered a discount; we are being asked to pay a premium. Every one of these names
also trades above the **high** end of its fair-value range — AAPL is 37.7% above its $230 high,
MSFT 9.9% above its $437.32 high, V 6.1% above its $345.97 high. That is the zone where we *trim*
a holding, not where we start one.

AAPL fails twice over: its conviction is **3**, below our threshold of 4, so it could not be bought
today at any price. That is by design — the thesis itself names valuation as "the dominant risk"
and lists a pullback into the $200–$230 zone as the catalyst that would make it interesting.

**Budget used: $0 of $5,000.** The unspent budget does not roll over, and that is fine. A missed
buy costs nothing; an overpriced buy costs money.

### Why not stretch the rules "just a little"?

Because the whole point of writing the rules down in calm times is to follow them in tempting ones.
We have $1,000,000 in cash and zero positions, and the pull to "put money to work" is real. But the
gap here is not marginal — MSFT would have to fall **35.5%** and V **33.7%** to reach a buyable
price. No sensible tweak to a threshold bridges a one-third overvaluation. Shaving the bar to force
a trade into an expensive market is precisely the mistake the cautious posture exists to prevent.

### Risks of doing nothing (stated honestly)

Holding 100% cash is not a risk-free position. If the market keeps climbing, we lag SPY, and we are
measured against SPY. That is a genuine cost and I am not pretending otherwise. The counter is that
our fair-value estimates are filing-backed and conservative, and buying a wonderful business at a
30%+ premium locks in years of poor forward returns even if the business performs exactly as
expected. We accept tracking error in exchange for not overpaying.
