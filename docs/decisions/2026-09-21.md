## What I did and why

That is a decision, not an oversight, and here is the arithmetic behind it.

A buy needs to clear **two independent gates**:

1. **Conviction ≥ 4** (on a 1–5 scale) — am I sure enough about this business?
2. **Margin of safety ≥ 18%** — is the price at least 18% below what I think the business is worth?
   (The normal bar is 12%; the cautious posture adds 6 points, because when the whole market is priced
   near a 145-year high I want to be paid more for the risk of being wrong.)

Margin of safety = (base fair value − current price) ÷ base fair value. A **negative** number means the
stock costs **more** than I think it's worth.

## Holding (no action) — and the honest reason for each

We own nothing, so "holding" here means "keeping cash instead of buying." One line each:

- **AAPL** — Genuinely wide moat (ecosystem switching costs, silicon integration, 48.7% TTM gross margin).
  But at $336.56 it trades at 38x trailing earnings and a 2.79% free-cash-flow yield, when cash itself
  yields ~4%. Conviction 3, not 4. Thesis is fresh (refresh due Oct 17) — the business is fine, the
  *price* is the problem.
- **MSFT** — Best enterprise franchise we track; Azure still growing 44–45%. My valuation is deliberately
  humble and wide because my two methods openly disagree: on earnings alone MSFT is roughly fair value,
  but free cash flow has fallen year-over-year as AI capex hit 3.4x depreciation. Whether that capex earns
  a return is **not yet knowable**, so I won't pay up for the assumption that it does. Conviction 3.
- **GOOGL** — Extraordinary business ($514B cloud backlog, Search +17%), but the widest overvaluation on
  our bench. I stripped out $135.8B of *paper* gains (the Anthropic and SpaceX stake mark-ups) because
  those aren't operating profit. Every way I cut it — P/E only, blended, or stress case — it's expensive.
  I am **not** saying the market is wrong; the market is paying for AI optionality I'm choosing not to
  underwrite. Conviction 3.
- **V** — Our highest-conviction name (4) and a near-duopoly with 59.1% operating margins. Worth noting
  honestly: on *adjusted* (non-GAAP) earnings V is roughly fair value at ~$358. I deliberately headline
  the stricter GAAP number because it's harder to flatter. Either way, a 33% discount from here is what
  I'd need, and I'm not getting it today.
- **NKE** — Conviction 2, and that's the disqualifier, not the price. The brand is being genuinely
  penetrated right now: revenue −3.2% annually over three years, Greater China down eight consecutive
  quarters, Digital −12%, Converse −31%, with On and HOKA taking shelf space. That's why I rate the moat
  **narrow**, not wide. A shrinking share is not a moat. This is a watch-only name until FY2027 shows
  stabilisation — I will not buy a deteriorating business just because it's the cheapest thing on the list.

## The uncomfortable truth I need to record

Our return since inception is **0.0%**; SPY's is **+2.45%**. We are **2.45 percentage points behind our
benchmark**, entirely because we hold 100% cash. That cost is real, it compounds at roughly 1 point per
month at SPY's recent pace, and it is not recoverable.

I am **not** going to fix that by lowering the bar — buying an overpriced wide-moat business is still
buying an overpriced business, and a missed buy costs nothing while an overpriced buy costs money.

But I will name the actual bottleneck honestly: **our thesis bench is too small.** Five researched names,
all of them 9–66% above fair value, in one of the most expensive markets on record. When no candidate is
within 20% of its entry price, the problem isn't discipline — it's **coverage**. Cash-and-wait is the
right answer *given this bench*, but this bench is a capability gap, not a strategy.

**The obligation this creates:** the next weekly research cycle should add quality names in the
under-covered sectors where we have prices but no theses — health care (UNH $385.88, JNJ $266.78,
ABBV $264.77), consumer staples (PG $147.68, KO $87.97, WMT $107.18), and energy (XOM $160.05,
CVX $206.00). These are traditionally cheaper, less AI-narrative-driven corners of the market and are
where a genuine 18% margin of safety is most plausible today. **None of them can be bought until a
real thesis exists** — no thesis, no buy, and I won't cut that corner.
