## What the words mean (quick glossary)

- **SPY**: a fund holding the 500 largest US companies — our benchmark for "the market."
- **200-day average**: the average SPY closing price over roughly the last 200 trading days.
  When today's price is above it, the market is in an uptrend; below it, a downtrend.
- **CAPE (Shiller P/E)**: a valuation measure that smooths earnings over 10 years to reduce
  noise. The long-run average is about 17. Higher means more expensive. Near 40 is historically
  extreme.
- **10-year Treasury yield**: the interest rate on a US government bond maturing in 10 years.
  When this rate is high, money earns decent returns doing nothing — raising the bar for stocks.
- **FOMC / the Fed**: the US central bank committee that sets short-term interest rates. A
  rate "hike" raises borrowing costs and tends to pressure stock prices down.
- **CPI**: Consumer Price Index, the main inflation measure. Hot CPI = the Fed likely keeps
  rates high or raises them further.

## Where things stand today

**The trend.** SPY closed at 773.99, which is 7.2% above its 200-day average of 721.95.
That is a healthy uptrend. But an uptrend at extreme valuation is not the same as cheap.
It means the market has not pulled back to attractive levels.

**Valuation.** The Shiller CAPE ratio is approximately 41.1. The long-run average is 17.
The only time it has been higher was the brief dot-com peak of 44.2 in December 1999,
which preceded a multi-year bear market. The market has been continuously above CAPE 40
since May 2026 — there is no recent precedent for this.

**Interest rates.** The Federal Reserve restarted its rate-hiking cycle on September 16,
raising the overnight rate to 3.75-4.00%. The 10-year Treasury yield is at approximately
4.70% this morning (slightly down from a recent range of 4.95-5.01%). High rates mean
even safe government bonds earn a real return, making it harder for overpriced stocks to
compete for investor capital.

**This morning's futures.** S&P 500 futures are pointing up about +0.35% before the open
and Nasdaq futures +0.69%. This is mild and not unusual — not a crisis signal, but also
not a meaningful buying opportunity.
