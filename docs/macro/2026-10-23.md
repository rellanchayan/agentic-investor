# Market Weather: Friday, October 23, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market remains expensive (~28-29x trailing earnings), interest rates are elevated and still rising (Fed funds at 3.75-4.00%, 10-year Treasury near 5%), inflation is hot (September CPI +3.7% year-over-year), and a Fed decision plus major tech earnings land this coming Wednesday — a high-uncertainty week that demands a larger margin of safety before buying anything.

---

## What the numbers say

**SPY (our benchmark):** $761.65, which is 4.8% above its 200-day moving average of $726.99. The 200-day average is the average closing price over roughly the last 200 trading days. Being above it means the market is in a general uptrend — not a sign of panic. But "above the 200-day" also means prices are elevated relative to recent history, not cheap.

**Valuation:** The S&P 500's trailing price-to-earnings ratio was about 28.71x as of July 2026 and is likely in the 28-30x range now. A P/E of 29x means investors are paying $29 for every $1 of earnings a company produces. Historically the long-run average is around 16-17x. Expensive markets require higher discounts before buying — our cautious posture enforces this by requiring an 18% margin of safety and conviction of at least 4.

**Interest rates:** The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate to 3.75-4.00% in September 2026 — its first hike since 2023, driven by inflation. The 10-year US Treasury yield crossed 5% in mid-September and is estimated to still be in that vicinity (around 5.2-5.3% based on prior journal data; could not be confirmed precisely for today). When risk-free government bonds yield 5%+, stock valuations face real competition. A stock with a P/E of 29x earns roughly 3.4% — below the risk-free rate.

**Inflation:** September CPI (released October 14-15) came in at +3.7% year-over-year and +0.6% month-over-month, both above consensus. This is well above the Fed's 2% target and supports the case for rates staying high or going higher.

**Futures this morning:** No reliable pre-market futures data found for October 23, 2026. This is noted as a data gap.

Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon all report this week; their guidance and AI spending plans will set the tone for the rest of earnings season.

## What this means for us today

We hold cash. No candidate in our bench is within striking distance of a cautious entry price. The closest is V (Visa), our only conviction-4 name, at $413.94 vs. a cautious entry of roughly $240 — still about 72% above where we would buy. The FOMC meeting Wednesday creates real potential for market movement in either direction; we have no reason to rush. Our caution is not fear — it is arithmetic. Overpaying for a good business today locks in a lower long-term return. We wait, we watch, and we keep the powder dry.

The next concrete obligation is to update the stale theses for AAPL, MSFT, and V (all marked overdue as of October 17). Completing those refreshes is the one action that could shorten the path to a first buy — but only if prices fall to meet fair value, not if we raise our estimates to meet the price.

## Glossary (quick reference)

- **SPY**: A fund holding the 500 largest US companies. Our benchmark.
- **200-day average**: Average SPY price over ~200 trading days. Above it = general uptrend.
- **Fed / interest rates**: Higher rates make borrowing costlier, competing with stocks for investor money.
- **Inflation / CPI**: How fast prices rise. Hot inflation keeps rates high.
- **Earnings season**: The quarterly period when companies report profits. More news, more volatility.
- **Margin of safety**: The discount we require vs. our estimated fair value before buying.
