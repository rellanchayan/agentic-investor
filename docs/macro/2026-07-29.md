# Market Weather — 2026-07-29

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** Three overlapping risks hit today at once -- a Fed rate decision with a meaningful hike probability, a fresh Iran missile attack lifting oil again, and peak big-tech earnings volatility -- making this the wrong time to lower our standards on price.

---

## What each term means (plain English)

- **SPY** is a fund that holds the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark -- the market.
- **200-day average** is the average SPY closing price over roughly the last 200 trading days. When SPY is above it, the long-run trend is up. When below it, the trend is down.
- **Interest rates / the Fed** -- the US central bank sets short-term borrowing costs. Higher rates make cash and bonds more attractive relative to stocks, and tend to push stock prices down.
- **Inflation (CPI/PCE)** -- how fast prices are rising. Hot inflation keeps the Fed from cutting rates, which keeps pressure on stock valuations.
- **Earnings season** -- the weeks each quarter when companies report profits. This week is peak earnings season for the largest US tech companies.

## What Changed Since July 23

The previous posture was also CAUTIOUS. Between then and now:

- **SPY dipped slightly** from $739.05 to $734.22 -- a 0.7% slide over six days. Not dramatic, but no cheap-up-signal either.
- **Iran re-escalated overnight.** Iran launched ballistic missiles at US forces in the Middle East. Oil spiked 3-4% this morning. Earlier in July, Brent hit $100/bbl before talks brought it down toward $87-90. It is climbing again.
- **The FOMC meeting is concluding today.** The decision is announced at 2:00 PM ET. Markets are pricing roughly a 70% chance the Fed holds rates steady, but a 30% chance of a hike. A 30% probability is not a tail risk -- it is a real possibility that would surprise markets and push yields and stocks sharply.

## What This Means for Us Today

Hold all cash. Do not lower the margin-of-safety bar because of FOMO or because SPY is in an uptrend. The FOMC decision at 2pm ET alone is enough reason to be patient today -- we would rather miss a 0.2% morning gain than buy a good company at a bad price right before a surprise hike announcement. The Iran escalation adds another layer of uncertainty to energy prices and risk sentiment. This is precisely the situation the playbook is designed for: a market that is not cheap, loaded with near-term binary events, and none of our names on sale. The right move is to wait.
