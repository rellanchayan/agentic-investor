## The Big Picture in Plain English

**SPY and the 200-day average.** SPY — the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies and our benchmark — closed at 765.14. Its 200-day average (the average of the last ~200 closing prices) is 708.87. When the current price is above that average, the market is in a general uptrend; today it is about 8% above it. That is a positive technical signal. However, "in an uptrend" does not mean "cheap" or "safe" — it just means the overall trend is up. The market can be in an uptrend and also be expensive and event-prone at the same time, which is exactly where we are today.

**Valuations: the market is expensive.** The S&P 500's trailing price-to-earnings ratio (what investors pay for each dollar of company profits) is approximately 26-29x. The long-run historical median is roughly 18x. That means investors are paying 35-60% more than the historical average for every dollar of earnings. A related measure, the Shiller CAPE (which smooths earnings over 10 years to avoid short-term distortions), stands around 39x — also elevated. Expensive markets are not guaranteed to fall, but they do leave less room for error, and when something goes wrong the drop tends to be larger. We demand a bigger discount (a larger margin of safety) before buying into an expensive market.

**Interest rates.** The US 10-year Treasury yield sits around 4.7%. This is meaningful because when investors can earn ~4.7% risk-free from government bonds, they need a good reason to take on stock market risk. Higher yields make future company profits worth less in today's money (they are "discounted" at a higher rate), which pushes fair values down. The Fed's next rate decision is not until September 16 — no cut or hike today — but what the Fed says this week at Jackson Hole will shape expectations.
