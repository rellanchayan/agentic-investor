## What SPY is doing

SPY, the fund that tracks the 500 largest US companies and our benchmark, closed yesterday at **770.58**. Its 200-day average (the average price over roughly the last 10 months of trading) is **728.11**. SPY is about **5.8% above** that average, which means the overall market is in an uptrend — not a panic, not a crash.

That is a mild positive signal, but it does not mean stocks are cheap. The trend says the direction of travel has been up. Valuation is a separate question.

---

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Two different measures give different answers:

- **Forward P/E (looking ahead 12 months): approximately 19-20x.** This is right around the market's five-year average of ~19.9x. By this measure, the market looks roughly fairly priced, because corporate earnings have grown fast enough (up ~29-39% year-over-year in recent quarters) to keep the multiple in check.

- **CAPE / Shiller P/E (looking back 10 years of inflation-adjusted earnings): approximately 38-42x.** This is near an all-time record, second only to the 2000 dot-com peak. When this number is this high, the market has historically delivered lower long-run returns going forward. This is the "big picture expensive" warning.

The Fed's own financial stability reports have flagged that valuations are near the upper end of their historical range.

**Bottom line on valuation:** Not a crash signal, but not a buy-everything signal either. Long-run buyers should demand a real discount before paying these prices.

### What is the Fed expected to do today?

Prediction markets put about an **83% probability on a hold** — meaning the Fed keeps the federal funds rate where it is at **3.50-3.75%**. The logic: September CPI came in above expectations (approximately 3.6% year-over-year, above the 2% target), and the labor market has been resilient. Fed officials have recently signaled no urgency to cut. A surprise cut is roughly a 4% probability.

A hold is not a bad outcome for stocks, but markets can still move on the wording of the statement, on any hints about December, or on the press conference tone. Scheduled events like this create short-term uncertainty.

## Inflation update

The most recent CPI (released October 14 for September data) came in **above expectations at approximately 3.6% year-over-year**. That is well above the Fed's 2% target, which is why rate cuts are not happening now. The next CPI is November 10. The PCE inflation number tomorrow (October 29) will be the more closely watched print this week.

## What this means for us today

We are long-term investors. We do not panic about single-day moves. But we do pay attention to the environment when we are deciding whether to buy.

Today's posture is **cautious**, which means: if a candidate is near its buy threshold, we demand a somewhat wider margin of safety before pulling the trigger. We prefer to hold cash than to buy at full price on a day where two large scheduled events (FOMC today, GDP + PCE tomorrow) could move everything materially.

None are close to a buy trigger at today's prices. So today's posture is mainly a reminder: if the market fell sharply this afternoon on a surprise from the Fed, we would still want confirmation and a real discount before acting — not a rushed entry.
