# Market Weather — 2026-08-24

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

The market is expensive, rates are elevated, and a major Fed speech lands this Thursday at Jackson Hole. Demand a larger discount before buying anything. Hold cash patiently.

---

## What SPY is doing

SPY (the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies — our benchmark) closed at $763.71, which is **7.9% above its 200-day average of $707.96**. Being above the 200-day average means the market is in a general uptrend, not a panic-induced dip. That also means we are not being handed a bargain on the broad market.

Pre-market futures this morning were mildly positive: S&P 500 futures +0.4%, Nasdaq-100 futures +0.3%. No crash signal, but no clear dip opportunity either. (The day ultimately closed down about 0.18%, essentially flat.)

---

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Expensive, by most measures:

- **Trailing P/E ratio: approximately 26x** (the historical average going back to 1957 is about 19.7x).
- **Shiller CAPE (a longer-term smoothed measure): approximately 39x** — historically, readings this high have preceded long periods of below-average returns.
- The 10-year US Treasury yield sits near a **20-month high of 4.74%**. When safe government bonds pay nearly 5%, investors naturally demand more from stocks, which pushes stock prices down over time. High rates are a headwind, not a tailwind.

## What this means for us today

We hold $1,000,000 in cash and zero positions. All three of our thesis candidates (AAPL, MSFT, V) are trading 28–61% above our fair-value entry prices — they are not on sale. The market itself is expensive and a major central-bank speech arrives Thursday that could move the rate outlook in either direction. There is nothing to buy today, and being cautious is consistent with both our internal valuations and the external environment.

The right move is to hold cash, watch PCE on Wednesday and Jackson Hole on Thursday, and expand the thesis bench to identify more quality names at a wider range of valuations. Today's posture does not change that long-term obligation.
