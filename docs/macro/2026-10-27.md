# Market Weather — Tuesday, October 27, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The Federal Reserve's two-day meeting is happening right now with the rate decision tomorrow afternoon, Microsoft and Alphabet report earnings tomorrow after the close, and PCE inflation plus GDP land Thursday — four potential market-movers in 48 hours on top of an already expensive market make a larger-than-normal discount mandatory before buying anything.

---

## Background: what these terms mean

- **SPY** is the fund that tracks the 500 largest US companies. It is the benchmark we measure ourselves against.
- **200-day average** is the average SPY closing price over roughly the last 200 trading days. When SPY is above this line, the market is in a general uptrend.
- **The Fed / interest rates** — the US central bank (Federal Reserve) sets short-term borrowing rates. Higher rates make borrowing costlier, which tends to push stock prices down.
- **CPI / PCE / inflation** — how fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation can push the Fed to raise rates, which spooks markets.
- **Earnings season** — the stretch each quarter when companies report their profits. Markets can move sharply on surprises.

## Where things stand today

SPY is at **$767.95**, which is **5.5% above its 200-day average of $727.73**. The market is in an uptrend. That is not a panic environment — stocks are not crashing — but it also means there has been no pullback to create a margin of safety. We are paying a premium to own the index.

### This morning's open

Markets opened modestly higher: the S&P 500 was up approximately **+0.30%** and the Nasdaq up approximately **+0.45%** at the open. The Dow was roughly flat to slightly lower. Easing oil prices (tied to some de-escalation in the Iran conflict) and anticipation of major earnings this week supported the open. The calm open does not change the big-picture risk — the events that could move things are coming in the next 48 hours.

### The huge week: four events in 48 hours

**Wednesday, October 28:**
- **FOMC interest rate decision at 2:00 PM ET** — the Fed announces whether it holds, raises, or cuts the current 3.75-4.00% target rate. Press conference follows at 2:30 PM ET. This is a Day 1 meeting today; the decision lands tomorrow afternoon. The widely expected outcome is a **hold** (roughly 87% probability in professional forecaster surveys), but an October hike is not impossible (~13-14% probability). Even a "hold" with a hawkish statement — suggesting future hikes — can push long-term yields up and stock prices down.
- **Microsoft (MSFT) earnings after the close** — options markets are pricing a move of approximately +/-6% around the report. MSFT is roughly 7% of SPY.
- **Alphabet (GOOGL) earnings after the close** — options markets are pricing a move of approximately +/-7.3%. GOOGL is roughly 4-5% of SPY. Combined, MSFT and GOOGL are about 12% of the index. A surprise in either direction — good or bad — ripples through SPY.

**Thursday, October 29:**
- **Q3 GDP (advance estimate) at 8:30 AM ET** — the first look at how fast the US economy grew last quarter.
- **September PCE inflation at 8:30 AM ET** — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. If this is hot like the September CPI was (+3.7% year-over-year), it raises the odds of a November hike.
- **Amazon (AMZN) earnings after the close.**

**Later:** Apple (AAPL) earnings Monday November 2 after the close.

### Valuation: expensive

- **Shiller CAPE** (a long-run smoothed P/E): approximately **41.6** — near all-time record territory, last seen around the dot-com bubble peak in 1999.
- **Trailing P/E:** approximately **28.7x** — well above the historical average.
- **Forward P/E:** approximately **20x** — more moderate, but relies on earnings growth coming through.
- **The Treasury yield problem:** The 10-year Treasury yield is approximately **5.28%**. The S&P 500's earnings yield (what you earn per dollar invested) is roughly 3.5%. That means risk-free government bonds pay more than the average stock's earnings yield. This is an unusual and unfavorable environment for paying full price for equities. Our fair-value models already account for this discount rate, which is why our entry prices sit far below current market prices.

The Fed raised rates by 25 basis points to 3.75-4.00% on September 16. Whether they hike again tomorrow is the single most important question this week. The September PCE print Thursday will also matter for November expectations.

## What this means for us today

**Hold cash. Do not buy.** The cautious posture means we require a larger-than-usual discount — a margin of safety of at least 18% below fair value — before entering any position. Not a single candidate comes close to that bar even on normal terms, let alone the wider cautious threshold. Paying a 30-50% premium over fair value the day before four major market-moving events is not disciplined investing.

The one productive action available today is **thesis refreshes**: AAPL, MSFT, and V are 10 days past their scheduled refresh date. Updating those in calm morning hours — not in reaction to tomorrow's post-earnings price swings — is exactly the kind of preparation the playbook calls for ("do the work in calm markets so that the only remaining gate is price").
