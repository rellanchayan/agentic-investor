## Key terms (for new readers)

- **SPY**: a fund holding the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — "the market."
- **200-day average**: the average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. If SPY is
  above this line, the market is in an uptrend (prices have generally been rising).
- **Shiller CAPE / P/E ratio**: how expensive the whole market is relative to earnings. A high
  number means stocks are pricey; a low number means stocks are cheap.
- **30-year Treasury yield**: the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 30 years.
  When this rises, it competes with stocks for investors' money and tends to push stock prices down.
- **FOMC minutes**: written notes from the Fed's policy meetings. Released three weeks after the
  meeting, they reveal how much disagreement exists and what the Fed was thinking.

## The big event today: FOMC minutes at 2:00 PM ET

At 2:00 PM ET this afternoon, the Federal Reserve (the Fed) releases the written minutes from its
July 28-29 policy meeting. At that meeting, the Fed held its short-term interest rate steady at
3.50%–3.75%. However, three of the twelve voting members wanted to raise rates. That is a notable
split — the written minutes will reveal what arguments those dissenters made and how close other
members were to agreeing.

Markets can move sharply when the minutes come out if traders read them as more hawkish (inclined
to raise rates) or more dovish (inclined to cut) than expected.

**Why it matters for us:** Higher rates make borrowing costlier and reduce what people are willing
to pay for stocks. If the minutes signal that more hikes are possible, that is a headwind for our
candidates.

## Rates and inflation: the longer-term picture

The 30-year US Treasury bond yield hit 5.33% on Monday August 18 — its highest level in nearly
19 years (since mid-2007). It eased slightly to ~5.20% today. This is significant: a 5.20%
risk-free return from a government bond is real competition for stocks, especially expensive ones.

The most recent inflation reading (July 2026 CPI, released August 12) showed prices rising 3.4%

ran at 2.5% annually. Both are above the Fed's 2% goal. Middle East tensions are keeping oil

The next CPI report (August data) comes on September 11, 2026. The next Fed interest-rate decision

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Expensive — by a wide margin.

- **Trailing P/E ratio**: approximately 27x. The historical median is about 18x, and the normal
  range is roughly 21x–29x. We are in the upper half of the normal range by this measure, but only
  just.
- **Shiller CAPE** (a more reliable long-run measure): 41.2x. This is the 98.9th percentile of
  all months since 1881. Only 18 months in history have been pricier — all of them in the
  1999–2000 dot-com bubble peak. This is the strongest "expensive" signal we have.

A market priced this high is not guaranteed to fall. But history says that buying at the 99th
percentile of valuation produces much lower returns over the following decade than buying at normal
valuations. As long-term investors, this matters.

Sources: [GuruFocus — S&P 500 P/E August 2026](https://www.gurufocus.com/economic_indicators/57/sp-500-pe-ratio) | [Shiller CAPE — thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/shiller-cape)
