## What SPY is doing

SPY (the fund that tracks the 500 largest US companies and our benchmark) is at **768.30**. Its **200-day average** is **728.49**. That is a mild positive signal for the long-term trend, but it says nothing about whether stocks are cheap or expensive. It just tells us the direction of travel.

---

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

Two different lenses:

- **Forward P/E (expected earnings 12 months out): approximately 19-21x.** This is modestly above the long-run historical average of around 16-18x. Markets have been supported by strong earnings growth — S&P 500 earnings are expected to grow roughly 24-30% year-over-year in Q3 2026 — which keeps the multiple from looking alarming.

- **CAPE / Shiller P/E (10-year inflation-adjusted earnings): approximately 38-42x.** This is near an all-time record, second only to the dot-com peak in 2000. When this number is this high historically, forward returns over the next 10 years have tended to be below average. This is the "expensive by long-run standards" warning.

**Bottom line on valuation:** Earnings growth is keeping the forward multiple manageable, but the long-run CAPE is a reminder that the market is priced for continued excellence. A patient buyer should still demand a real discount.

### What did the Fed do yesterday?

The FOMC finished its two-day meeting on October 28. No confirmed announcement text is available in this morning's web search. Based on pre-meeting data, markets had priced approximately an 83% chance of a hold (no change) and roughly a 17% chance of a quarter-point hike. If the hold scenario played out, the current federal funds rate target remains at **3.50-3.75%** (or possibly 3.75-4.00% if a September hike that appears in some sources is confirmed). Either way, rates are meaningfully above zero and act as a moderate headwind to stock valuations. No cut is expected anytime soon given inflation running near 3.6% year-over-year against the Fed's 2% target.
