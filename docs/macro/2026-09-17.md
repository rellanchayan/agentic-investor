# Market Weather — Thursday, September 17, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The Fed raised interest rates yesterday for the first time since 2023, signaled more hikes are likely, inflation is still running at 3.4%, and the market is not cheap — all three of these conditions together call for demanding a full margin of safety before buying anything.

---

## What happened and why it matters

### The Fed raised rates yesterday (September 16)
The Federal Reserve's FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate by 25 basis points, bringing the target range to **3.75%–4.00%**. This is the first rate hike since 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh confirmed the Fed still sees inflation as too high and is willing to keep tightening.

Why this matters to us: Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive for businesses and consumers, which tends to slow growth and push stock prices down. When rates rise, future profits are also worth less in today's dollars, which means the price a rational buyer should pay for a business goes down. A rising-rate environment demands a bigger discount before buying.

16 of 18 Fed officials penciled in at least one more hike before year-end. The tightening cycle is not over.

### Inflation is still elevated
The August CPI report (released September 11) showed headline inflation at **3.4% year-over-year**, with core (ex-food and energy) at **2.4%**. Energy prices drove much of the headline — gasoline up 27% over a year ago. The Fed's target is 2%. We are not there yet.

### The market is not cheap
The S&P 500 trailing price-to-earnings ratio is approximately **25.7**. The forward PE (based on next 12 months' expected earnings) is **19.1**, slightly above the 10-year historical average of 19.0. This is not a screaming buy — it is a market that is priced for decent outcomes, leaving little room for disappointment.

### SPY is above its 200-day average — the trend is intact
SPY closed at **761.94** yesterday, above its 200-day moving average of **715.84**. This tells us the broad market remains in an uptrend. We are not in a crash or a bear market. This is why the posture is cautious, not "sell everything" or "sit on hands indefinitely."

### This morning: futures are recovering
S&P 500 futures are up roughly 0.80% in pre-market, as investors digest the FOMC decision. Markets often sell on the hike day and recover the next day as the news is absorbed. Today's bounce does not change the underlying picture.
