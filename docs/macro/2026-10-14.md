# Market Weather: Wednesday, October 14, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** September CPI came in hot at +3.7% YoY (+0.6% MoM), inflation is reaccelerating, the Fed just raised rates in September and meets again October 27-28, and the broader market is historically expensive on a cyclically adjusted basis.

---

## Key Terms (for reference)

- **SPY:** The fund tracking the 500 largest US companies; our benchmark. SPY is at 772.74 today.
- **200-day average:** The average SPY closing price over the last ~200 trading days (724.20). SPY is above it — the market is in a technical uptrend.
- **CPI:** The Consumer Price Index, which measures how fast prices are rising (inflation). Today's print was hotter than expected.
- **Interest rates / the Fed:** The US central bank (the Federal Reserve) sets short-term borrowing rates. They raised rates to 3.75-4.00% on September 17, 2026, and meet again October 27-28.
- **CAPE:** The Cyclically Adjusted P/E ratio — a measure of how expensive the stock market is relative to long-run average earnings. Near 41.6 today, which is historically very high.

---

## What Happened This Morning

**CPI release (8:30am ET today, BLS):**
Headline inflation for September 2026 came in at +3.7% year-over-year and +0.6% month-over-month. Core CPI (stripping out food and energy) rose +2.6% year-over-year. The prior month headline was 3.4%. This is a step up — inflation is not cooling; it is reaccelerating. The 0.6% monthly gain is elevated and will be noticed by the Fed.

**Federal Reserve:**
The Fed raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points on September 17, 2026, bringing it to 3.75-4.00%. That is the most recent decision. The next meeting is October 27-28, 2026. A hot CPI print keeps the door open to another hike or to rates staying elevated for longer. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive for companies and generally pull stock prices down over time.

**Market valuation:**
- SPY forward P/E: approximately 20x, roughly at its 5-year average — not obviously cheap, not obviously screaming expensive on this one measure.
- CAPE (cyclically adjusted P/E): approximately 41.6 — near its all-time high. The only prior comparable was late 1999, just before the dot-com crash.
- Trailing P/E: approximately 28.7, well above the historical average of roughly 16.
- Bottom line: the market is fairly valued on near-term earnings expectations but historically expensive on longer-horizon measures.

**SPY trend:**
SPY (772.74) is above its 200-day moving average of 724.20. That is a positive technical signal — the market is in a broad uptrend. We note this but do not let a healthy trend override valuation discipline.
