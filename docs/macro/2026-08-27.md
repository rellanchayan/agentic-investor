# Market Weather — 2026-08-27

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is historically expensive, core inflation has been stuck at 3.3% for four straight months, the Fed Chair speaks at Jackson Hole today with the September rate decision still live, and all three of our thesis candidates remain deeply above any sensible entry price — the right response is patience, not stretching.

---

## The Big Picture in Plain English

**SPY and the 200-day average.** SPY closed at $769.59. Its 200-day average is $709.37. That puts the current price roughly 8.5% above the long-run trend line, which confirms the market is in a general uptrend. An uptrend is a positive signal — it means the overall direction has been up. But "in an uptrend" is not the same as "cheap." Right now the market is in an uptrend and also quite expensive at the same time. Both things are true.

**Futures this morning.** S&P 500 futures and Nasdaq 100 futures are up roughly 1% in pre-market trading. Dow futures are slightly negative. The driver is Nvidia's blockbuster Q2 earnings report, released after yesterday's close. Nvidia's CFO signaled strong sales growth through fiscal 2028, which lifted the whole AI-and-tech sentiment. This is a single-stock catalyst, not a change in the underlying macro backdrop — the expensive valuations, sticky inflation, and rate uncertainty are all still in place.

**Valuations: the market is expensive.** The S&P 500's trailing price-to-earnings ratio (what investors pay for each dollar of company profits) is approximately 26x. The historical median is around 18x, meaning investors are paying roughly 45% more than average for every dollar of earnings. A longer-term measure, the Shiller CAPE (which smooths earnings over a decade), stands at about 41x — that places today's valuation at the 98.9th percentile of all months since 1881. Only 18 months out of 145 years have ever been more expensive, and all of them were during the dot-com bubble of 1999-2000. Expensive markets are not guaranteed to fall, but they do leave very little room for disappointment.

**Inflation is stuck.** The Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure — called core PCE — came in at 3.3% year-over-year for July, released yesterday morning. That is four consecutive months (April through July) at essentially the same level, with no net improvement. The Fed's target is 2%. Inflation running 1.3 percentage points above target, with no downward trend, makes it very hard for the Fed to cut rates. In fact, the next FOMC meeting on September 15-16 is described in financial press as a "finely balanced" call — a rate hike is a genuine possibility depending on what happens with inflation and geopolitics between now and then.

**Interest rates.** The Fed's policy rate has been held at 3.50-3.75% since December 2025, following three cuts in the second half of 2025. The 10-year Treasury yield currently sits at 4.66%, and the 2-year at 4.22%. When government bonds pay 4.66% with essentially zero risk, investors need a meaningful reason to accept the volatility of equities. Elevated yields also compress the "fair value" of stocks by discounting future profits at a higher rate — this is one reason our own fair-value estimates for AAPL, MSFT, and Visa sit well below current market prices.

- **Visa (V)** at $379.61 — our base fair value is $296.91. It is 28% above our base estimate and 10% above our bull case ($345.97). We would need it near $243 or below.
- **AAPL** at $313.60 — our base fair value is $195, and our conviction on AAPL is only 3 (below the buy threshold of 4). It would need to fall nearly 50% to clear any bar, and even then conviction would need to be re-evaluated.

The cautious posture raises our required margin of safety from 12% to 18%. But as yesterday's journal noted, that distinction is irrelevant for these three names — they are nowhere near entry territory at any bar. Cautious posture is still the right call because of the event risk today (Warsh), the expensive market, and sticky inflation. If Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks are notably hawkish, yields could move further and equity valuations would face additional pressure.

Today's job is to watch, document, and wait. We do not buy anything we do not have a thesis for. We do not buy anything deeply above fair value. Both rules are satisfied by doing nothing today.
