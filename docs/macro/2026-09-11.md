## What each term means (quick reference)

- **SPY**: the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — "the market."
- **200-day average**: the average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. Price above it = market in an uptrend; below it = downtrend.
- **Interest rates / the Fed**: the US central bank (Fed) sets short-term interest rates. Higher rates make borrowing costlier and tend to push stock prices down; lower rates do the opposite.
- **Inflation (CPI)**: how fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation can lead the Fed to raise rates, which spooks markets.
- **FOMC meeting**: the scheduled gathering where the Fed votes on interest rates. A surprise hike or cut can move stocks significantly.

## Big events this week and next

### TODAY — CPI (August 2026), released 8:30 AM ET
The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported the August consumer price index:
- **Headline CPI**: +0.4% month-over-month, +3.4% year-over-year. In line with Wall Street consensus, but still well above the Fed's 2% target.
- **Core CPI** (excludes food and energy): +0.3% month-over-month — 0.1 percentage point above forecast. Annual core rate: +2.4%.

The summary: inflation did not spike, but it did not cool either. Energy (oil near $100) is keeping headline inflation stubborn. Core was a touch hotter than hoped. This is not a disaster print, but it gives the Fed less comfort about cutting or holding.

### NEXT WEEK — FOMC meeting, September 15-16
The Federal Reserve's policy committee meets Monday and Tuesday. The rate decision lands **Wednesday, September 16 at 2:00 PM ET**. This is the single biggest event risk on the calendar right now.

- Most economists in a Reuters poll expect the Fed to **hold rates steady**.
- However, following the hot CPI and energy shock, market-implied odds of a **25-basis-point rate hike** sit at roughly 30-40%.
- JPMorgan strategists now expect a hike; Goldman Sachs calls a hike "very unlikely."
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has consistently avoided giving forward guidance, so the outcome is genuinely uncertain.

A surprise hike would likely push stocks lower across the board. A hold with hawkish language could also disappoint.

### Later — Jobs report (nonfarm payrolls): October 2, 2026

No jobs number this week; the next one arrives October 2.

## Rates: elevated and uncertain

The prevailing interest rate environment is elevated relative to recent years and the direction from here is contested. Energy prices (oil near $100/barrel, tied to geopolitical tension around Iran) are feeding inflation, reducing the Fed's room to hold without credibility risk. This environment makes expensive growth stocks more vulnerable and cash more valuable than in a declining-rate environment.

## What this means for us today

We are long-term investors. We do not panic about a single CPI print. But today's environment checks several of the boxes that make a careful investor more selective:

1. The market is already expensive by historical P/E standards (~26 vs. median ~18).
2. Inflation is running at 3.4% — nearly double the Fed's target — and the Fed meeting five days from now carries real uncertainty.
3. All three of our active thesis candidates (AAPL, MSFT, V) are trading between 25% and 72% above our conservative fair-value estimates regardless of posture. The posture does not change the immediate outcome — there is nothing to buy today.

The CAUTIOUS posture means: if anything were close to our fair-value range, we would demand an extra margin of safety (say, 15%+ rather than 10%) before acting. We hold cash patiently and wait for the FOMC noise to settle next week before reassessing.
