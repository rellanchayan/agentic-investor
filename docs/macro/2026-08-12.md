## What the big terms mean (quick reference)

- **SPY** is a fund that holds the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark. When we say "the market," we mostly mean SPY.
- **200-day average** is the average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above that line means the market is in a general uptrend; below it means a downtrend.
- **The Fed / interest rates**: the US central bank sets short-term borrowing costs. Higher rates make it more expensive to borrow money and tend to push stock prices down by raising the "discount rate" applied to future profits.
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**: measures how fast prices in the economy are rising. The Fed's target is 2% annual inflation. Above that, the Fed tends to keep rates high or raise them further.
- **P/E ratio**: the price you pay per dollar of earnings. A P/E of 26x means you are paying $26 for every $1 of annual profit. Historically the S&P 500 average is around 18-19x. Higher P/E = more expensive.
- **VIX**: measures expected stock-market volatility over the next 30 days. A reading below 15 means investors are relatively calm. Above 30 means fear.

## Today's key event: July CPI released this morning

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the July 2026 Consumer Price Index report at 8:30am ET today (August 12). The numbers:

- Headline inflation: **+0.1% for the month; +3.4% year-over-year** (down slightly from 3.5% in June)
- Core inflation (excluding food and energy): **+0.2% for the month; +2.5% year-over-year**
- Shelter drove most of the monthly increase; gasoline fell ~2.8%

The reading came in exactly in line with Wall Street forecasts, which is neither a positive nor negative surprise. The good news: inflation is slowly drifting lower. The bad news: 3.4% annual is still well above the Fed's 2% target, which keeps rate-hike risk alive.

## Interest rates and the Fed

The Federal Reserve's benchmark rate is currently **3.50-3.75%** (reserve balance rate: 3.65%). This rate has been on hold since late 2025. The Fed removed its previous language about expecting to cut rates; instead, officials have signaled that a further increase is possible if inflation does not cool.

Markets are currently pricing in roughly a **46-51% probability of a rate hike at the September 16 FOMC meeting.** That is a near-coin-flip, which creates genuine uncertainty for rate-sensitive sectors (real estate, utilities, financials, and growth stocks with long earnings runways).

The **10-year US Treasury yield is near 4.7%**. This is the risk-free rate investors can earn by lending money to the US government for a decade. When this rate is high, stocks become relatively less attractive — investors demand higher returns from equities too, which pushes prices down. A 4.7% 10-year yield is elevated and competes directly with equity returns.

## Market valuation

The S&P 500 currently trades at:
- **Trailing P/E: approximately 26x** (GuruFocus, as of August 11)
- **Forward P/E: approximately 20.4x** (as of August 7)
- Historical average trailing P/E: approximately 18-19x

The market is not at bubble extremes, but it is meaningfully above its long-run average on both measures. Paying above average prices requires above-average confidence in earnings growth — and with GDP decelerating and rate-hike risk present, that confidence is harder to justify.
