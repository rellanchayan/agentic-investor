# Market Weather — Monday, September 21, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is in a calm bull trend today, but the S&P 500 is priced at
the second-most-expensive level in 145 years of data and the Fed just raised borrowing costs last
week — that combination demands a larger discount before we buy anything.

---

## What the numbers say (plain English)

**SPY (our benchmark):** $768.77, which is 7.3% above its 200-day average of $716.69.

The 200-day average is the average price of the 500 largest US companies over the last ~200
trading days. When today's price sits above that average, the market is in an uptrend. We are
firmly in one. That is a mild positive signal.

**Futures this morning:** S&P 500 futures are up roughly 0.41-0.70% before the open, and
prediction markets put an 81% chance of a higher open. The Nasdaq is also up ~0.56%. Short
version: today is expected to open green.

**VIX (the "fear gauge"):** ~14.81 as of Friday's close, down from 15.2 a week ago. The VIX
measures how nervous options traders are. A reading below 20 is calm. At 14.81 the market is
relaxed, not fearful.

**CAPE ratio (how expensive the market is):** ~40.58. The CAPE is a measure of how much
investors are paying for $1 of earnings, smoothed over 10 years. The long-run average is about
17. At 40.58 investors are paying more than twice the historical norm. This reading sits at the
98.8th percentile of all months since 1881 — only the dot-com bubble of 1999-2000 ever reached
higher. A few sources put the reading at 40.68 or 41.1 depending on the exact date and method;
all point to the same conclusion: the market is extremely expensive.

**Interest rates / the Fed:** On September 16, the Federal Reserve raised the benchmark interest
rate by 0.25 percentage points to a range of 3.75-4.00%. Higher rates make it costlier for
businesses to borrow and grow, and they make bonds more attractive compared to stocks. The
10-year US Treasury bond now yields approximately 5.01% — that is a risk-free return that
competes directly with what stocks offer. The next Fed meeting is not until October 27-28, so
there is no rate decision this week.

## What this means for us today

The surface picture is friendly: market is in an uptrend, futures are green, VIX is calm, and
there are no scary events on the calendar this week. For a long-term investor that is pleasant
background noise, not a buying signal.

The deeper picture is what drives the posture. The CAPE at 40.58 means we are buying into one
of the most expensive markets in history. The 10-year Treasury at 5.01% means investors
demand more from stocks to justify the extra risk. When rates are this high and the market is
this expensive, the right response is to demand a bigger discount before buying — our cautious
posture raises the minimum margin of safety from 12% to 18%.

Looking at our five thesis candidates:
- AAPL: trades 44.5% above its fair-value base — needs to fall ~43% before it clears the bar
- MSFT: trades 23.2% above fair-value base — needs to fall ~33% before clearing the bar
- GOOGL: trades 66.4% above fair-value base — extremely stretched
- V: trades 23.3% above fair-value base — needs to fall ~35% before clearing the bar
- NKE: the closest candidate at -8.9% below fair-value base (price $35.50 vs base $32.60),
  but still needs to fall further and has the lowest conviction rating (2/5)

None of the five clears the 18% cautious bar today. We hold cash and wait.

The quiet week is not a reason to relax the posture. The elevated valuation and rate environment
are structural conditions, not this week's headlines.
