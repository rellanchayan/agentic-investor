## What the market looks like today

**SPY** (our benchmark fund holding the 500 largest US companies) closed Friday at **$774.93**. Its **200-day average** is **$703.46**. That means SPY is sitting **10.2% above** its long-term average price — a sign the market has been trending upward for a sustained period. An uptrend is generally good, but being 10% stretched above the average also means there is more room to fall back before reaching "normal."

**VIX** (the fear gauge — measures how much turbulence the options market expects over the next 30 days) is at **15.45** this morning, up slightly from Friday's close of 14.90. The 52-week range runs from 13.38 to 35.30. At 15.45, fear is moderate — investors are not panicking, but they are not asleep either. A spike toward 25-30+ would signal genuine stress.

**Futures** (pre-market contracts that preview where the market will open): S&P 500 futures +0.12%, Nasdaq 100 futures +0.38%. A gently positive open is expected.

## Valuation — is the market cheap or expensive?

The **trailing P/E ratio** (price divided by last twelve months of earnings) for the S&P 500 is approximately **27x**, depending on the source (range: 25-30x across data providers). The long-term historical average is roughly 18-19x, and the current reading sits near the **70th percentile** of the past decade. The market is not in bubble territory, but it is not cheap either — it is moderately expensive by historical standards.

## Interest rates and the Fed

The Federal Reserve's short-term interest rate sits at **3.50–3.75%**. The Fed held rates steady at its June 2026 meeting and penciled in one possible cut later in 2026, but the timing is uncertain. There is no FOMC meeting this week. Rates at this level are not low — they raise the hurdle rate for owning stocks versus simply holding cash.
