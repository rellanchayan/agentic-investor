# Market Weather — Friday, September 18, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The Fed raised interest rates just two days ago for the first time in three years, signaled more hikes are coming, inflation is still running at 3.4%, and the market is historically expensive by long-term metrics — all three conditions together mean we should demand a full margin of safety before buying anything.

## What happened and why it matters

### The rate-hiking cycle just resumed (two days ago)

On Wednesday, September 16, the Federal Reserve raised the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to **3.75%–4.00%**. This was the first rate hike since 2023 and was unanimous (12-0). Fed Chair Kevin Warsh confirmed the Fed still sees inflation as too high. Critically, **16 of 18 officials project at least one more hike before year-end**. The tightening cycle is not over.

Why this matters: Higher rates make future corporate earnings worth less in today's dollars. They also raise the "risk-free" return available from sitting in cash or short-term bonds. When cash earns ~4% and the market trades at 25x earnings (a 4% earnings yield), stocks offer very little premium for the extra risk. A rising-rate environment demands a bigger discount before buying.

The 10-year Treasury yield sits at approximately **4.95%** today — down slightly from yesterday's 5.01% but still near the highest level since 2007. This is the rate the whole economy borrows against.

### Inflation is still elevated

The August 2026 CPI report (released September 11) showed:
- Headline: **+3.4% year-over-year**
- Core (ex-food and energy): **+2.4%**

The Fed's target is 2% (measured by PCE, which tends to run a touch below CPI). We are not there. Energy (gasoline +27% year-over-year) is driving a chunk of the headline, but core being 2.4% means the problem is not only energy.

### The market is not cheap

Three ways to measure stock market valuation:

| Metric | Today | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing P/E (S&P 500) | ~25.7x | You pay $25.70 for every $1 of last year's earnings. Historical average is roughly 17-18x. |
| Forward P/E | ~19.5x | Based on next 12 months expected earnings. Roughly at the historical average — assumes earnings growth comes through. |
| CAPE / Shiller P/E | ~39.7x | Smooths earnings over 10 years to remove cyclical noise. Historical average is ~20.8. The market is 91% above that average. |

The CAPE number is striking. It is not a timing tool — an expensive market can stay expensive for years — but it does mean the margin for error is thin. If something goes wrong (another rate hike surprise, a recession, earnings disappointments), there is a lot of air below.

### SPY is above its 200-day average — the broad trend is intact

SPY closed at **$758.99** yesterday, above its 200-day moving average of **$716.24** (about 6% above). This tells us the broad market remains in an uptrend. We are not in a crash or bear market. This is why the posture is *cautious*, not *sell everything*.

### This morning: futures are up sharply

S&P 500 futures are up roughly **+1.94%** in pre-market, with Nasdaq up about **+2.26%**. Technology and semiconductors are leading. This appears to be a continuation of yesterday's post-FOMC recovery rally — markets often absorb a rate hike and bounce in the following sessions. Crude oil is down sharply (-6.46%), which helps consumers but raises a mild question about demand expectations.

This morning's rally is real momentum, but it does not change the valuation picture or the rate environment.

### VIX: calm, not cheap

The CBOE VIX (the "fear gauge") is at approximately **15.21**, down slightly on the day. A VIX below 20 means options markets do not currently expect large short-term swings. This is comforting for people already holding stocks but does not signal that stocks are cheap. A low VIX in an expensive, rising-rate market reflects confidence, not safety.
