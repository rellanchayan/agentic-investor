---

## What each term means (plain English)

- **SPY**: a fund that holds the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark.
- **200-day average**: the average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days.
  If SPY is above it, the broad trend is up; below it, the trend is down.
- **FOMC / Fed funds rate**: the US central bank's short-term interest rate. When the Fed
  raises rates, borrowing costs go up and stock prices tend to come under pressure.
- **CPI**: Consumer Price Index, the main inflation gauge. A hot print can push the Fed to raise
  rates or keep them elevated longer.
- **Earnings season**: the few weeks each quarter when companies report profits. More news, more
  volatility. Q3 season is just starting.

## Fed policy

The Fed has held the funds rate at 3.50–3.75% since December 2025. That followed a series of
cuts from the peak of 2023–24. The July 29 meeting vote was 9–3 to hold; all three dissenters
wanted to raise by 25 basis points. The September 15–16 meeting includes a new Summary of
Economic Projections (the "dot plot"), which will show where each official thinks rates are headed.
Higher rates mean higher borrowing costs for companies and consumers, and generally put downward
pressure on stock valuations.

## Geopolitical risk

The US-Iran military conflict, now in its seventh month, escalated this week with US forces
destroying Iranian crude tankers in retaliation for attacks on an American warship. Iran's Houthi
allies also struck Saudi Aramco facilities. The result:

- Brent crude: **$99.12/barrel** (+1.23% this morning, up 8% for September)
- WTI crude: **$94.01/barrel** (+1.05% this morning)

Oil near $100 is a direct inflationary input — it flows into gasoline, transportation, and
manufacturing costs. If the August CPI due Thursday reflects even part of the September oil
move, the print could surprise to the upside. Goldman Sachs warns Brent could exceed $120 in
2027 if Gulf output remains disrupted. This is a real, ongoing risk — not a tail scenario.
