---

## What the terms mean (quick reference)

- **SPY:** A fund holding the 500 largest US companies. When we say "the market," we mean SPY.
- **200-day average:** The average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it signals a general uptrend; below it signals a downtrend.
- **Fed / interest rates:** The US central bank sets short-term rates. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive and tend to push stock prices down.
- **CPI (inflation):** How fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation can spook markets and push the Fed to raise rates further.
- **Earnings season:** The few weeks each quarter when companies report profits. More surprises, more price moves.
- **CAPE:** Cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio. A long-run valuation measure that smooths earnings over 10 years. A reading of 41.6 means the market is near its most expensive level in recorded history.

## Why the macro environment is challenging

**1. Inflation is still above target.**
September CPI came in at +3.7% year-over-year, above the +3.6% consensus. This was the second consecutive above-consensus print. The 10-year breakeven inflation rate is approximately 2.33%, meaning markets expect inflation to stay above the Fed's 2% target for an extended period.

**2. Interest rates are elevated and could go higher.**
The Federal Reserve raised its target rate to 3.75-4.00% at its September 15-16 meeting. The effective Fed funds rate was approximately 3.88% as of early October. The 10-year Treasury yield was approximately 5.22% as of October 9 (the freshest data found; it may have moved since). At that level, government bonds yield nearly as much as the S&P 500's earnings yield — investors are not being paid much extra to take stock risk versus holding Treasuries.

The FOMC meets in 8 days. With inflation running hot, the probability of a second consecutive hike on October 28 is meaningful. Rate uncertainty alone justifies demanding a larger discount before buying equities.

**3. The stock market is expensive by long-run measures.**
The S&P 500's cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE) is approximately 41.6, within a few points of its all-time record from late 1999. The trailing P/E is approximately 28.7x. The forward 12-month P/E is approximately 20.0x, slightly above the 5-year average of 19.9x. On the forward measure the market looks roughly fairly valued; on every long-run measure it looks stretched. When prices are this high relative to earnings history, there is little cushion if earnings or guidance disappoint.

**4. This is peak Q3 earnings week — elevated noise.**
Approximately 350 companies report this week, including Netflix tonight, Tesla and Meta on Wednesday, and MCD plus Mastercard on Thursday. Earnings surprises in either direction at mega-cap companies can move SPY materially in a single session. Heightened daily volatility is to be expected through Friday.

**5. SPY is in an uptrend — but that does not override valuation.**
SPY at $771.72 sits 6.3% above its 200-day average of $725.85. An uptrend means the market is not in freefall. But an uptrend in an expensive market is a reason to be patient, not a reason to pay any price. The cautious posture is not about predicting a crash; it is about demanding the full margin of safety because there is not enough cushion if we are wrong.
