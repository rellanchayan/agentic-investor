## What "cautious" means in plain English

We require an 18% margin of safety before buying (our base is 12%; cautious adds 6%). In practice, that means we only buy if a stock is trading at least 18% below our estimate of what it is worth. We prefer to hold cash and wait. We do not panic-sell anything we already own (we have nothing to sell today), but we do not chase prices.

## Interest rates and the Fed

The Federal Reserve last met on June 17 and held its benchmark rate at **3.50–3.75%**. Tomorrow's decision will be the next move — or non-move.

The June dot plot (the chart of where Fed officials think rates should go) turned notably hawkish: the median expected year-end 2026 rate was revised up to 3.8% from 3.4% in March, and 9 of 18 officials penciled in at least one rate increase this year. Markets are currently assigning a **35% probability of a 25 basis-point hike tomorrow**. That is unusually high uncertainty for a Fed decision — typically the market has a much clearer read by the day before. If the Fed hikes instead of holds, that would raise borrowing costs across the economy and would likely push stock prices lower in the short term.

## Inflation (CPI)

The June CPI report (released July 14) showed a meaningful improvement. Headline inflation fell to **3.5% year-over-year**, down from 4.2% in May — its first monthly decline of 2026, at -0.4% month-over-month. Core CPI (stripping out food and energy) fell to **2.6%** from 2.8%.

This is genuinely encouraging. The May reading of 4.2% was one of the main reasons for the cautious posture held since early July. With inflation cooling, pressure on the Fed to hike is reduced — though not eliminated, since 3.5% is still well above the 2% target. No new CPI data is due until August 12.

June PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) releases Thursday morning. This will be important: if PCE also shows cooling, it reduces the hike probability. If it surprises hot, it increases it.

Source: [Treasury Yields and CPI - CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/14/treasury-yields-rise-as-fed-rate-hike-expectations-grow.html)

## The semiconductor sell-off

The most visible market story today is a severe, global sell-off in semiconductor and chip stocks. The Bloomberg semiconductor index fell **7.5%** — its biggest single-day drop since April 2025. Specific moves: NVIDIA -4.92%, AMD -8.31%, SK Hynix (South Korea) -15%, with South Korea's KOSPI index triggering its circuit breaker and falling nearly 11%.

The drivers are two concerns that have been building for weeks:
1. **China's advanced chipmaking is catching up faster than expected**, threatening market share and pricing power for US and South Korean chipmakers.
2. **AI capital spending sustainability**: Alphabet's recent earnings showed soaring capex, which spooked investors who are now questioning whether the AI spending boom can continue to justify chip valuations. This week's earnings from Microsoft, Apple, and Amazon will be watched closely for any signal on AI spend.

This sell-off is sector-specific, not a systemic crash. But it matters to us because MSFT, AAPL, NVDA, and GOOGL are all on our watch list — all in tech.

Sources: [Bloomberg chip rout](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/korean-stocks-sink-as-chipmakers-plung-on-deepening-ai-fatigue), [Bloomberg US tech slide](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/us-tech-stocks-poised-to-slide-as-global-chip-selloff-deepens), [CNBC SK Hynix plunges](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/28/sk-hynix-plunges-semiconductor-selloff-deepens-samsung-softbank.html), [Yahoo Finance semi rout](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nasdaq-semiconductor-stocks-lead-tech-151000500.html)

## Valuation and yield

The S&P 500 forward 12-month price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio sits at approximately **19.76-20.4x** as of late July 2026. The 10-year average is 19.0x, and the 5-year average is 19.9x. The market is not wildly expensive by recent standards, but it is not cheap either — there is no "sale" across the board.

The 10-year US Treasury yield is holding at approximately **4.64%**, broadly unchanged from the July 23 journal. Elevated yields make future corporate earnings less valuable in today's dollars, and they compete with stocks for investor capital. Until the Fed actually cuts rates (a September cut is priced at ~80% odds), this pressure remains.

Q2 GDP came in at **2.8%** on the advance estimate, above the 2.0% consensus. The economy is not in recession, which is supportive for corporate earnings.
