# Market Weather — Friday, September 25, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The S&P 500 is trading near the second-most-expensive valuation in recorded history (Shiller CAPE ~41x) while the Fed is actively raising rates and inflation remains above target — conditions that require a meaningfully larger discount before any new buy makes sense.

---

## What the numbers say

**SPY (our benchmark):** $768.00, which is **+6.9% above its 200-day moving average** of $718.41. A price above the 200-day average means the market is in a general uptrend — prices are higher on average than they were over the past year. This is not a beaten-down market where fear is creating a fire sale.

**Valuation (CAPE ratio ~41x):** The Shiller CAPE is a measure of how expensive the S&P 500 is relative to long-term earnings, smoothed over a decade to iron out short-term profit swings. The historical average is about 17x. At roughly 41x, we are at the second-highest reading in 145 years of data — the only time the market was more expensive was at the peak of the dot-com bubble in late 1999 (44.2x), which preceded a 49% market decline over the following two and a half years. High CAPE does not predict when a decline will happen, but it does mean you are paying historically steep prices for each dollar of future earnings.

**Interest rates:** The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 0.25 percentage points on September 16, 2026 — its first hike in over three years — to a range of 3.75%–4.00%. Sixteen of eighteen policymakers expect at least one further increase before year-end. The 10-year US Treasury bond now yields approximately 5.16%, up about 5 basis points from yesterday and nearly 95 basis points higher than a year ago. Higher rates matter for two reasons: (a) borrowing costs more for companies and consumers, slowing earnings growth; and (b) a "risk-free" government bond now pays over 5% per year, which is real competition for stocks priced as if they will deliver 3-4% real returns.

**Futures this morning:** S&P 500 futures are up +0.24% (about +19 points to 7,786), Nasdaq futures +0.48%, Dow futures +0.18%. The open is pointing modestly higher — no dramatic directional move. Asian markets showed a mixed picture overnight (Nikkei up, Shanghai and Hang Seng slipped).

## What this means for us today

We are long-term investors who buy good businesses at a fair price and hold for years. The combination of near-record market valuations (CAPE ~41x) and rising interest rates means we should demand a **larger-than-normal margin of safety** before placing any buy. Our normal threshold is 12%; under a cautious posture, we require at least **18% margin of safety** (a 6-percentage-point premium) on any name we consider.

In practice, this changes nothing today: our three thesis candidates with current valuations (AAPL at -44.6%, MSFT at -28.7%, V at -22.4%) are all far below the entry prices we would need — none is anywhere close to even the normal 12% bar, let alone the 18% cautious bar. NKE (margin of safety -9.5%, conviction 2/5) remains the nearest candidate but still does not clear the cautious bar and carries only narrow-moat conviction.

The account is 100% cash ($1,000,000). The cautious posture does not mean we never buy — it means we wait until the price is right. Patient inaction when the market is expensive is discipline, not failure.
