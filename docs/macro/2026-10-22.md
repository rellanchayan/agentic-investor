---

## What the terms mean (quick reference)

- **SPY:** A fund holding the 500 largest US companies. When we say "the market," we mean SPY.
- **200-day average:** The average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it signals a general uptrend; below it signals a downtrend.
- **Fed / interest rates:** The US central bank sets short-term rates. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive and tend to push stock prices down.
- **CPI (inflation):** How fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation can push the Fed to raise rates further, which hurts stocks.
- **Earnings season:** The few weeks each quarter when companies report profits. More surprises, more price moves.

## Why the macro environment stays challenging

**1. Inflation is still running hot.**
The most recent CPI print (September data, released Oct 14) came in at +3.7% year-over-year — above the +3.6% consensus and up from +3.4% in August. Core CPI was +2.6% YoY, also above estimates. Energy prices (gasoline +9.1%) drove the headline figure, but the core beat suggests underlying inflation has not been tamed.

**2. The Fed just hiked — and meets again in 6 days.**
The Federal Reserve raised rates to 3.75%-4.00% at its September 16 meeting (unanimous 12-0 vote). The FOMC meets again October 27-28, with the rate decision on October 28 at 2:00 PM ET. Markets currently price roughly a 17-20% chance of another hike — low, but not zero. Fed officials have signaled they want to "skip" October but keep the door open for December if inflation stays hot.

**3. Treasury yields are still elevated.**
The 10-year US Treasury yield is approximately 5.1-5.3% (last confirmed data from mid-October). At that level, a risk-free government bond yields roughly as much as the S&P 500's earnings yield (~3.5%). There is very little extra reward for taking equity risk versus simply holding Treasuries. This keeps pressure on stock valuations.

**4. The market is expensive.**
- Cyclically-adjusted P/E (CAPE): ~41.6 — near all-time record levels, last seen in 1999.
- Trailing 12-month P/E: approximately 28-29x.
- Forward 12-month P/E: approximately 20x, near its 5-year average of 19.9x.
CAPE and trailing P/E say expensive. Forward P/E says roughly fair. When rates are this high, even "fair value" on forward earnings leaves little cushion.

**5. Earnings season is active but not reassuring.**
We are in the Q3 2026 earnings season. About 9% of S&P 500 companies have reported, and 84% have beaten estimates — a strong beat rate. However, stocks are falling on average even when companies beat. Historically, companies that beat see their share price rise ~0.9% around the report; this season they are falling ~0.2%. That unusual pattern signals the market may already be priced for perfection, and that guidance disappointments carry more weight than beats.

Last week, tech stocks sold off hard: the Nasdaq Composite fell about 4.5% and the PHLX Semiconductor index fell roughly 11.4%, led by AI-linked chip stocks. SPY declined from ~$772 to ~$762 over the period.
