## What the terms mean (quick reference)

- **SPY:** A fund holding the 500 largest US companies. When we say "the market," we mean SPY.
- **200-day average:** The average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it signals a general uptrend; below it signals a downtrend.
- **Fed / interest rates:** The US central bank sets short-term rates. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive and tend to push stock prices down.
- **CPI (inflation):** How fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation can spook markets and push the Fed to raise rates further.
- **Earnings season:** The few weeks each quarter when companies report profits. More surprises, more price moves.

## Why the macro environment is challenging

**1. Inflation is still hot.**
September CPI came in at +3.7% year-over-year, above the +3.6% consensus and up from +3.4% in August. Core CPI (which strips out food and energy) was +2.6%, also above estimates. Two months in a row of above-consensus prints signals that inflation has not been tamed.

**2. The Fed just hiked — and may hike again.**
The Federal Reserve raised its target interest rate by 0.25 percentage points at its September 15-16 meeting, bringing the target range to 3.75-4.00%. The hot September CPI, released two days ago, raises the odds of another hike at the October 27-28 meeting. The 10-year Treasury yield is near 5.28% (from yesterday's data; we found no fresher quote this morning). At that level, "risk-free" government bonds yield roughly as much as the earnings yield of the S&P 500 — there is very little extra reward for owning stocks versus bonds.

**3. The stock market is expensive.**
The S&P 500's cyclically-adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE) is approximately 41.6 — near all-time highs and within a few points of the late-1999 peak before the dot-com bust. The forward 12-month price-to-earnings ratio is ~20x, slightly above the 5-year (19.9x) and 10-year (19.0x) averages. When stocks are priced this richly and rates are this high, there is very little cushion if earnings disappoint.

**4. Earnings season adds noise.**
Q3 earnings season is underway. The big banks reported this week with mixed results. The real fireworks arrive Oct 26-30 when the mega-cap technology companies report — the same week the Fed announces its next rate decision. Two major binary events in one week historically create elevated volatility.
