---

## What these words mean (for new readers)

- **SPY**: a fund holding the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — "the market."
- **200-day average**: SPY's average price over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it means the market is in a general uptrend.
- **Shiller CAPE**: a measure of how expensive the stock market is relative to long-run earnings. The historical average is about 17. At 41, stocks cost roughly twice what history says is "normal."
- **10-year Treasury yield**: the interest rate the US government pays to borrow for 10 years. At 5.22%, that is near multi-year highs. Higher rates make stocks less attractive (you can earn more just by lending to the government) and raise borrowing costs for companies.
- **PCE inflation**: the Fed's preferred inflation measure. A hot reading could push the Fed to raise rates further.
- **Jobs report (Nonfarm Payrolls)**: how many new jobs were added. A strong number suggests the economy is running hot; a weak one raises recession worries.

## What this means for us today

We stay CAUTIOUS. The market is structurally in an uptrend (SPY above its 200-day average), but it is trading at one of the most expensive valuations in 150 years, borrowing costs are elevated, and this week holds two data releases that could swing prices sharply. Under a cautious posture we require a larger margin of safety than normal before buying — meaning a candidate must trade at a bigger discount to fair value before we pull the trigger.

In practice: none of our current bench names pass both the price gate and conviction gate even at normal posture. Our closest candidate, MCD ($237.28), is just barely above its base fair value at the current price and still needs a conviction upgrade to 4. V ($370.88) passes conviction but needs a roughly 35% price decline to clear the cautious-posture bar. No buys are expected today.

The JOLTS miss (7.079M vs 7.225M) is a modest incremental piece of information, suggesting the labor market is cooling gently. It does not change the big picture enough to shift posture to normal.
