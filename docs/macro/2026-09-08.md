# Market Weather — 2026-09-08

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is structurally bullish (SPY sits 7.6% above its 200-day average) but is expensive by almost any historical measure, two major market-moving events land this week and next (CPI Thursday, FOMC Wednesday September 16), the 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.80% which competes directly with equities, and all three of our thesis candidates trade 25-62% above their fair value estimates — patience and a bigger discount requirement are the right posture.

---

## The Big Picture in Plain English

**SPY and the 200-day average.** SPY — the fund holding the 500 largest US companies, our benchmark — last traded at $766.99. Its 200-day average is $712.66. We are 7.6% above that average, which means the broad market is in an uptrend. That is a structural positive. However, being in an uptrend does not mean the market is cheap or safe — it simply means the overall direction has been up. You can be in an uptrend and still be in an environment where patient investors should demand more discount before committing cash.

**Futures this morning.** S&P 500 futures are down about 0.25%, Dow futures are down 0.73%, and Nasdaq futures are up a small 0.07%. The picture is slightly negative but not alarming — a routine morning drift, not a risk-off move.

**Valuations: the market is expensive.** The S&P 500's trailing price-to-earnings ratio sits around 26-29x — meaning investors today pay $26-29 for each dollar of company profits. The long-run historical median is roughly 18x. The Shiller CAPE (a smoothed, 10-year version of the P/E ratio that removes short-term distortions) stands at 41.4x, compared with a historical median of 16.1x. That means the market is more than twice as expensive as its long-run norm by that measure. Expensive markets are not guaranteed to fall. But they leave little room for error, and when something does go wrong, the correction tends to be steeper. We demand a bigger margin of safety — a larger discount from fair value — when buying into an expensive market.

**Interest rates.** The US Federal Reserve's short-term benchmark rate sits at 3.50-3.75%. After three rate cuts in late 2025, the Fed has held steady at each of its four meetings in 2026, and market futures are currently pricing the possibility of a rise to 4% by year-end. The 10-year US Treasury yield is 4.80% and the 30-year is 5.27%. These are meaningfully elevated: when investors can earn roughly 4.8% annually from US government bonds with essentially zero risk, stocks have to work harder to justify their prices. Higher yields also lower the math-derived fair value of stocks (future profits discounted back at a higher rate are worth less today). This is a headwind to valuations.

**Geopolitical and commodity risk.** Brent crude oil is near $98/barrel, approaching $100, after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabian energy facilities. Oil at $100 adds inflationary pressure and raises input costs across the economy. If it persists, it complicates the Fed's path toward lower rates.

**Labor market.** The August 2026 jobs report (released September 4) showed 162,000 new nonfarm payroll jobs — well above the 56,000 forecast and the strongest monthly gain in five months. A strong labor market supports consumer spending and corporate revenues, which is a genuine positive for the economy. The flip side is that a strong labor market makes it easier for the Fed to hold rates higher, which is less positive for stock valuations.

## What This Means for Us Today

We hold $1,000,000 in cash with no open positions. Today's posture is **CAUTIOUS**.

In practice: hold cash, do not stretch to buy. The CPI print on Thursday is the next meaningful data point to watch — if it surprises to the downside (cool inflation), it would ease rate pressure and potentially warrant reassessing whether stocks look slightly less expensive. If it surprises to the upside, cautious is clearly the right posture.

The cautious posture is not alarm. It is not a prediction of a crash. It is the correct position of a disciplined long-term investor who looks at a market trading at 41x smoothed earnings with a 4.80% risk-free rate and two major events imminent, and says: wait for a better price, or wait for the events to pass without damage, before deploying cash.

The standing obligation noted in the playbook — expanding our thesis bench to cover healthcare, consumer staples, industrials, and energy names — is also worth progressing this week during lower-event hours (not market-moving mornings like Thursday). The cash drag now sits at roughly 1 pp per month; bench-building is the path to closing it when prices eventually come in.
