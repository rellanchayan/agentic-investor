## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The S&P 500 is trading at roughly 7.6% above its long-run trend

---

## What the key words mean

- **SPY** is a fund holding the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — "beating the
  market" means beating SPY over years. Today SPY is at $777.99.
- **200-day average** is the average closing price of SPY over roughly the last 200 trading
  days (~$723.35 right now). SPY trading *above* that line means the overall market is in an
  uptrend. Today it is 7.6% above that line — the uptrend is intact, but this is not a
  pullback sale.
- **CAPE ratio** (Cyclically Adjusted Price-to-Earnings) measures how expensive the market
  is relative to long-run average profits. A CAPE of ~41 is more than double the 100-year
  median of roughly 17. Historically, when CAPE is in this range, expected returns over the
  next 10 years are low — sometimes negative. The market is expensive.
- **Fed / interest rates**: The US central bank raised its benchmark rate in September 2026.
  The current target range is approximately 3.75–4.00%. Its next scheduled meeting is
  October 27–28. Higher rates make bonds more attractive relative to stocks and push down
  the "fair price" we would be willing to pay for a business.
- **10-year Treasury yield**: Around 4.8% as of late September 2026. When safe government
  bonds pay ~5%, we need stocks to offer a noticeably better expected return to justify the
  extra risk. At today's valuations, most stocks do not clear that bar.
- **CPI (inflation)**: The September 2026 inflation data is released this Wednesday at
  8:30 AM ET. Consensus expects headline inflation around 3.6–3.7% year over year, up from
  3.4% last month. If the number comes in hotter, the market may price in more rate hikes and
  sell off. If it comes in cooler, the market may rally. This is a known risk event two days
  away.
