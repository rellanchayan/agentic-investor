# Market Weather — 2026-08-11

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** A major inflation report drops tomorrow morning, the market is expensive on every historical valuation measure, oil prices are elevated by a geopolitical disruption that adds inflation uncertainty, and none of our three thesis candidates are anywhere near their buy thresholds — so we hold cash and demand a bigger discount before buying anything.

---

## The Big Picture in Plain English

**SPY and the 200-day average.** SPY (the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies and our benchmark) closed at $773.56 yesterday. Its 200-day average — the average price over the last ~200 trading days — is $703.98. Because today's price is roughly 10% above that average, the market is in a general uptrend. That is not itself alarming, but it does mean the market has already run hard and is not giving us a "on-sale" signal.

**Futures this morning.** Before the 9:30 AM open, S&P 500 futures are up about +0.2% and Nasdaq 100 futures are up about +0.4%. Slightly green, but not meaningfully so. A soft July jobs report (payrolls fell by 23,000) is helping by reducing fears that the Fed will raise rates again.

**Valuation — is the market expensive or cheap?** Expensive, by almost every measure:
- The trailing price-to-earnings ratio on the S&P 500 is roughly 26-30x, well above the long-run average of about 19x.
- The Shiller CAPE (a 10-year smoothed earnings measure designed to filter out short-term swings) sits around 39x. Historically, readings above 30x are associated with below-average future 10-year returns.
- The forward P/E (based on expected earnings over the next year) is about 20x, modestly above average.
- In short: the market is not offering a discount. It is priced for a lot of good news already.

**Interest rates and the Fed.** The US Federal Reserve held its short-term rate at 3.50%–3.75% at its most recent meeting on July 28-29, 2026. The next scheduled decision is September 15-16. There is no Fed action this week. Rates are still elevated compared to the near-zero era of 2020-2021, which acts as a modest headwind for high-growth stock prices.

**Inflation (CPI).** The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases its Consumer Price Index report for July 2026 tomorrow, Wednesday August 12, at 8:30 AM ET. This is the biggest scheduled event of the week. Consensus expectation: headline inflation at +3.4% year-over-year (down slightly from June's +3.5%) and core inflation at +2.5% year-over-year (down from +2.6%). A benign print is expected, but it is not guaranteed. If the actual number comes in hotter than expected, markets could sell off sharply; if cooler, markets could rally. This uncertainty makes today a poor time to commit capital.

**Oil and geopolitics.** West Texas Intermediate crude is around $82/barrel and Brent crude around $88/barrel, elevated because the Strait of Hormuz — a narrow waterway through which roughly one-fifth of the world's oil normally travels — has seen dramatically reduced shipping since a US-Iran conflict began in February 2026. A deal to reopen the strait collapsed and negotiations remain stalled. This is relevant to us in two ways: (1) it adds upward pressure to energy prices, which could push tomorrow's CPI print higher than expected; and (2) it is an unresolved geopolitical wildcard that adds a general risk premium to markets.
