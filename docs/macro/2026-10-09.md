## Interest rates and the Fed

- The **10-year US Treasury yield** remains in the range of ~5.27-5.40%, touching its highest
  level since 2002 earlier this week. Even at the lower end of that range, a risk-free government
  bond now earns a competitive return, which reduces the attractiveness of equities priced at
  current levels.
- The **Federal Reserve raised rates 25bp on September 16** to 3.75-4.00%, restarting its
  hiking cycle. Markets price roughly a **25% chance of another hike on October 28** and treat
  a December hike as nearly certain.
- The **ISM Services Prices Paid index hit 74.0** on October 6 — its highest since July 2022.
  A reading above 70 historically signals persistent inflation. The Fed does not yet have the
  all-clear to stop hiking.

In plain terms: when bonds yield 5.3%, every stock we evaluate must clear a higher bar. That
makes us more demanding about entry price, not less.

## Valuation

The Shiller CAPE ratio stands near **41.1x**, against a long-run average of about 17x. This is
the second-highest sustained stretch on record, exceeded only by the brief dot-com peak of 44.2x
in December 1999. The market has been above CAPE 40 continuously since May 2026.

This does not predict an imminent crash. Expensive markets can stay expensive for years. But it
does mean that a buyer today has a thin margin for error, and the right response is patience.
