# Market Weather — Thursday, 2026-09-03

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is in an uptrend but fully priced, inflation is still above the Fed's target, bond yields rose today, and three big scheduled events — payrolls tomorrow, CPI next week, and a Fed rate decision in two weeks — make this a poor moment to accept a small discount.

---

## What the signals say

**SPY and the 200-day average**
SPY (the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies, and our benchmark) closed yesterday at 768.38. Its 200-day average price is 711.58. SPY is about 8% above that average, which means the long-term trend is clearly up — not a time when the whole market is "on sale." A market above its 200-day average tends to stay in its uptrend; there is no broad panic to take advantage of.

**Futures this morning**
S&P 500 futures opened nearly flat (around 7,672, or roughly -0.06%) before recovering to a mild gain of about +0.49% by mid-morning, lifted by a falling US dollar and easing Treasury yields after Fed Governor Waller's comments and economic data. The overall tone is calm but not excited, partly because traders are sitting on their hands ahead of tomorrow's big jobs report and a three-day weekend.

**Interest rates and the Fed**
The Federal Reserve's next meeting is September 15-16. The rate decision comes out on Wednesday, September 16 at 2:00 PM ET. The current federal-funds rate sits at 3.50-3.75%. Wall Street broadly expects the Fed to hold rates steady — no cut, no hike. That consensus can be rattled by a stronger- or weaker-than-expected jobs number tomorrow, which is why caution is warranted right now.

Higher interest rates make future profits worth less today, which puts a ceiling on how much an investor should pay for a stock. As long as rates stay at 3.5-3.75%, prices need to come to us — we do not go to them.

**Inflation**
The most recent official CPI reading (July 2026) showed headline inflation at 3.4% year-over-year, with core inflation (stripping out food and energy) at 2.5%. The Fed's target is 2%. We are not in a crisis, but we are not at the finish line either. Elevated inflation keeps the Fed from cutting rates aggressively, which keeps borrowing costs up across the economy.

**Bond yields**
Pre-market commentary this morning flagged rising Treasury yields as a reason index futures initially slipped. When bond yields rise, existing stocks become relatively less attractive (investors can get a decent return just from bonds), and the present value of future earnings falls. This is a mild headwind today.

**Our thesis candidates**
All three names with active theses are significantly above our fair-value estimates:
- AAPL: trading at $328.52 vs. our base fair value of $195 — roughly 68% above.
- MSFT: trading at $509.32 vs. our base fair value of $378 — roughly 35% above.
- V: trading at $370.09 vs. our base fair value of $297 — roughly 25% above.

None of these clears even a relaxed margin-of-safety hurdle at current prices. The cautious posture, which demands an even larger discount before buying, makes holding cash the only rational choice today.

## What this means for us today

Hold cash. No name is near a fair entry price. A cautious posture means we want a larger-than-normal discount before buying, and right now our candidates do not offer any discount at all — they are priced at premiums. The payrolls report tomorrow could move prices; we watch, we do not chase. If something sells off sharply, we re-evaluate then. Until prices come to us, patience is the correct position.

The cash drag (currently trailing SPY by about 1.47 pp since inception) is a real cost, acknowledged. But buying an overpriced asset to avoid cash drag is a worse outcome than waiting.
