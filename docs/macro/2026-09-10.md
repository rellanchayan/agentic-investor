# Market Weather — Thursday, September 10, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** A CPI report lands tomorrow morning and the Fed makes a rate decision in six days on a "live" meeting where three officials already wanted to hike, while the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 3-year highs and the S&P 500 trades at an elevated P/E above 25 — this is not the week to relax a margin-of-safety requirement.

---

## The market in plain English

**SPY (our benchmark):** SPY closed at $758.65, which is roughly 6.3% above its 200-day average of $713.68. In other words, the broad market is in an uptrend by that measure — prices are generally higher than they were over the past year. That is one positive signal. But a market can be in an uptrend and still be expensive, and right now it is both.

**Futures this morning:** S&P 500 futures are essentially flat (+0.09%, or about 6 points). The Nasdaq is slightly negative (-0.21%). The Dow is mildly positive (+0.24%). This comes after three straight days of market declines — the market is nervous but not panicking. No strong directional signal from the pre-open.

**How expensive is the market?** The S&P 500 trailing P/E ratio is about 25.9, versus a long-run historical median of around 18. The forward P/E (based on expected earnings) is about 19.5 — less alarming but still above average. Put plainly: investors are paying a premium relative to most of history. That means less room for disappointment before prices fall.

**Interest rates:** The Federal Reserve's target rate currently sits at 3.50–3.75%. The 10-year Treasury yield is above 4.85%, which is a 3-year high. High bond yields matter because they compete directly with stocks — if you can earn ~4.85% risk-free in bonds, stocks need to offer a better return to justify their prices. High yields tend to push fair values of stocks down.

**Inflation and oil:** Brent crude oil is trading above $102 per barrel amid U.S.–Iran tensions. More expensive oil feeds into inflation numbers. That matters for tomorrow's CPI report.

**Europe:** The European Central Bank (ECB) is expected to announce a rate hike today. Global central banks tightening simultaneously is a headwind for asset prices generally.

**The FOMC meeting is a live meeting.** That means markets do not consider the outcome predetermined. At the July 2026 meeting, the Fed held steady at 3.50–3.75%, but three voting members dissented and wanted to hike to 3.75–4.00%. If tomorrow's CPI comes in hot, a September hike becomes more likely. Markets do not like uncertainty about rate hikes.

## What this means for us today

We do not buy or sell based on macro weather alone. But the posture shapes how demanding we are about price before acting.

**In cautious mode:** We demand a larger-than-usual margin of safety before any buy. We do not stretch. We stay patient and hold cash rather than reach for a name that is only close to fair value.

Looking at our thesis bench: AAPL trades at $320, against a fair-value base of $195 (roughly 64% overvalued). MSFT trades at $490 against a base of $378 (roughly 30% above fair value). V trades at $357 against a base of $297 (roughly 20% above fair value). All three are well above our entry thresholds regardless of posture. There is nothing to buy today, and there would not be even under a normal posture.

The week ahead is a time to observe, not act. Let the CPI and FOMC data land, and re-evaluate after September 16.
