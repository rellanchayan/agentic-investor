# Market Weather — 2026-08-06

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is expensive by most historical valuation measures, and two of the most market-moving data releases of the month — the July jobs report and July CPI — land within the next six days, making this a week to watch and hold, not a week to buy.

---

## What the numbers say

**SPY vs its 200-day average**

SPY closed yesterday at $770.03. Its 200-day moving average — the average closing price over roughly the last 200 trading days — is $702.44. SPY is about 9.6% above that average. When price is above the 200-day average, the long-term trend is considered an uptrend. That is the good news.

The less good news: being 9.6% above the 200-day average also means the market has already run. It did not become cheap while we held cash. It got more expensive.

**Futures this morning**

S&P 500 futures rose about 0.08% before the open — essentially flat. The Dow was down about 0.26%, near recent record highs. The Nasdaq was up about 0.11% as chip stocks partially reversed early losses. There is no strong directional signal this morning; the market is digesting several things at once.

**Valuation — how expensive is the market right now?**

The S&P 500's trailing price-to-earnings ratio (what investors are paying for each dollar of the last 12 months of earnings) is approximately 26.5x. The long-run historical median is around 18x. The Shiller CAPE — a 10-year inflation-adjusted version of the same ratio — sits at approximately 39.1x, against a historical median of roughly 16x.

In plain terms: by most measures the market is meaningfully more expensive than its historical norm. This does not mean stocks will fall tomorrow. Markets can stay expensive for years. But it does mean a careful investor should demand a bigger discount before buying anything, because the margin for error is thin when prices already reflect a lot of optimism.

**Interest rates and the Fed**

The Federal Reserve's benchmark rate is 3.50%-3.75%, where it has been held steady for two consecutive meetings in 2026. The last meeting was July 29 (less than two weeks ago). The next scheduled decision is September 16, 2026 — about six weeks away. Rates are not an active risk this week; the Fed is in a holding pattern.

The effective rate of 3.63% is still restrictive enough to keep borrowing costs elevated relative to the near-zero era. The Fed is slowly expected to cut once more in 2026, but that is not imminent.

**Inflation**

The last official CPI reading covers June 2026. The next reading — covering July 2026 — comes out Wednesday, August 12 at 8:30am ET, one week from today. Hot inflation data could force the Fed's hand and rattle markets. Cool data would be a mild tailwind. We do not know yet which it will be.

**Earnings season**

Q2 2026 earnings season is in its peak period (roughly July 27 through August 14). As of today, 61% of S&P 500 companies have reported, and 86% have beaten earnings-per-share estimates. Overall S&P 500 earnings growth is running around 24.7% year-over-year — a strong quarter. That is supporting market prices.

However, a few chip names (AMD, SanDisk, Western Digital) disappointed on earnings this week and fell sharply. Nvidia gained after SpaceX announced it will exclusively use Nvidia chips for AI infrastructure. The market is picking winners and losers within tech rather than moving uniformly.

**Geopolitics**

A Strait of Hormuz shipping dispute — which had been a risk since the US-Iran military exchange in mid-July — appears to be de-escalating. Iran and Oman have reached a temporary shipping route agreement. Oil prices stabilized near $80/barrel, down from the spike levels of a few weeks ago. Gold is at multi-week highs reflecting residual uncertainty, but the acute geopolitical risk is lower than it was three weeks ago.

## What this means for us today

Hold cash. Do not buy.

The market's uptrend is intact (SPY well above its 200-day average) and earnings have been strong. But expensive markets and imminent data releases are a combination that calls for patience. The jobs report tomorrow and the CPI print next Wednesday are the two most important scheduled data points of the month. Either one could surprise and move markets meaningfully. There is no urgency to commit capital before knowing those numbers.

Our thesis candidates are all far from their buy thresholds. Even if we were in a "normal" posture today, we would hold cash. The cautious posture simply reinforces what the prices already tell us: wait, watch, and demand a real discount before buying.

The account has been entirely in cash since inception. That has cost us roughly 0.95 percentage points versus SPY. That gap can close when the market pulls back or when a great business becomes available at a fair price. Buying an expensive market to chase performance would be the wrong lesson to draw from a small trailing gap.
