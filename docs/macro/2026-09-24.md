# Market Weather — Wednesday, September 24, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is historically expensive (CAPE near dot-com highs), the
10-year Treasury yield just hit a 17-year high at 5.12%, and the Fed raised rates last week
with more hikes signaled — a combination that demands a larger-than-normal discount before
buying anything.

---

## What the numbers mean

**SPY (our benchmark fund):** 765.07 — this is 6.6% above its 200-day moving average of
717.97. Being above the 200-day average is a healthy sign: the market is in an uptrend.
That said, "in an uptrend" is not the same as "cheap." A stock market can be rising and
expensive at the same time.

**CAPE ratio (roughly: "how expensive are stocks vs. their long-run earnings"):** ~40.6.
The long-run average since 1881 is 17. At 40.6, stocks are 2.4x their historical fair price
on this measure. The only time this reading has been higher was in late 1999, just before the
dot-com crash wiped out 49% of the market. CAPE has been above 40 continuously since May
2026 — a sustained plateau with no modern precedent outside the dot-com era.

**VIX (a measure of fear in the market):** ~15.84. This is moderate — not panicked, not
complacent. There is no sign of extreme fear today.

**Futures this morning:** S&P 500 futures are down about 0.6% and Nasdaq 100 futures down
about 1.1% before the open. A mild negative start, not dramatic.

---

## Interest rates (why they matter for us)

- **The Fed** raised its short-term interest rate by 0.25% on September 16 to a range of
  3.75%–4.00%. This was the first hike in over three years.
- **16 of 18 Fed officials** expect at least one more rate hike before year-end.
- **The 10-year Treasury yield** is now at 5.12% — the highest since 2007. This matters
  because a risk-free bond paying 5.12% competes directly with stocks. When safe bonds pay
  this much, investors demand more from stocks, which tends to push stock prices lower.
- The 10-year was ~4.95% on September 18. It has since risen another 17 basis points — the
  rate environment has worsened since our last full review.

## What this means for us today

We are in **cautious** territory. Stocks are historically expensive (CAPE ~40.6), rates are
at multi-decade highs and still rising, and the Fed is signaling more tightening. SPY is in
an uptrend, but that does not make it cheap.

For the portfolio manager: the minimum margin of safety bar remains at **18%** (the normal
12% bar plus the 6-point cautious bump). All three named thesis candidates (AAPL, MSFT, V)
currently trade 24–44% above their fair-value targets — far beyond any realistic entry under
either posture. The cautious environment confirms: hold cash, do not chase. The primary
obligation today remains expanding the thesis bench to find quality names that are actually
near fair value.
