## What these words mean (plain English)

- **SPY** is the fund we measure ourselves against — it holds the 500 largest US companies. Today it is at **$764.89**.
- **200-day average** is the average price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days, which acts as a long-run trend line. SPY's 200-day average is **$707.51**. SPY is currently **8.1% above** that line, which means the market is in a general uptrend — that is a technically healthy sign.
- **But price and trend are two different things.** A market can be in an uptrend and still be expensive. Right now it is both: trending up, and very pricey.
- **Shiller CAPE** is a valuation measure that smooths earnings over 10 years to cut through short-term noise. A reading of **41.2x** means you are paying $41.20 for each dollar of long-run average earnings. This ranks in the **98.9th percentile** of all months since 1881 — only 18 months in 145 years have been more expensive, all in the 1999-2000 dot-com bubble.
- **Interest rates / the Fed**: the Federal Reserve's short-term rate is **3.50–3.75%** and has been held there for five straight meetings. Three of the voting members wanted to raise it further in July. The higher the rate, the more attractive safe bonds become relative to stocks, and the more expensive it is for companies to borrow.
- **10-year Treasury yield at 4.71%** is the rate the US government pays to borrow for 10 years. At this level, a risk-free 10-year bond pays a meaningful income, which competes with stock returns and pushes fair values for stocks lower.
- **Jackson Hole** is an annual Fed conference in Wyoming. New Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** delivers his first keynote speech on **Friday August 28**. Markets treat this as a major risk event because Fed chairs have used Jackson Hole speeches to announce or signal policy shifts. We do not know yet what Warsh will say.
