## What Is SPY Doing? (The Big Trend)

**SPY** is the fund that holds the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark.

Today SPY stands at **771.78**. Its **200-day average** is **702.93**. SPY is **9.8% above** that average. When SPY is above its 200-day average the market is in an uptrend — which is a good background condition. The uptrend is intact. However, being nearly 10% above the 200-day average means the market has already run meaningfully. It is not "on sale."

S&P 500 futures are pointing **up ~0.5%** this morning (Nasdaq up ~1.0%), driven by a weak July jobs report that raised hopes of future Fed rate cuts.

---

## Today's Big News: July Jobs Report

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the July jobs report this morning at 8:30am ET. The headline:

- **Nonfarm payrolls: -23,000** (the economy shed jobs). Economists expected +83,000. This is a significant miss.
- **Unemployment rate: 4.1%** (down from 4.2%, but driven by people leaving the workforce — participation fell to 61.4%, a five-year low).
- **Wage growth: +3.2% year-over-year** — the slowest since May 2021.

Why are markets rising on bad jobs news? Because weaker employment and slower wages reduce inflation pressure, which makes it more likely the Federal Reserve will cut interest rates in the future. Lower rates are generally good for stock prices.

This data is already reflected in this morning's prices. It is not a surprise for the rest of the day.

## Interest Rates and the Fed

The **Federal Reserve** last met on July 29 and voted **9-3 to hold rates steady** at **3.5% to 3.75%**. Three regional Fed presidents dissented — they wanted to *raise* rates further because inflation has stayed above the Fed's 2% target for years. This is a hawkish Fed, not a friendly one.

The next Fed meeting is **September 15-16**. A Summary of Economic Projections (the "dot plot") will be released then, which will tell us what the Fed expects rates to look like for the rest of 2026. Many economists currently expect a rate hike, not a cut. Higher rates = more expensive borrowing = headwind for stock prices.

## Is the Market Expensive or Cheap?

The S&P 500 is trading at roughly **27x trailing earnings** (some sources put it between 26x and 30x depending on methodology). The historical average going back decades is around **19-20x**. Even the forward P/E (based on next twelve months' earnings estimates) is around 19.5x.

Plain English: the market is priced for a lot of good news to keep coming. When the market is expensive, a careful long-term investor should demand a bigger discount (a larger margin of safety) before buying.

---

## Market Fear Gauge (VIX)

The **VIX** measures how nervous options traders are about the next 30 days. A reading below 20 is generally calm; above 30 is fearful.

Today's VIX: **~15**. That is calm. Over the past month it ranged between 15 and 21, so it spiked to moderate levels recently but has settled. No sign of widespread panic. This is not a fear-driven selloff that would create a buying opportunity.

## Geopolitical Wild Card: US-Iran / Strait of Hormuz

The Trump administration is in indirect nuclear talks with Iran, mediated by Qatar, Pakistan, and Oman. A temporary sanctions waiver was issued through August 21. Oil prices fell 4-6% earlier this week on deal hopes. However, Iran has denied direct negotiations, and prediction markets put the odds of a final deal before January 2027 at only around 30%. If talks collapse and the Strait of Hormuz becomes a flashpoint, oil prices could spike sharply — a negative for the broad economy and markets.

This is a background risk, not a certainty. We note it and monitor.
