# Market Weather — Tuesday, October 6, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The broad market is expensive at roughly 29x earnings (more than double its long-run average) while the Fed is actively raising interest rates — a combination that demands a larger-than-normal discount before buying any name.

---

## What the signals say

### SPY and the 200-day average
SPY closed at **780.10**, sitting **8.1% above** its 200-day moving average of 721.46. Being above the 200-day average means the market is in a broad uptrend — the long-term price direction is still up. That is a positive signal on its own. However, an uptrend does not mean prices are cheap; it only means the trend is intact.

### Is the market expensive or cheap?
Expensive. The S&P 500 forward price-to-earnings (PE) ratio is approximately **28–29x**, against a long-run historical average near 16x. The Shiller CAPE ratio (a smoother 10-year average measure) was roughly **41–42 as of mid-2026**, against a long-run average near 17. By either measure the broad market is trading at roughly 1.8–2.4 times its historical norm. Good businesses exist inside this expensive market, but we should not expect the market to bail us out if we overpay — valuations are a real headwind to future returns.

### Interest rates and the Fed
The Federal Reserve raised its short-term interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to **3.75%–4.00%** on September 16, 2026 — the first rate hike since July 2023. The unanimous 12-0 vote and hawkish language from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh indicate the Fed believes inflation is still too high and the economy can absorb tighter conditions. Futures markets currently price roughly an **87% probability of another 25bp hike at the October 28 meeting**. The 10-year US Treasury yield is near **5.0–5.2%**, a historically elevated level. Rising rates are a headwind for stock prices: they raise the discount rate used to value future earnings, meaning higher rates imply lower fair values for stocks.

### Inflation
The Fed's inflation goal is 2%. Projections for year-end 2026 put inflation near **3.6%**, well above target. Hot inflation is what is driving the rate hikes. The next CPI reading (for September) comes out on **October 14** and will either confirm or challenge that picture.

## What this means for us today

We hold all cash ($1,000,000) and have no positions. The cautious posture means **we demand a larger margin of safety than normal before buying any name.** The market's current expensive valuation combined with rising interest rates is exactly the environment where paying close to fair value is most dangerous — if earnings disappoint or rates rise further, prices can fall sharply. Our watchlist names are already priced well above our fair-value estimates (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL all 30–60% above fair value). MCD is near fair value but still below our conviction threshold.
