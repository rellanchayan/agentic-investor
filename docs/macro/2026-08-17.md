# Market Weather — 2026-08-17

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The market is running about 10% above its long-term trend line, bond yields are near 19-month highs at 4.68%, inflation remains persistently above target, and the Fed's July meeting minutes drop Wednesday with a real chance of a hawkish surprise — all of which argue for demanding a bigger discount before buying anything.

---

## What the market is doing right now

**SPY (our benchmark):** $775.24 — sitting about 9.8% above its 200-day moving average of $705.87. That means the market has been trending upward for an extended stretch. Three straight weeks of gains going into today.

**This morning's futures:** S&P 500 futures crept up about 0.11% before the open, suggesting a broadly flat start. Prediction markets put a 62% chance on a positive open, but the actual open came in slightly negative (-0.2%). The market is "treading water" while investors wait for news this week.

If the minutes read hawkish (meaning, several members think rates need to go higher), bond yields could rise further and stocks could pull back.

## Interest rates and inflation

- **The Fed's rate:** Currently 3.5–3.75%. Held steady in July, but with dissent. Rate futures now price in the federal funds rate reaching 4.0% by year-end — meaning the market thinks another hike is more likely than not.
- **10-year Treasury yield:** 4.68%, near a 19-month high (the 30-year Treasury is at a 19-year high). High bond yields matter for us because they make bonds a more attractive alternative to stocks, and they raise the discount rate we use to calculate what a company's future profits are worth today. When the discount rate rises, fair values fall.
- **Inflation:** Has been above the Fed's 2% target for more than five years. The University of Michigan's survey shows people's one-year-ahead inflation expectations came in above 4% for the fifth month in a row. Until this number falls convincingly, the Fed has a reason to keep rates "higher for longer."
- **Oil:** Brent crude around $88-89 per barrel — elevated, partly from Middle East tensions. High oil prices feed into inflation and consumer costs.

---

## How expensive is the market?

- **Trailing price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio:** Roughly 26-30x earnings depending on the source. The long-run historical median is about 18x. This is elevated.
- **Forward P/E (based on expected future profits):** About 20x — closer to normal, but still above the historical median of ~18-20x.
- **What this means:** The market is not screaming cheap. At these prices, investors are paying a meaningful premium for each dollar of corporate earnings. When rates are also high (see above), that premium is harder to justify. The market needs either earnings to grow fast enough to justify the price, or rates to fall.

## Consumer health check

Retail sales fell 0.6% from June to July, following a weak +0.2% in June. The consumer appears to be pulling back. This week's retail earnings (Walmart, Target, Home Depot) will tell us whether companies see the same thing. A weak consumer outlook could weigh on equities broadly.

---

## What this means for us today

We hold $1,000,000 in cash and zero positions. We are not in danger of losing anything — but we are watching a market that has run hard, is priced above historical norms, faces higher bond yields as a structural headwind, and has a potentially hawkish Fed minutes release midweek.

**The cautious posture does not mean panic.** It means: if a name somehow dropped into our buy range this week, we would demand a wider margin of safety than usual before pulling the trigger — closer to our "low" fair value estimate, not just our "base." Given that all three candidates are 25-56% above even our base fair value, this week is firmly a watch-and-wait period.

Hold cash patiently. Check prices again Tuesday and Wednesday before and after the FOMC minutes. If retail earnings paint a weak consumer picture and yields spike, prices could come in — that would be worth noting.
