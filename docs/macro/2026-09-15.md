## What each term means (for newcomers)

- **SPY** is a fund that holds the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — "the market."
- **200-day average** is the average closing price of SPY over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it means a general uptrend.
- **Fed / interest rates** — the US central bank sets short-term borrowing costs. Higher rates tend to push stock prices down because money costs more and safer assets (bonds) become more attractive.
- **CPI / inflation** — how fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation pressures the Fed to raise rates.
- **Earnings season** — the few weeks each quarter when companies report profits. Not the main story this week.

### The Fed — the biggest event this week
The Federal Reserve's September meeting started **today (Tuesday)** and the interest-rate decision lands **tomorrow, Wednesday September 16, at 2:00 PM Eastern Time**. This is one of four meetings a year that also includes the "dot plot" — a chart of where Fed officials expect rates to go.

The current interest rate is **3.50–3.75%**. Markets are now pricing more than a 50% chance of a **25 basis-point (0.25%) hike** to 3.75–4.00%. The hike scenario gained force after Fed Chair Warsh gave a hawkish speech at Jackson Hole on August 28, and after August inflation came in hotter than expected.

What this means for stocks: if the Fed hikes tomorrow, borrowing costs rise, growth companies get hit harder, and the market may sell off. Even if the Fed holds, the dot plot could signal more hikes ahead — also a negative for equities.

### Inflation (CPI)
The most recent CPI reading (for August 2026, released September 11) showed:
- **Annual inflation: 3.4%** — same as July, still well above the Fed's 2% target.
- **Monthly: +0.4%** — a significant acceleration from July's +0.1%. Gasoline rose 3.9% in August.
- **Core CPI (strips out food and energy): +2.45% annual** — also above target.

Hot CPI reinforces the case for the Fed to hike tomorrow. There is no "all clear" on inflation.

Source: [BLS — Consumer Price Index Summary, Aug 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)

### Oil — a new inflationary shock
Brent crude is trading around **$107.63 per barrel**; WTI (US oil) is around **$103.31**. Oil is up roughly 18% in September alone.

The driver is the ongoing US-Iran military conflict. Tit-for-tat strikes have effectively disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about 20 million barrels of oil per day. The IEA called this the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market. Goldman Sachs warned Brent could rise above $120 in 2027 if disruptions persist.

What this means for us: oil at $107+ is a direct inflationary shock. It makes it harder for the Fed to cut rates (or even pause hiking), it raises costs for most businesses, and it adds uncertainty to the economic outlook.

Sources: [CNBC — US crude tops $100 as market braces for prolonged Iran war](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/iran-us-oil-hormuz-supply-trump-military-brent-wti.html) | [CNBC — Brent tops $100 on US-Iran strikes](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-us-iran-hormuz-attacks.html)

### Market valuation — still expensive
The S&P 500 is trading at roughly **26 times trailing earnings** (some measures show up to 29.6x). The long-run historical average is about **19.7x**. By at least one measure, the market is more than 90% above its modern-era average valuation.

An expensive market does not mean a crash is coming. But it does mean that future returns are likely to be lower than average, and that when bad news hits (like a Fed hike or an oil shock), the market has more room to fall than it would if it were cheap.
