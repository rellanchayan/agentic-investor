# Market Weather — 2026-08-31

**Posture: CAUTIOUS**

Demand a larger margin of safety than usual before buying anything today. The market is expensive, monetary policy is tightening again, a geopolitical shock is live in the energy market, and a week of heavy economic data lies ahead.

---

## What is SPY doing?

SPY closed Friday at **$765.77**. Its 200-day average price is **$710.23**, so SPY is sitting about **7.8% above** that long-run trend line. Being above the 200-day average is generally healthy — the market is in an uptrend, not a downtrend. That is the one green light this morning.

However, SPY futures are pointing **down about 0.27%** before the open. The crowd on prediction markets gives only a 36% chance of a higher open today, leaning bearish.

---

## Is the market expensive or cheap?

The S&P 500 is expensive on a trailing basis:

- **Trailing P/E ratio: ~29.5x** — investors are paying $29.50 for every $1 of current earnings. The long-run average is roughly $19.70. That means the market is priced about **50% above its historical norm** on this measure.
- **Forward P/E ratio: ~20.1x** — using next year's expected earnings the picture looks near "fair value" by recent 10-year standards.

The split verdict (expensive trailing, near-fair forward) reflects investor optimism about future profit growth. When things disappoint — slower growth, higher rates, a shock — expensive trailing multiples compress quickly. The honest read is: the market has priced in a lot of good news and gives little room for error.

---

## Interest rates and the Fed

The Federal Reserve does not meet again until **September 16, 2026**. However, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivered a notably **hawkish speech at the Jackson Hole symposium on Friday, August 28**. He said inflation remains too high (PCE at 3.7%, well above the 2% target), vowed to return it to target, and called interest rates the Fed's "predominant tool." Markets now price a **60% probability of a quarter-point rate hike on September 16**, up from about 56% before the speech.

Higher rates matter for long-term investors because they raise the discount rate used to value businesses — meaning today's earnings are worth less in present-value terms, which pushes fair-value estimates down. If the Fed does hike, all of our fair-value models (which use a cost-of-equity assumption) should be revisited upward.

## The geopolitical wildcard: US-Iran conflict

Over the weekend, US forces struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island near the **Strait of Hormuz** — a chokepoint through which roughly 20% of global oil supply passes. Iran retaliated with missile and drone strikes on US air bases in Jordan. This is an active military conflict that has been running for over six months.

The market impact this morning:

- **Brent crude: above $90/barrel**, up roughly 5% from Friday
- **WTI crude: ~$85.51/barrel**, up about 2.5%
- Average US gasoline prices jumped ~12 cents overnight (GasBuddy), the largest single-day rise since March 2022

Energy costs feeding into inflation at exactly the moment the Fed is already fighting above-target PCE is a compounding risk. Any further escalation near the Strait would be a serious supply shock.
