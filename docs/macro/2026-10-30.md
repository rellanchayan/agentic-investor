## The Federal Reserve

The Fed held its benchmark interest rate at 3.75%–4.00% at its October 27-28 meeting, confirmed October 28, 2026. The vote was 10-2. Two Federal Reserve officials (Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack) dissented, both wanting to raise rates by another 0.25 percentage points immediately.

Why does this matter? Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive for everyone — companies, homeowners, consumers. When rates go up, the future earnings of a business are worth less in today's money, so stock prices tend to fall. The Fed raised rates in September 2026 for the first time since July 2023, breaking a long pause. Now, with two officials pushing for further hikes and markets pricing another increase in December, we are in a rising-rate environment. That historically puts pressure on valuations.

The new Fed Chair is Kevin Warsh, who described this meeting as "a heck of a family fight." That language suggests internal disagreement about the path forward. Uncertainty at the top of the Fed is a reason to be careful.

**Next FOMC meeting: December 8-9, 2026.**

## Inflation and Economic Growth

Headline PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) was running at 3.4% year over year as of August 2026. That is above the Fed's 2% target. As long as inflation stays elevated, the Fed has a reason to keep rates high or raise them further.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis released the Q3 2026 advance GDP estimate and September PCE data yesterday morning (October 29, 8:30 AM ET). Pre-release forecasts expected GDP growth of about +3.6% annualized for Q3 (up from Q1's weak 1.6%), with core PCE expected around +3.1% year over year. I was unable to find confirmed actual results in time for this write-up; the numbers are unknown here. When confirmed, they will matter for whether the December hike gets priced more firmly.
