## The Big Picture

**The market is expensive.**
- Trailing PE ratio: roughly 26x (source: GuruFocus). The long-run historical median is about 18x. That means investors are paying nearly 50% more per dollar of earnings than the historical norm.
- Forward PE: about 20x — closer to the historical median, but this figure assumes strong earnings growth materialize. If growth disappoints, the forward PE looks less reassuring.
- The 10-year Treasury yield is approximately 4.7%. When bonds pay 4.7%, stocks have to compete harder to justify their prices. That squeezes what a fair PE should be.
- PCE inflation (the Fed's preferred measure) came in at 3.7% year-over-year for July. The Fed's target is 2%. We are nearly double that.

**The Fed is the central story today.**
- At the July 28-29 meeting, the Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75% for the fifth straight meeting — but three of the twelve voters wanted to raise rates. The hold was not unanimous.
- Today (Friday, August 28), Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is delivering his keynote at the Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium — his first as Chair. He has already signaled that inflation is "still too high" and that rate hikes may be needed in the coming months. This is the most hawkish signal the Fed has sent in a while.
- The next FOMC meeting is September 15-16. Markets currently price roughly a one-in-three chance of a rate hike there. Warsh's speech today may push that probability up or down.
- **Higher rates are bad for stock valuations.** When the rate investors can earn on safe bonds rises, stocks have to be cheaper (or more profitable) to compete. An expensive market plus rising rate risk is a double headwind.

When the environment is like this, a careful long-term investor waits for a larger discount — not because a crash is certain, but because the margin for error is smaller. We do not chase prices higher; we let prices come to us.

## Glossary (for anyone new)

- **SPY**: an ETF holding the 500 largest US companies; our benchmark.
- **200-day average**: the average SPY price over the last ~200 trading days. Above it = market in uptrend.
- **VIX**: the "fear index." Above 30 = high fear. Below 20 = calm. Currently 15.21 (calm).
- **PE ratio**: price divided by earnings per share. Higher = more expensive.
- **CAPE**: a smoother version of PE using 10 years of inflation-adjusted earnings. Currently elevated (~39x historically).
- **PCE inflation**: the Fed's preferred price gauge. Target is 2%. Currently 3.7%.
- **Jackson Hole**: an annual gathering of central bankers and economists. Fed Chair speeches here often move markets.
- **Margin of safety**: the discount we demand below our estimate of fair value before we will buy.
