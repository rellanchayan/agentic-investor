## What We Found This Morning

**Futures:** US stock index futures are pointing slightly higher on the open (S&P 500 mini contracts up roughly 0.2-0.6%), partly lifted by Micron's strong earnings giving AI-adjacent names a boost. However, the Nasdaq is slipping as rising bond yields weigh on technology stocks. Mixed picture.

**PCE Inflation (released today, 8:30am ET):** The Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge — the Personal Consumption Expenditures index — came in cooler than expected. Core PCE rose 3.0% year-over-year for August, below the 3.3% economists had forecast. Month-over-month core PCE was 0.2%, also below the 0.3% expected. This is mildly good news: inflation appears to be decelerating a little, which may reduce pressure on the Fed to hike at its October 28 meeting. However, 3.0% is still well above the Fed's 2% target.

**Interest rates:** The 10-year US Treasury yield hit approximately 5.33% today — the highest level since April 2002, more than two decades ago. Rising long-term yields are a headwind for stocks because (a) they make future corporate profits worth less in today's dollars, and (b) bonds start to compete more seriously with stocks as an investment. The Fed raised its short-term rate to 3.75-4.0% in September; 16 of 18 Fed officials projected at least one more hike this year.

**Market valuation:** The Shiller CAPE ratio (a measure of how expensive the overall stock market is, using 10 years of smoothed earnings) stands at approximately 40.6x. For reference, the long-run average since 1881 is about 17x. The only time this ratio was higher was during the dot-com bubble peak of 1999-2000, when it reached 44.2x. This is the second-highest reading in 150 years of data. When the CAPE has been above 30 in the past, 10-year forward returns for the market have averaged below 4% per year. The market is not cheap.

**Oil:** Remains elevated above $107/barrel following the US-Iran diplomatic breakdown noted last week. High energy costs add to inflation and squeeze corporate margins.
