# Market Weather — Monday, September 22, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The Fed raised rates just six days ago, signaled more hikes ahead, the 10-year yield is sitting near 5%, and the stock market's CAPE valuation ratio is at 40.6x — the second-highest reading in recorded history — leaving almost no margin for error and demanding a larger discount before we buy anything.

## What the market looks like today

### SPY and the trend

SPY closed Friday at **$773.14**, which is **7.8% above its 200-day moving average of $717.16**. The 200-day average is the simplest long-term trend indicator: when the price is above it, the market is in a general uptrend. We are in an uptrend. This is a positive signal but it is only one signal — and uptrends can persist for a while at very expensive valuations before correcting.

This morning, S&P 500 futures are pointing modestly higher, up roughly **+0.1% to +0.6%** in pre-market. Nasdaq futures are up about **+0.1%**. The tone is broadly steady after Monday (yesterday) saw a recovery. The rally is being led by technology and AI-related names, not the broad market, which is worth noting.

### Interest rates — the Fed just tightened, and is not done

On **September 16 (last Tuesday)**, the Federal Reserve raised the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to **3.75%–4.00%**. This was the first rate hike since 2023. The vote was unanimous. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh described inflation as still too high. Of 18 Fed officials, 16 projected at least one additional hike before year-end.

The **10-year Treasury yield** sits near **4.97%** today, roughly unchanged from Friday. This is the rate that the whole economy borrows against — mortgages, corporate debt, everything. When it is near 5%, bonds compete directly with stocks for investor dollars. A long-term Treasury paying 5% with no default risk is a real alternative to an S&P 500 that offers a 2.5% earnings yield (one divided by a P/E of 40). That math is unfavorable for stocks.

The next FOMC meeting is **October 27–28**. Traders will be listening closely all week to Fed speakers for clues about whether another hike at that meeting is likely.

### Inflation — still above target

The August 2026 CPI report (released September 11) showed:
- Headline: **+3.4% year-over-year**
- Core (ex-food and energy): **+2.4%**

The Fed's target is 2%. We are not there. The **PCE index** — the Fed's preferred inflation measure — is expected to be released this week (Friday or Saturday, September 25-26). If PCE comes in hot, it would raise the chance of another rate hike in October.

### Valuation — the market is historically expensive

The **CAPE ratio** (Shiller P/E) for the S&P 500 stands at **40.6x** in September 2026. To put that in plain terms:

- The long-run historical average is roughly 17x.
- Today's reading is about 2.4 times that average.
- The CAPE has been higher only once in recorded history: the dot-com bubble peak of 1999–2000, when it reached 44.2x.

The CAPE is not a timing tool — an expensive market can stay expensive for years. But it tells you how thin the margin for error is. At 40.6x, the market is pricing in a lot of continued strong earnings growth at a time when borrowing costs are rising and the Fed is actively tightening. That is not a combination that rewards casual buying.

### VIX — calm, not cheap

The CBOE VIX (the "fear gauge") is near **14.87** today, below 15. A VIX this low says options markets do not expect large short-term swings. This is reassuring for people already holding stocks, but a low VIX in an expensive, rising-rate environment reflects confidence, not safety. It does not mean stocks are cheap — it means people are not scared right now.

Neither scenario changes our entry prices; both remind us why a cautious posture is appropriate when conditions are tight.
