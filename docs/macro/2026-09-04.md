---

## What the key terms mean (for reference)

- **SPY**: a fund holding the 500 largest US companies. It is our benchmark — the thing we are trying to beat over years.
- **200-day average**: the average SPY closing price over the last ~200 trading days. SPY above it means the market is in a general uptrend.
- **NFP (nonfarm payrolls)**: the monthly US jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. A strong number means the economy is hiring; a very strong number can also scare markets if it means the Fed will raise rates.
- **Fed / interest rates**: the US central bank sets short-term rates. Higher rates make borrowing costlier and tend to push stock prices down.
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**: how fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation usually means more Fed rate hikes ahead.

## The big events this week and next

### 1. Today — August NFP jobs report (released 8:30am ET, September 4)
The US added **162,000 jobs** in August. Wall Street expected only 53,000. That is a massive beat. Additionally, June and July payrolls were revised up by a combined 55,000.

What this means: a labor market this strong makes it harder for the Fed to justify cutting (or even holding) rates. Futures for the September 16 FOMC meeting now show a higher probability of a 25 basis-point rate hike. Treasury yields rose on the news and early equity futures turned slightly negative.

Source: [BLS / CNBC jobs report coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/04/jobs-report-august-2026.html)

### 2. Friday, September 11 — August CPI inflation report (8:30am ET)
This is the last major inflation read before the Fed meets. If CPI comes in hot (above expectations), the probability of a September rate hike rises further. If it comes in cool, some of today's rate-hike fear will ease. Either way, this will move markets.

### 3. Wednesday, September 16 — FOMC rate decision (2:00pm ET)
The Federal Reserve (chaired by Kevin Warsh) announces whether it will raise, hold, or cut its benchmark rate. The current rate is 3.50–3.75%. The July 29 meeting was a hold (9-3 vote, meaning three dissenting members wanted to hike already). Today's blowout jobs number increases the risk of a hike on September 16. A hike would raise borrowing costs across the economy and typically pushes stock prices lower, especially for companies with high P/E multiples.

Source: [FOMC calendar](https://fedratecalc.com/next-fomc-meeting/)

## Background risk: Iran conflict

A US-Israeli military conflict with Iran is ongoing. The main market risk runs through oil: Iran controls access to the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of global oil flows. With shipping traffic disrupted, Morgan Stanley expects oil to average $80–$90 per barrel in 2026, up from a prior estimate of around $60. Higher oil prices feed through to higher inflation (both CPI and PPI), which complicates the Fed's job. This conflict also adds general geopolitical uncertainty that can cause sudden sharp selloffs on negative headlines.

Sources: [Morgan Stanley Iran oil analysis](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/iran-war-oil-inflation-stock-market-2026), [Columbia Energy Policy](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/us-israeli-attacks-on-iran-and-global-energy-impacts/)
