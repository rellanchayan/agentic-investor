# Market Weather — Monday, October 26, 2026

## Posture: CAUTIOUS

**One-sentence reason:** The Federal Reserve announces its rate decision on Wednesday afternoon, inflation is running hot, and stocks remain historically expensive — this week demands a larger-than-normal discount before buying anything.

---

## Background: what these terms mean

- **SPY** is the fund that tracks the 500 largest US companies. It is the benchmark we measure ourselves against.
- **200-day average** is the average SPY closing price over roughly the last 200 trading days. When SPY is above this line, the market is in a general uptrend.
- **The Fed / interest rates** — the US central bank (Federal Reserve) sets short-term borrowing rates. Higher rates make borrowing costlier, which tends to push stock prices down.
- **CPI / inflation** — how fast consumer prices are rising. Hot inflation can push the Fed to raise rates, which spooks markets.
- **Earnings season** — the stretch each quarter when companies report their profits. Markets can move sharply on surprises.

## Where things stand today

### SPY and the trend
SPY closed at **$765.62**, which is **5.3% above its 200-day average of $727.36**. The market is in an uptrend. This is not a panic environment — but the premium above the average is not extreme either.

### The big event this week: FOMC rate decision
The Federal Reserve's two-day policy meeting begins **tomorrow, Tuesday October 27**, with the interest rate decision announced **Wednesday October 28 at 2:00 PM Eastern Time** (press conference 2:30 PM ET).

Markets are pricing roughly an **87% chance the Fed holds rates steady** at 3.75-4.00% and about a **13-14% chance of a 25-basis-point hike**. Those odds moved down slightly from 16-17% earlier in October, but September's hot CPI print (+3.7% year-over-year, above the ~3.6% consensus) keeps the hike risk alive. A hold is the base case, but a surprise hike — or even a hawkish statement — could move stock prices sharply lower given how high the 10-year Treasury yield already sits (~5.28% as of October 15).

### Valuation: expensive
- **Shiller CAPE (cyclically adjusted P/E):** approximately 41.6 — near all-time record territory, last seen around the dot-com peak in 1999.
- **Trailing P/E:** approximately 28.7x.
- **Forward P/E:** approximately 20x, roughly in line with its five-year average — the least alarming measure, but CAPE and trailing P/E both flash caution.
- At a 10-year Treasury yield of 5.28%, risk-free bonds are yielding more than the earnings yield of the average S&P 500 stock. That is not a normal environment for paying full price for equities.

### Inflation and rates
September CPI came in at **+3.7% year-over-year / +0.6% month-over-month** — above consensus and uncomfortably warm. The next CPI print (covering October) is due **Tuesday November 10** — nothing this week on that front.

### Earnings season
We are in peak Q3 earnings season. Many large-cap companies report results this week. More scheduled news means more potential for sharp moves in either direction.

## What this means for us today

**Hold cash. Do not buy.** A cautious posture means we require a larger-than-usual discount (margin of safety) before entering any position. No candidate is anywhere near that threshold even at normal margins — let alone the wider bar that cautious requires. The FOMC event on Wednesday could reprice the market up or down; paying a 30-50% premium over our fair-value estimates the day before that decision is not disciplined investing.

The one productive action is thesis refreshes: AAPL, MSFT, and V were due for refresh on October 17 and remain stale. Completing those in calm markets — not in reaction to a price move — is the correct next step.
